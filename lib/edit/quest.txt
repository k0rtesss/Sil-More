I:
I:It was Aulë who wrought the deeps of the earth, and the mountains, and the vessels that bear the Sun and Moon; and so great is his love of making that he is kin in spirit to all who shape and build and delve. Yet he holds no work above the worth of the labour that made it. He regarded the wanderer with a deep and knowing gaze, as a master weighs an unfinished thing and sees what it might yet become.
I:
I:"I have seen your works," he said, "the stone you have hewn and the metal you have shaped in your long going-down, and I tell you there is a true spark of the fire of making in you. I do not give my regard lightly, nor my teaching."
I:
I:[newpage]
I:
I:"But a spark is not yet a flame, and a willing hand is not yet a skilled one. Craft must be honed by trial, and the stubborn substance of the earth does not yield up its secrets to any who have not earned them. It must be wooed, and understood, and at the last persuaded, never merely forced."
I:
I:[highlight]
I:He laid his hand upon the forge before them, whose fires burned with the heat of the heart of the world. "Here, then, is my forge, and here is your trial. Take up the hammer, and from the base matter of this place fashion for me a thing of true worth and purpose: not a trinket, but a work in which your craft and your will are made one."
W:The wanderer laid the finished work before the Smith, and Aulë took it up in his great hands; and for a long while he said nothing. He turned it over and over, feeling the weight of it and the balance, hearkening as though to some voice within the metal, and missing no least detail of its making.
W:
W:"It is well-wrought," he said at last, and there was gladness in his voice. "You have not forced the shape upon the substance, but listened to it, and learned its nature, and so persuaded it to your will. That, and not mere cunning of the hands, is the very heart of true craft."
W:
W:"There are many who can make a thing that is fair to look upon, but few indeed who understand what they make. You are of the few, and you are worthy of my blessing."
W:
W:"Go now, and make, and mend," said the Maker. "May your arm never tire at the anvil, and may the hidden flaws of rock and steel reveal themselves freely to your eye, so that nothing false or weak shall ever come from your hand."
