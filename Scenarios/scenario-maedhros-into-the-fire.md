# Into the Fire: Maedhros and the Burning Silmaril

## Scenario Design Document

---

## I. Lore Foundation

### The Last Crime and the Final Choice (Silmarillion, Chapter 24)

After the Great Battle and the overthrow of Morgoth, the Silmarils were recovered from the ruin of Thangorodrim and taken into the keeping of Eönwë, herald of Manwë. The Valar summoned all the Eldar to return to Valinor and receive pardon and judgment. But Maedhros and Maglor, last of the seven sons of Fëanor, could not surrender the Oath their father had laid upon them.

They debated long in secret. Maglor said that if they broke the Oath, they would be destroyed by the Everlasting Darkness they themselves had invoked; but if they kept it through violence and treachery, they would be destroyed also. Better to trust in the mercy of the Valar.

But Maedhros said: If we beg pardon and surrender the Oath, we admit it was false. And if it was false, then all we did — Alqualondë, Doriath, Sirion, the blood of our kin — was not necessity but *choice*. Could you bear that, Maglor? To stand before the Valar and say: "I chose to slay innocents. Not for an oath, but because I wanted to."

In the end, Maedhros prevailed. They crept into Eönwë's camp by night and stole the two remaining Silmarils.

But when Maedhros took the jewel in his hand, it burned him. For the Silmarils were hallowed by Varda herself, and they rejected all who came to them through evil, even if that evil was done in the name of recovering them. The jewel that Fëanor had made, that Maedhros's father had commanded him to reclaim at any cost, that he had murdered and betrayed and suffered for — it would not suffer his touch.

The pain was unbearable. The burning went deeper than flesh. It seared the spirit.

Maedhros could not endure it. In anguish and despair, he cast himself into a gaping chasm of fire, and the Silmaril he bore went with him into the depths of the earth, and was lost forever.

Maglor lived on, and cast his Silmaril into the sea, and wandered the shores singing songs of lament. But Maedhros chose the fire. Because the fire, at least, would end.

### Thematic Analysis

Where the Thangorodrim scenario is about **forgetting in torment** and then **remembering through rescue**, this scenario is about **clarity in despair** — the terrible moment when you understand everything perfectly, and the understanding offers no hope.

Maedhros has fought for centuries. He has endured captivity, maiming, loss of brothers, the failure of the Union, the kinslayings. He has led armies, built alliances, held the line against Morgoth's darkness. And now, in the hour of victory — Morgoth overthrown, the Silmarils recovered — he finds that he has lost.

Not militarily. Not politically. *Spiritually*.

The Oath was supposed to justify everything. If the Oath was binding — if Eru himself held them to it — then the crimes were not theirs. They were slaves to fate, not murderers by choice.

But the Silmaril rejects him. And in that rejection is the answer: the Oath was never absolute. He always had a choice. And he chose wrong. Again and again and again.

This is the unique tragedy of Maedhros among all the doomed of the Noldor. Fëanor died still defiant. Maglor lives on still singing. But Maedhros is the only one who sees the whole arc — from the swearing to the recovery to the rejection — and understands completely that it was all for nothing.

In gameplay terms, this creates an **inverse victory campaign** where success leads to greater despair. The player descends into Angband to find... what? Absolution? Proof? A reason to keep living? But each Silmaril recovered only makes the weight heavier. The heroes are Maedhros's brothers, his victims, his crimes, his regrets — every run is a confrontation with a piece of the past he cannot undo. And at the end, regardless of "victory," there is only the chasm and the fire.

The question is not *whether* Maedhros will throw himself into the fire. Tolkien's text is clear: he does. The question is *how many ghosts he must face before he does*.

## II. Scenario Overview

**Name:** Into the Fire
**Subtitle:** Maedhros and the Burning Silmaril
**Type:** Despair Campaign (Silmaril-gated, fixed ending, inverse victory)
**Runs Required:** 7 (one for each brother of Fëanor's house)
**Victory Condition:** Recover 7 Silmarils, then confront the final truth
**Ending:** Always the same — the chasm and the fire
**Hero Pool:** 7 heroes (the six dead brothers + Maglor who still lives)
**Difficulty:** Extremely high — heroes are strong but curses stack with each Silmaril

### Narrative Frame

The player is Maedhros, standing in the ruins of Angband after the Great Battle. Morgoth is overthrown. The Silmarils are recovered. The war is over.

And Maedhros has never felt more lost.

He and Maglor have debated for days. Maglor begs him to surrender the Oath, to trust in the Valar's mercy. But Maedhros cannot. Because if the Oath was false, then the kinslayings were murder. Simple. Unforgivable. And Maedhros cannot bear to stand before the Valar and admit that.

So they steal the Silmarils. And the jewels burn.

And Maedhros understands: he has lost. Not the war. His soul.

The campaign is a countdown to suicide. Each descent into the metaphorical "Angband" is Maedhros confronting a piece of his past — a brother who died, a victim he slew, a choice he made. Each Silmaril recovered is another weight added to the unbearable burden.

By the seventh Silmaril, the weight is total. And there is only one escape: the fire.

### Progression Structure

The campaign is divided into seven runs, each representing one of the seven sons of Fëanor — Maedhros's brothers, and himself.

| Run | Brother | Fate | Narrative Focus |
|-----|---------|------|-----------------|
| 1 | Fëanor | Burned to ash after Dagor-nuin-Giliath | The father who commanded this doom |
| 2 | Celegorm | Slain at Doriath | The hunter who betrayed Lúthien |
| 3 | Curufin | Slain at Doriath | The cruel one who turned craft to treachery |
| 4 | Caranthir | Slain at Doriath | The wrathful one consumed by his own rage |
| 5 | Amrod & Amras | Slain at Sirion | The twins who followed to the bitter end |
| 6 | Maglor | Lives, but wanders | The singer who will survive and lament |
| 7 | Maedhros | About to die | The eldest who bears all their fates |

### Inverse Victory Mechanic

Unlike other scenarios, **success makes things worse** in this campaign:
- Each Silmaril recovered adds heavy curses to subsequent runs
- Heroes grow weaker, not stronger, as the campaign progresses
- Narrative text becomes darker and more despairing with each run
- The "victory" at the end is the chasm — a release from unbearable burden

This inverts the typical roguelike structure: instead of building toward triumph, you are building toward the moment when you cannot carry on.

### The Burning Hand

A unique mechanic for this scenario: Maedhros holds the Silmaril in his left hand (his right was lost on Thangorodrim). The jewel burns constantly. Each run, the pain increases. Each Silmaril recovered, the burning deepens.

By Run 7, the pain is unbearable. And the chasm is mercy.

## III. Heroes: The Seven Brothers

Each hero represents one of the seven sons of Fëanor, as Maedhros remembers them. These are not the heroes themselves — they are dead (except Maglor). These are Maedhros's memories, guilt-laden and grief-stricken, walking into the dark on his behalf.

*Father, oathbinder, spirit of fire*

```
S:3:1:2:2
C:6:2:6:3:6:5  # Jeweller, Enchantment, Artifice
P:3
D:Greatest of the Noldor and most terrible, whose craft surpassed all the
D:Children of Ilúvatar, whose pride exceeded his craft, whose oath exceeded his
D:pride. He made the Silmarils to capture the light of the Two Trees, and when
D:Morgoth stole them, he swore an oath that bound seven sons to ruin. He died
D:in fire, his body consumed by the strength of his own spirit, and his last
D:words were a command: *Hold the Oath. Avenge me.* You obeyed. You are still
D:obeying. And it has cost you everything. Yet you cannot regret him, because
D:he was your father, and you loved him, even as his ghost drives you into the
D:fire.
```

**Lore notes:** Fëanor died commanding his sons to uphold the Oath. Maedhros obeyed that command for his entire life. This hero is the weight of paternal authority and the impossible burden of living up to a legacy of brilliance and doom.

## IV. Narrative Script

### Opening Scene (Plays Once, at Campaign Start)

> **Title: The Burning Jewel**
>
> The war is over.
>
> Morgoth is cast into the Void. Thangorodrim is broken. Angband is a ruin.
> The host of the Valar stands victorious on the field, and Eönwë, herald of
> Manwë, holds the Silmarils—the three jewels your father made, the three
> lights that cost uncounted lives.
>
> You and Maglor stand apart from the host. You are not invited to the victory
> celebration. You are sons of Fëanor. Kinslayers. Oath-bound.
>
> Eönwë summons you. "The Valar call all the Eldar to return to Valinor and
> receive pardon. Will you come?"
>
> You look at Maglor. He looks at you. Neither of you answers.
>
> Pardon. The word is ash in your mouth.
>
> ---
>
> Later, in the tent, Maglor speaks: "We should surrender the Oath. Beg mercy.
> The Valar are just. They will forgive—"
>
> "Forgive what?" you interrupt. "Alqualondë? Doriath? Sirion? If we say the
> Oath was false, then those were not necessity. They were *choices*. We chose
> to murder our kin. Can you say that, Maglor? Can you stand before Manwë and
> say: 'I killed them because I wanted to, not because I was bound'?"
>
> Maglor's face is stricken. "But if we take the Silmarils—"
>
> "They are *ours*," you say. The words come out harsher than you intend. "By
> right of my father's craft. By the Oath we swore. By every drop of blood we
> shed. They are ours."
>
> "They will burn us," Maglor whispers. "We are no longer worthy."
>
> You look at your left hand. The only hand you have. The hand that will hold
> the jewel.
>
> "Then we burn," you say.
>
> ---
>
> The theft is simple. You and Maglor creep into Eönwë's camp by night. The
> guards are vigilant, but you are desperate, and the Oath drives you forward
> like a wind at your back.
>
> You find the casket. Two Silmarils within. (The third is in the sky—Eärendil's
> star, forever out of reach.)
>
> You take one. Maglor takes the other.
>
> And the moment your fingers close on the jewel, it *burns*.
>
> Not the surface. The *soul*. The fire goes deeper than flesh, deeper than
> bone, into the core of what you are, and it says: *Unworthy. Kinslayer.
> Oath-breaker and oath-keeper both. You are not fit to hold me.*
>
> You scream. Maglor screams. The camp awakens. You flee into the dark,
> clutching your prizes.
>
> The Valar do not pursue. Why would they? You are already damned.
>
> ---
>
> You run until you cannot run. You collapse in the ruins of Angband, the
> Silmaril clenched in your fist, the pain unbearable.
>
> Maglor is beside you, weeping, his own Silmaril burning his hand.
>
> "I cannot bear this," you gasp.
>
> "Neither can I," he sobs.
>
> You look at him. Your last brother. The only one of seven who remains.
>
> "There is a chasm," you say. "To the east. Fire within. If I go into it—"
>
> "No!" Maglor seizes your arm. "No, brother, do not—"
>
> But you see it in his eyes. He understands. The burning will not stop. The
> Oath is broken—the Silmarils are beyond reclaiming by force—but the breaking
> brings no relief. Only proof that you were wrong. About everything.
>
> "I cannot live with this," you say quietly.
>
> Maglor's grip loosens. He knows. He has always known.
>
> "Then I will sing for you," he whispers. "When you are gone. I will sing, and
> the world will remember."
>
> You nod. It is all you can offer. All he can accept.
>
> You rise. The Silmaril burns in your hand. And you walk east, toward the
> chasm, toward the fire.
>
> But first—before the end—you will face them. All of them. Your brothers. Your
> victims. Your choices.
>
> The ghosts await.

> Silmaril that Elwing threw into the sea before you could claim it.

> In the ruins, you found two children. Elrond and Elros. Twins. The same age
> Amrod and Amras had been when the Oath was sworn.
>
> You could have killed them. The Oath might have demanded it. But you could
> not. You looked at their faces and saw your youngest brothers, and the sword
> fell from your hand.
>
> You loved Elrond and Elros. But the love does not erase the blood. It only
> makes the contradiction unbearable.
>
> The ghosts walk into the dark. Two boys. Two brothers. The same.

---

#### Run 6 — Maglor

> **Title: The Brother Who Remains**
>
> Maglor. The singer. Your last companion. Who argued against the theft and
> followed you anyway, because you are his brother and he cannot abandon you.
>
> He holds a Silmaril too. It burns his hand too. But he will not choose the
> fire. He will choose the sea, casting the jewel into the waves and wandering
> the shores forever, singing songs no one hears.
>
> You are leaving him. Alone. The last son of Fëanor, with no brothers, no
> companions, no hope.
>
> The guilt of that is its own fire.
>
> But you cannot stay. The burning is unbearable. And Maglor—Maglor is strong
> enough to endure. You are not.
>
> The ghost of your living brother walks into the dark, and you follow, knowing
> you will not walk beside him much longer.

---

#### Run 7 — Maedhros

> **Title: The Last Son**
>
> You.
>
> Maedhros the Tall. The One-Handed. The High King who ceded the crown. The
> leader of the Union. The survivor of Thangorodrim.
>
> You have fought for five centuries. You have endured captivity, maiming,
> betrayal, the deaths of six brothers, three kinslayings, the failure of every
> hope.
>
> And now you stand in the ruins of victory, holding a Silmaril that rejects
> you, and you understand: it was all for nothing.
>
> The Oath was not absolute. You had choices. And you chose wrong.
>
> Alqualondë: you chose to fight for the ships instead of walking away.
> Doriath: you chose to attack instead of waiting.
> Sirion: you chose the Oath over mercy.
>
> Every time, you told yourself you had no choice. But the Silmaril's burning
> says otherwise.
>
> You are unworthy. Not because the Valar say so. Because you *are*.
>
> And the only escape is fire.
>
> The ghost of yourself walks into the dark. And at the end of the dark is the
> chasm. And you are ready.

---

### Victory Messages (Type 1: Per Run)

**Runs 1-3:**
> The ghost returns, and the weight is heavier. Another face. Another failure.
> Another brick in the wall of guilt that surrounds you. The Silmaril burns
> brighter. The chasm calls louder.

**Runs 4-5:**
> You cannot carry this anymore. The memories are too heavy. The burning is too
> deep. Each ghost you face makes the fire more appealing. At least the fire
> will *end* it.

**Runs 6-7:**
> The last ghosts walk beside you. Maglor, who will wander. Yourself, who will
> fall. The symmetry is complete. The weight is total. The chasm is close now.
> You can smell the smoke. You can hear the roar of flame. And it sounds like
> mercy.

---

### Defeat Messages (Type 1: Per Run)

**Runs 1-3:**
> The ghost scatters. The memory will not stay. But it does not matter. The
> weight remains. The burning remains. The chasm remains. You will face them
> all eventually. There is no escape but forward.

**Runs 4-5:**
> You fall, and the darkness takes you, and when you rise again you are still
> holding the Silmaril, and it is still burning, and the ghosts are still
> waiting. There is no release in failure. Only delay.

**Runs 6-7:**
> Even in defeat, the truth remains: you are going into the fire. The only
> question is how many ghosts you see first. And the answer, you realize, does
> not matter. The ending is fixed. The chasm waits.

## V. Final Scene — The Chasm

**This scene ALWAYS plays after Run 7, regardless of victory or defeat in individual runs. The ending is inevitable.**

### The Edge

> You stand at the edge of the chasm.
>
> It is vast. A wound in the earth, carved by the breaking of Thangorodrim in
> the Great Battle. Fire churns in its depths—not dragon-fire, not forge-fire,
> but the deep fire of the earth's heart, the molten stone that flows beneath
> the crust of Arda.
>
> The Silmaril is in your left hand. The hand Fingon saved when he cut off your
> right. The hand that has held swords, written orders, clasped Maglor's in
> brotherhood.
>
> It is burning. The jewel is burning you. The pain goes deeper than flesh. It
> is not heat—it is *rejection*. The Silmaril, hallowed by Varda, blessed by
> the Queen of Stars, is saying: *You are not worthy. You are stained. You
> cannot hold me.*
>
> And you cannot argue. Because it is right.
>
> ---
>
> Behind you, Maglor stands. He has followed you here. He knows what you intend.
>
> "Brother," he says. His voice is breaking. "Please. Do not do this."
>
> You turn. His face is ravaged by tears, by grief, by the same burning you
> feel. He holds his own Silmaril, and it torments him as yours torments you.
>
> "I cannot bear it," you say simply.
>
> "Neither can I," he says. "But I will. I will endure. And so can you."
>
> You shake your head. "No, Maglor. You are stronger than I am. You always were.
> You will cast your jewel into the sea and wander the shores, and the song
> will carry you. But I—I have nothing left."
>
> "You have me," he says desperately. "I am here. I will not leave you."
>
> "I am leaving you," you say quietly. "I am sorry."
>
> His face crumples. He knows. He has always known.
>
> "Then let me sing for you," he whispers. "One last time. Before you go."
>
> You nod.
>
> ---
>
> Maglor's voice rises, and the song is the most beautiful thing you have ever
> heard. It is the song of the Two Trees in Valinor, of the light before the
> Darkening. It is the song of seven brothers standing in a line, swearing an
> oath that would doom them all. It is the song of the Gap you held for four
> hundred years, of the Union you built and saw shatter, of Fingon's rescue
> and the hand you lost and the crown you surrendered.
>
> It is the song of love and loss, of choices made and roads not taken, of the
> weight of the Oath and the burning of the Silmaril.
>
> And through it all, the refrain: *You tried. You failed. But you tried.*
>
> The song ends. Maglor's voice breaks on the final note.
>
> "Goodbye, little brother," you say.
>
> "Goodbye, Russandol," he whispers. The old name. The name from before the
> Oath. Copper-top. The boy you were in Valinor, before the darkness.
>
> You smile. It is the first smile in decades.
>
> And you step forward.
>
> ---
>
> The fall is longer than you expected. The chasm is deep. The fire rushes up
> to meet you, and the heat is immense, but the pain in your hand is already so
> great that the fire is almost a relief.
>
> In your mind, you see them: Fëanor burning to ash. Celegorm's golden hair
> matted with blood. Curufin's sneer. Caranthir's rage. Amrod and Amras, the
> youngest, following to the end. Maglor, singing on the shore above.
>
> And yourself. Maedhros. The eldest. The leader. The one who bore the weight
> of all their fates.
>
> The fire takes you. Your body burns. The Silmaril in your hand sinks with you
> into the molten stone, into the heart of the earth, lost forever.
>
> One Silmaril in the earth. One in the sea. One in the sky.
>
> The three jewels Fëanor made are sundered across all the circles of the
> world. And you, his eldest son, are ash.
>
> ---
>
> Above, Maglor watches the fire close over you. He does not scream. He has no
> voice left for screaming.
>
> He turns. He walks west, toward the sea. The Silmaril burns in his hand.
>
> And he begins to sing.

---

## VI. Epilogue — Maglor's Lament

**This plays after the final scene, a brief coda.**

> The waves wash up upon the shore. The waves retreat. And Maglor stands in the
> surf, the Silmaril burning in his hand, and he sings.
>
> He sings of seven brothers who swore an oath that doomed them all.
>
> He sings of Maedhros, the eldest, the tall, the one-handed, who hung on
> Thangorodrim and was rescued by grace, who led the Union and saw it fail, who
> stole the Silmaril and could not bear its burning, who chose the fire because
> the fire, at least, would end.
>
> He sings of the chasm. The fall. The ash.
>
> And then he raises his hand—the hand that holds the Silmaril—and he casts it
> into the sea.
>
> The jewel sinks. The light goes down, down, down into the deeps. And the
> burning stops.
>
> But the pain does not.
>
> Maglor sinks to his knees in the surf. He is alone. The last son of Fëanor.
> The only one who remains.
>
> And he begins to sing again. A new song. A song of lament that will never
> end.
>
> The sea listens. It does not forgive. But it listens.
>
> And that, perhaps, is all that remains.

## VII. Implementation Notes (For Future Coding)

### Campaign Structure
- **Run count:** 7 (one for each son of Fëanor)
- **Silmaril requirement:** 7 (one per brother)
- **End trigger:** After Run 7, always fire Final Scene (the chasm)
- **Ending:** Fixed — Maedhros always dies. No "true victory" alternate.

### Inverse Victory Mechanic
- Each Silmaril recovered **adds curses** to subsequent runs (not blessings)
- Heroes do not grow stronger — they grow weaker, representing despair
- Late campaign runs are harder than early ones (inverted difficulty)
- The "reward" for success is reaching the ending faster

### Unique Mechanics
- **BURNING_HAND unique:** Constant damage over time that increases each run
- **KINSLAYER flag:** All heroes carry this (represents Oath-guilt)
- **MAIMED_HAND flag:** Maedhros cannot use shields/two-handed weapons
- **Despair meter:** Visual indicator of how close to the chasm (run count)

### Hero Pool
- 7 heroes, one per brother of Fëanor's line
- All are "memories" — not the actual people, but Maedhros's guilt-constructs
- High base stats but heavy curse penalties
- No hero growth between runs (memories are static)

### Narrative System Integration
- Memory Chapters (Type 0): 7 chapters, one per run
- Victory/Defeat messages (Type 1): Same messages regardless (doom is inevitable)
- Final Scene: Always plays after Run 7
- Epilogue: Maglor's Lament, brief coda
- Opening Scene: The theft and the burning
