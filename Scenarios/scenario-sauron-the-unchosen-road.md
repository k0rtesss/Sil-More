# The Unchosen Road

## Scenario Design Document

---

## I. Lore Foundation

### Sauron's Moment of Choice (Silmarillion, "Of the Rings of Power")

When Thangorodrim was broken and Morgoth overthrown, Sauron put on his fair
hue and did obeisance to Eonwe the herald of Manwe, and abjured all his evil
deeds. And some hold that this was not at first falsely done, but that Sauron
in truth repented, if only out of fear, being dismayed by the fall of Morgoth
and the great wrath of the Lords of the West. But it was not within the power
of Eonwe to pardon those of his own order, and he commanded Sauron to return
to Aman and there receive the judgement of Manwe. Then Sauron was ashamed, and
he was unwilling to return in humiliation and to receive from the Valar a
sentence, it might be, of long servitude in proof of his good faith; for under
Morgoth his power had been great. Therefore when Eonwe departed he hid himself
in Middle-earth; and he fell back into evil, for the bonds that Morgoth had
laid upon him were very strong.

### The Spirit Before the Fall (HoME X, Morgoth's Ring)

Sauron was originally Mairon, a Maia of Aule. He was the greatest of Aule's
servants, a craftsman of surpassing skill who loved order, precision, and the
satisfaction of things well-made. He was corrupted by Morgoth (then Melkor)
before the making of Arda was complete — drawn not by cruelty but by Melkor's
promise of a world *ordered* to perfection, where nothing was wasted, nothing
random, nothing left to chance. The corruption was gradual: the love of order
became the love of control; the desire to shape became the desire to dominate;
the craftsman became the tyrant.

Tolkien notes that Sauron "was not indeed wholly evil, not unless all 'reform'
is vain." His repentance before Eonwe was genuine, if imperfect — he was
honestly moved by the fall of his master and the display of the Valar's power.
But his pride was too great to accept humiliation, and his fear of judgment too
deep to trust in mercy. And so the greatest pivot point in the history of
Middle-earth turned on a single spirit's inability to kneel.

### Thematic Analysis

Where the Glorfindel scenario tells a story of *remembering forward* — a hero
recovering lost identity — and the Feanor scenario tells a story of
*remembering backward* — a dying spirit reliving its trajectory — and the
Fingolfin scenario tells a story of *remembering in combat* — and the Maglor
scenario tells a story of *remembering through confusion* — the Sauron
scenario tells a story of **choosing in the present**.

There is no past to relive. No identity to recover. No duel to fight. There is
only a spirit standing at the edge of two roads, arguing with itself, while the
tide turns and the ships wait.

The player does not know they are Sauron. The narrative refers only to "you" —
a spirit who served the Enemy, who was once a craftsman of the Blessed Realm,
who has been offered pardon and cannot decide whether to accept. Clues
accumulate across seven runs — references to shapeshifting, to wolves, to a
fortress on an island, to a duel of songs, to a maiden who broke the
protagonist's power. Attentive players who know their Tolkien will piece it
together. The final scene confirms the identity and delivers the ending.

The tragedy — and the genius of Tolkien's conception — is that this single
choice reshapes all of history. If Sauron had returned to Valinor, the Rings
of Power would never have been forged. The One Ring would never have existed.
Numenor would not have fallen. The long wars of the Second and Third Ages, the
suffering of millions, the corruption of nine kings of Men — none of it would
have happened. The entire Lord of the Rings is the consequence of one spirit's
pride at this exact moment.

In gameplay terms, this creates a **medium campaign** (7 runs) where the
player's cumulative score determines the ending. High score (many Silmarils
recovered) produces the good ending: the spirit chooses Valinor, and history
is rewritten. Low score produces the bad ending: the spirit flees into
Middle-earth, and history unfolds as Tolkien wrote it. The bad ending is
*canonical*. The good ending is the road not taken.

### Key Lore Touchstones Referenced

- **Mairon, Maia of Aule** — Sauron's original name and nature. Greatest of
  Aule's servants, a craftsman who loved order. (HoME X, Morgoth's Ring)
- **The corruption by Morgoth** — Melkor seduced Mairon with visions of a
  perfectly ordered world. The love of order became the love of domination.
  (HoME X)
- **Tol-in-Gaurhoth** — The Isle of Werewolves. Originally Minas Tirith of
  Finrod, captured by Sauron, filled with wolves and horror. His seat of
  power in Beleriand. (Silmarillion, "Of Beren and Luthien")
- **The duel of songs with Finrod** — Sauron and Finrod dueled in songs of
  power. Sauron prevailed, stripping the company's disguise. Finrod was
  imprisoned and later died fighting a werewolf. (Silmarillion, "Of Beren
  and Luthien"; Lay of Leithian)
- **The defeat by Luthien and Huan** — Luthien, half-Maia daughter of Melian,
  came with Huan the great hound. Sauron sent wolves, then came forth in
  wolf-form himself. Huan held him. Luthien commanded him to yield. He
  shifted through many shapes but could not escape. He surrendered the tower
  and fled as a vampire. (Silmarillion, "Of Beren and Luthien")
- **The War of Wrath** — The final battle of the First Age. The Host of the
  Valar, led by Eonwe, broke Angband, captured Morgoth, and cast him through
  the Door of Night into the Void. (Silmarillion, "Of the Voyage of Earendil")
- **Eonwe's command** — The herald of Manwe summoned all of Morgoth's servants
  to submit. Sauron came and abjured his deeds. Eonwe commanded him to
  return to Aman for judgment. Sauron refused. (Silmarillion, "Of the Rings
  of Power")
- **Osse's return from corruption** — The Maia of Ulmo was briefly seduced by
  Morgoth but returned to serve Ulmo. He was pardoned. The direct precedent
  for Sauron's offered redemption. (Silmarillion, "Of the Valar")
- **Aule and the Dwarves** — Aule himself, Sauron's former master, created the
  Dwarves in impatience, outside Iluvatar's design. When confronted, he wept
  and submitted. He was forgiven. The parallel is exact: a maker who
  overstepped, who humbled himself, who was pardoned. (Silmarillion, "Of
  Aule and Yavanna")
- **Finarfin's turning back** — At Araman, hearing the Doom of Mandos,
  Finarfin turned back and begged the Valar's pardon. He was forgiven and
  rules in Tirion. The Elven parallel to the choice offered to Sauron.
  (Silmarillion, "Of the Flight of the Noldor")

## II. Scenario Overview

**Name:** The Unchosen Road
**Type:** Medium Campaign (run-count-based, not silmaril-gated)
**Runs:** 7
**End Condition:** After 7 runs, the campaign concludes regardless of outcome
**Scoring:** Total score (or Silmarils recovered) across all 7 runs determines
the ending
**Hero Pool:** 10 heroes (3 new Maiar + 7 existing characters)

### Narrative Frame

The player is a spirit — unnamed, unidentified — standing in the ruins of
Angband after the War of Wrath. The war is over. Your master has been cast
into the Void. The herald of the Valar has offered you pardon: return to
Valinor, submit to judgment, begin the long road of penance.

You have not decided.

Each run into the ruins of Angband is a descent into the wreckage of your
past — confronting what you were, what you became, and what you might yet be.
The heroes you play are voices in the argument: faces from your past and
present, each embodying a facet of the choice. Silmarils recovered are
fragments of light reclaimed from the shadow, pulling you toward redemption.
Deaths in the dark are the shadow pulling you back.

After seven descents, the choice is made. The cumulative weight of light
versus shadow tips the balance. The ships sail. And you either board them or
watch them go.

### Progression Structure

| Run | Theme | Internal Voice |
|-----|-------|---------------|
| 1 | Ruin | "It is over. Everything I served is broken." |
| 2 | Memory | "I was not always this. I was a craftsman once." |
| 3 | The Offer | "The herald says I may return. The door is open." |
| 4 | The Corruption | "How did I fall? The bonds are still so strong." |
| 5 | The Deeds | "What I did. The fortress. The wolves. The singer." |
| 6 | The Temptation | "I could order this world. Not as he did. Better." |
| 7 | The Choice | "The ships wait. The dark waits. I must choose." |

### The Meter

Each run's outcome shifts an internal "meter" between Light (Valinor) and
Shadow (Middle-earth):

- **Silmaril recovered:** Strong push toward Light
- **Survived without Silmaril:** Slight push toward Shadow
- **Died in the ruins:** Strong push toward Shadow

After each run, a short "Inner Voice" passage plays reflecting the current
state of the argument. At the end of Run 7, the meter determines the ending:
- **High score:** Good ending (boards the ship, returns to Valinor)
- **Low score:** Bad ending (flees into Middle-earth — canonical)

## III. New Heroes

Three new Maiar heroes, representing the spiritual voices in the debate.

### Hero 1: Eonwe

*Herald of Manwe, Mightiest of the Maiar in Arms*

```
# Herald of Manwe, led the Host of the West in the War of Wrath.
# Mightiest in arms of all the Maiar. Warrior-commander.
# Stats +9, Power 3

N:XX:Eonwe
Q:[auto-generated]
A:, Herald of Manwe
B:You bear the word of the Elder King-mightiest herald, whose mercy waits with the patience of the West.
F:MEL_AFFINITY | EVN_AFFINITY | WIL_AFFINITY | SMT_PENALTY
S:3:2:2:2
C:5:9:0:10  # Majesty, Zone of Control
P:3
D:Herald of Manwe and banner-bearer of the Valar, mightiest in arms of all
D:the Maiar, who led the Host of the West across the sea to break the gates
D:of Angband and overthrow the Dark Lord Morgoth at the ending of the Elder
D:Days. His voice carries the authority of the Elder King, and his command
D:is gentle and absolute: he does not compel, but neither does he relent.
D:When the War was won he summoned all of Morgoth's servants to submit and
D:be judged, offering pardon to those who would return to Aman in repentance;
D:it was not within his power to forgive, but within his charge to offer
D:the road home to any spirit willing to walk it.
```

**Lore notes:** Eonwe is described in the Silmarillion as "the greatest of all
the Maiar in arms" and is Manwe's herald. He led the Host of the Valar in the
War of Wrath. Critically, Tolkien specifies that Eonwe *could not* pardon
Sauron himself — only Manwe had that authority over fellow Maiar. Eonwe could
only command Sauron to return. This is important: the mercy is real but
requires the supplicant to *go to the source*. Eonwe represents the voice of
legitimate authority — not harsh, not lenient, simply clear.

### Hero 2: Osse

*Maia of Ulmo, Who Returned from Corruption*

```
# Maia of the coastal seas. Was briefly corrupted by Morgoth, returned.
# Tempestuous, fierce, unpredictable. The precedent of redemption.
# Stats +6, Power 2

N:XX:Osse
Q:[auto-generated]
A:, Vassal of Ulmo
B:You rise from dark water-wave-lord whose fury was tamed, whose return proved the road exists.
F:EVN_AFFINITY | WIL_AFFINITY | MEL_AFFINITY | SNG_PENALTY | SMT_PENALTY
S:1:2:1:2
C:2:1:5:2  # Blocking, Strength in Adversity
P:2
D:Maia of Ulmo, lord of the coastal seas, whose delight is in storms and
D:the violence of wave upon shore. In the youth of the world Morgoth drew
D:him into shadow with promises of power over all the waters, and for a
D:time he served the Enemy; but Uinen his spouse restrained him and brought
D:him to repentance, and he returned to Ulmo and was pardoned. He remains
D:tempestuous and fierce, for his nature was not changed by the pardon,
D:only redirected; he is proof that a spirit may serve the shadow and yet
D:return to the light, not unchanged but unbroken, bearing the scars of
D:corruption as a wave bears the memory of the storm that shaped it.
```

**Lore notes:** Osse is the single most important lore precedent for Sauron's
offered redemption. He is explicitly described in the Silmarillion as having
been seduced by Morgoth and then returning to serve Ulmo. He was pardoned. This
proves that Maiar *can* come back from corruption. The Valar have done it
before. The road exists. The key difference: Osse's corruption was brief and
shallow. Sauron served Morgoth for the entire history of Arda. The question is
whether the depth of the fall makes return impossible — or just harder.

### Hero 3: Arien

*Maia of the Sun, Spirit of Laurelin's Fire*

```
# Maia who guides the vessel of the Sun. Spirit of holy fire.
# Devoted to the light without wavering. Even Morgoth feared her.
# Stats +5, Power 2

N:XX:Arien
Q:[auto-generated]
A:, the Sun-bearer
B:You burn without consuming-flame that serves the world, whose brightness even the Enemy feared.
F:WIL_AFFINITY | SNG_AFFINITY | PER_AFFINITY | MEL_PENALTY | EVN_PENALTY
S:0:1:0:4
C:7:7:7:16  # Song of the Trees, Song of Mastery
P:2
D:Maia of the Sun, who in the years of the Trees tended the golden flowers
D:in the gardens of Vana; when the Trees were slain and the last fruit of
D:Laurelin was fashioned into the vessel of the Sun, she was chosen to
D:guide it across the sky, for she alone of the Maiar could endure its
D:sacred fire. Leaving Valinor she forsook the form she had worn and became
D:as a naked flame, terrible in splendour, too bright for Elven eyes to
D:endure; and Morgoth feared her and dared not assail the Sun while she
D:bore it. She is proof that service to the light need not diminish the
D:servant but may perfect her, that faithfulness is not submission but
D:fulfillment, that fire can burn holy as well as cruel.
```

**Lore notes:** Arien is described in the Silmarillion's "Of the Sun and Moon."
She originally served Vana (Yavanna's sister) and tended the golden flowers
that Laurelin's light nourished. When chosen to bear the Sun, she "forsook the
form and raiment which she had worn" and became pure fire. Tolkien explicitly
states that Morgoth feared her. She represents the polar opposite of Sauron's
path: a Maia who devoted herself wholly to service, who *became more* through
faithfulness rather than less. Her fire is holy. Sauron's fire became corrupt.
Both are Maiar. Both are spirits of craft and flame. The difference is the
choice.

### Design Rationale

Every hero serves a dual purpose: a voice in the internal debate, and a clue
to the protagonist's hidden identity.

**Voices of Mercy (arguing for Valinor):**

- **Eonwe** — The direct offer. "Come home." His patience is the patience of
  Manwe. He cannot compel. He can only wait.
- **Osse** — The proof. "I returned. It can be done." His corruption was
  shallower, but the road is the same road.
- **Arien** — The mirror. "This is what faithful service looks like." Her fire
  is holy. The protagonist's fire became corrupt. Both are Maiar. Both chose.
- **Finarfin** — The parallel. An Elven prince who heard the Doom of Mandos,
  turned back, knelt before the Valar, and was pardoned. The exact analogy.
- **Melian** — The alternative. A Maia who went to Middle-earth for love, who
  served through protection, who used power to shelter rather than dominate.

**Voices of Conscience (whose fates illuminate the choice):**

- **Finrod** — Died in the protagonist's dungeon. The noblest of the Noldor,
  who kept a promise to a mortal man. His death is on the protagonist's
  hands. *Clue: the duel of songs.*
- **Luthien** — Broke the protagonist's fortress with a song of love. The
  ultimate defeat. *Clue: the maiden and the hound.*
- **Beren** — Survived the protagonist's dungeon through courage alone. A
  mortal who was stronger than a Maia. *Clue: the fortress of wolves.*
- **Galadriel** — Sees through all disguise. The protagonist's fair form is
  transparent to her. She represents the terror of being *truly known*.
- **Celebrimbor** — The greatest smith after Feanor. His craft mirrors the
  protagonist's love of making. His future vulnerability to the protagonist's
  deception foreshadows the Second Age. *Clue: the craft connection.*

### Clue Progression

The heroes are ordered so that early choices provide subtle clues and later
choices provide obvious ones:

| Hero | Clue Level | What It Reveals |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Eonwe | Subtle | Protagonist is a Maia being offered pardon |
| Osse | Subtle | Protagonist was corrupted like Osse was |
| Arien | Moderate | Protagonist is a fire-spirit who went wrong |
| Melian | Moderate | Protagonist is a Maia who could have served through love |
| Finarfin | Moderate | The humiliation of kneeling is the core obstacle |
| Celebrimbor | Moderate | Protagonist is a craftsman, connected to Aule |
| Galadriel | Strong | Protagonist is a shapeshifter/deceiver |
| Beren | Strong | Protagonist held a dungeon with wolves |
| Finrod | Very Strong | Protagonist won a duel of songs against a prince |
| Luthien | Very Strong | Protagonist was overthrown by a maiden and a hound |

## V. Narrative Script

### Opening Scene (Plays Once, at Campaign Start)

> **Title: The Silence After Thunder**
>
> The mountains are broken.
>
> Where Thangorodrim stood — three peaks of iron and malice, capped with
> smoke and dark fire — there is now a wound in the earth, a chasm where
> the sea rushes in, salt water filling the caverns where once your master
> sat enthroned. The Valar came in wrath. The Host of the West shattered
> the gates, broke the pillars, tore the mountains down to their roots.
> The sky is clear, blue, pitiless. The sun burns where shadow ruled for
> ages uncounted.
>
> You stand in the wreckage. You wear a shape that is fair — or was fair
> once, before you learned to use beauty as a weapon. Two hands, two eyes,
> a face that might inspire trust in those who do not know what lies
> behind it. The shape feels almost right. Almost honest. As though some
> part of you remembers being the person this form once was.
>
> Around you, the Host of the West makes camp. Maiar in shining raiment.
> Vanyar warriors golden-haired. The banners of Valinor ripple in a wind
> that smells of the sea. And among them, taller than any, Eonwe the
> Herald, whose voice carries the authority of Manwe himself.
>
> He spoke to you. You remember what he said.
>
> *"Return to Aman. Submit to the judgment of Manwe. The door to pardon
> is open."*
>
> He said it simply, as one who expects obedience. And you knelt. You
> spoke words of repentance. You were not wholly lying.
>
> That is the worst part. You were not wholly lying.
>
> The ships wait in the harbor. The west calls. Behind you, the ruins of
> everything you served for ages of the world. Before you, the road to
> Valinor — to judgment, to penance, to the humiliation of standing before
> those you betrayed and saying: *I was wrong.*
>
> You have not boarded the ships. You stand at the edge of the ruin, and
> you do not move.
>
> Something in the wreckage calls to you. Not your master — he is gone
> beyond the Walls of Night, thrust into the Void, his power broken
> forever. But something else. Something older. The memory of what you
> were, and the question of what you might yet become.
>
> You descend into the ruins. Not because you must. Because the answer
> to your choice lies somewhere in the dark.
>
> The ships wait.
>
> The dark waits too.

### Memory Chapters (Run Progression — Type 0)

These play at the START of each run.

---

#### Run 1 — The Ruins

> **Title: What Remains**
>
> The tunnels of Angband gape like the throat of a dead beast. The forges
> are cold. The smithies where you once shaped weapons and instruments of
> torment stand empty, their anvils cracked, their fires drowned. The iron
> that lined these walls is twisted and rent by powers beyond any craft of
> yours.
>
> You walk through halls you helped to build — not always with your hands,
> but with your *will*, your vision of order imposed on the world's raw
> matter. Every corridor, every chamber, every precisely angled passage
> was part of a design. Your master provided the power. You provided the
> *architecture*.
>
> And it is all ruins now.
>
> In the silence, a thought: *Was any of it worth building?*
>
> The forges were instruments of cruelty. The tunnels housed slaves. The
> order you imposed was the order of the whip and the chain. You knew this
> then. You know it now. But the *making* itself — the act of shaping, of
> imposing form on chaos — that, at least, was real. That was yours before
> it was his.
>
> You remember hands that shaped without malice, in a forge lit by holy
> fire, under the eye of one who made for the joy of making.
>
> The memory hurts more than the ruins.

---

#### Run 2 — The Forge

> **Title: The Apprentice**
>
> A memory, bright and sharp, cutting through the ash:
>
> A forge in Valinor, lit by the radiance of the Two Trees. The ring of
> hammer on mithril. A presence beside you — vast, patient, warm — one
> of the great Powers, who made for the love of making, whose joy was in
> the thing created, not in the power it granted.
>
> You were his pupil. His finest. He said so once, and the pride you felt
> was clean then — the honest pride of a craftsman who has mastered his
> art, not the corroded pride that later drove you to dominion.
>
> You remember the day he showed you the secret of binding spirit to
> matter — the craft that is the foundation of all enchantment. "The
> key," he said, "is not command. It is *understanding*. You must know
> what the material wants to become, and help it." You listened. You
> understood. And later, ages later, you twisted that teaching into
> chains and instruments of binding, and the understanding became
> compulsion, and the help became slavery.
>
> But in the forge, in the light, with the sound of the hammer true and
> the metal singing under your hands — you were good. You were admirable.
> And the name you bore was not a lie.
>
> Where did that craftsman go?
>
> You are walking through the ruins, and the question follows you like
> a shadow.

---

#### Run 3 — The Summons

> **Title: The Herald's Words**
>
> Eonwe's voice echoes in your memory, each word precise as a sword-stroke:
>
> *"The Lords of the West do not seek your destruction. They seek your
> return. You were not always what you became, and the works of your
> first days were not all evil. The door stands open. Walk through it."*
>
> He spoke without cruelty. That was the hardest part. If he had raged —
> if he had condemned you, threatened you, demanded your groveling — you
> could have *resisted*. Resistance is what you know. But mercy is a
> weapon you have no defense against.
>
> You remember kneeling before him and speaking words that were — what?
> True? *Partly* true. You said: "I abjure my evil deeds." And in that
> moment, with the ruin of your master's fortress still smoking behind
> you and the clear light of the Blessed Realm shining in Eonwe's eyes,
> you *meant* it. The repentance was real.
>
> But it was not complete.
>
> Because even as you knelt, a voice within you whispered: *If you return,
> they will judge you. They will humble you. You, who commanded armies,
> who shaped the world to your design — you will be made to serve. To
> obey. To wait upon the pleasure of those who never understood what you
> could build.*
>
> The door is open. The mercy is real. And you cannot walk through.
>
> Not yet. Not yet.

---

#### Run 4 — The Corruption

> **Title: How the Dark Found You**
>
> You did not fall all at once.
>
> He came to you in the guise of a fellow seeker — one who understood the
> yearning for order that burned in you like a forge-fire. "The Valar are
> wasteful," he said. "They create and then leave their creations to
> chance. The world could be *shaped*. Perfected. Made to run without
> flaw, every part in its place, every purpose fulfilled."
>
> And you listened. Because it *was* what you desired. Not cruelty. Not
> destruction. *Order*. The tidiness of a forge where every tool hangs
> in its place. The beauty of a mechanism that functions without waste.
>
> He never told you the cost. Or perhaps he did, and you chose not to
> hear. The order he promised required obedience. Obedience required
> force. Force required fear. And fear required everything you became.
>
> You remember the first time you broke another's will. A lesser spirit,
> confused and wandering. You *showed* it its place in the design. You
> *helped* it understand. The compulsion was gentle at first. Almost kind.
>
> After that, it was easier. After that, you had a new name. After that,
> the forge-light dimmed, and the only light was the fire of Angband.
>
> In the ruins, in the dark, you hear his voice still. He is gone beyond
> the Walls of Night, but the *bonds* he laid upon you are very strong,
> and his whisper coils through your thoughts like smoke:
>
> *"You are mine. You were always mine. Return to Valinor if you wish.
> They will see what I made of you, and they will not forgive."*

---

#### Run 5 — The Deeds

> **Title: The Isle of Wolves**
>
> You cannot descend into these ruins without remembering what was done
> in them. Not only by your master. By *you*.
>
> There was an island once. A fair tower built by an Elven king, set
> upon the river to guard against the shadow. You took it. You filled
> it with darkness. The wolves were your servants, bred from shadow and
> malice, and their lord was a beast of your making, and the tower that
> was once a beacon of light became a dungeon where captives were
> devoured one by one.
>
> A company came to that isle. Disguised. Brave. Foolish. An Elven prince
> led them — a singer, noble beyond measure — and with him a mortal man,
> doomed and terribly brave. You challenged the singer to a duel of
> power, wrought in song. His voice was fair. Yours was stronger. You
> stripped their disguise. You cast them into your dungeons. You sent
> the wolves.
>
> The singer died there. He broke his bonds at the end and wrestled the
> wolf with bare hands, and killed it, and died. For a mortal. For a
> promise.
>
> And then *she* came. A maiden with the power of the Ainur in her blood,
> and a hound of Valinor beside her, and a courage born of love. She
> overthrew you. She broke your tower. She freed the captives. You fled
> in a shape of darkness, shrieking, and the taste of that defeat has
> never left your mouth.
>
> You remember the singer's face. Noble. Unbroken. Even in chains, he
> did not beg.
>
> You remember the maiden's voice. Clear as starlight. She commanded you
> to yield, and you could not refuse, because her power was not built on
> compulsion but on something you had forgotten — something you abandoned
> when you first chose the order of the chain over the order of the heart.
>
> These are your deeds. This is what you became.
>
> Can the Valar forgive this? Can *you*?

---

#### Run 6 — The Temptation

> **Title: What I Could Build**
>
> In the deepest ruin, where the forges once burned hottest, you find
> something unexpected: not a memory but a *vision*.
>
> Middle-earth, reshaped. Not by your master's iron hand — his method was
> crude, born of spite and the desire to mar Iluvatar's creation. But you
> are not your master. You never were. You are a *craftsman*. And the
> world is raw material.
>
> You could bring order. Not through conquest — through *wisdom*. Go
> among the Elves who love making, among the Dwarves who love craft,
> among the Men who love progress, and *teach* them. Share the secrets
> you still carry. Help them build. Guide them — gently, patiently —
> toward the ordered world you have always envisioned.
>
> No chains. No wolves. No dungeons. Just guidance. Just the steady hand
> of one who sees the design when others see only chaos. Through
> instruments of craft that bind not with force but with shared purpose.
> Through *rings*, perhaps. Tools of making, not of mastery.
>
> The vision is beautiful. The vision is a lie.
>
> You know it is a lie because you have heard this argument before — in
> your master's voice, in Valinor, ages ago: *"The world could be shaped.
> Perfected."* And you believed it then, and it led to chains and wolves
> and a maiden singing your fortress to ruin.
>
> But perhaps this time it would be different. Perhaps without him.
> Perhaps the order could be achieved through willing cooperation.
>
> The thought shines in the dark like a forge-fire newly lit. And you
> cannot tell whether it is redemption or the same old corruption wearing
> a fairer mask.
>
> The ships wait. The vision burns.
>
> The choice is near.

#### Run 7 — The Choice

> **Title: The Western Shore**
>
> You emerge from the ruins for the last time.
>
> The Host of the West has made preparations to depart. The ships in the
> harbor are laden with the spoils of a war that lasted since the world
> was young. The Maiar stand in shining companies. The Vanyar sing songs
> of victory. Eonwe's banner catches the western wind.
>
> The sea stretches before you, grey and gold in the light of the setting
> sun. Beyond the horizon, beyond the Shadowy Seas, beyond the Enchanted
> Isles — Valinor. The Blessed Realm. Home.
>
> You have not stood on those shores in ages beyond reckoning. You have
> not heard the Music since you turned away from it. You have not felt
> the light of the Trees — now gone, but remembered in every stone and
> leaf and breath of Aman — since you chose the shadow.
>
> Eonwe approaches. His eyes hold neither anger nor pity — only patience.
>
> *"The ships depart with the evening tide,"* he says. *"There is a
> place for you. There has always been a place for you."*
>
> You look at the sea. You look at the ruins behind you. You look at
> your own hands — fair hands, craftsman's hands, that once shaped beauty
> under holy light and later shaped horrors in the dark.
>
> The tide is turning. The wind is from the west.
>
> This is the last moment. After this, the ships sail, and the door
> closes, and whatever you choose becomes what you are forever.
>
> You draw a breath. The air smells of salt and endings.

These play when a run ends in defeat. They represent the shadow pulling the

#### Run 1 Defeat — The Familiar Dark

> was.

#### Run 5 Defeat — The Weight of Blood

#### Run 6 Defeat — The Vision Sharpens

## VI. Hero Victory and Defeat Messages

Each message is written from the protagonist's perspective. Victory messages
(with Silmaril) lean toward the light and argue for Valinor. Defeat messages
lean toward shadow and argue for remaining.

The protagonist is never named. Clues to their identity are embedded in each
message.

---

### 1. Eonwe

**Victory:**

> *Herald.* Your face blazes with the authority of Manwe, and in this
> light — the Silmaril burning like a star in the dark — you see what
> Eonwe sees: not the monster, but the servant who went astray. He does
> not despise you. He does not fear you. He simply *waits*, with a
> patience that is not weakness but the supreme confidence that good
> outlasts evil if given enough time. "There is a place for you," he
> said. And the Silmaril's light says: *He meant it.* The mercy is real.
> The door is open. You could walk through it. You could go home.

**Defeat:**

> *Herald.* Eonwe turns away, and in his turning you see the limits of
> mercy: he cannot *force* you to accept pardon. He could drag you to
> Valinor in chains — he is the mightiest of the Maiar in arms, and you
> are diminished — but he will not. He can only offer. And you cannot
> accept. Because accepting means admitting that everything you built was
> worthless, and the pride will not bend, not even before the gentlest
> authority in Arda.

---

### 2. Osse

**Victory:**

> *Wave-lord.* You see Osse as he was when he returned from the
> shadow — tempestuous, wild, but *free*. He was corrupted, as you were.
> He served the Enemy, as you did. And he came back. Uinen restrained
> him, and Ulmo received him, and the Valar pardoned him. He is still
> fierce, still himself, but no longer in chains. The Silmaril's light
> shows you what you most fear to believe: *It can be done. The road
> back exists. Others have walked it.* If Osse — proud, untameable
> Osse — could return, then perhaps...

**Defeat:**

> *Wave-lord.* Osse's corruption was brief. A season's folly, in the
> youth of the world. But you served the Enemy from before the shaping
> of Arda, through all the ages of the Trees and the Sun. The Valar
> pardoned a Maia who strayed for a season. Will they pardon one who
> was the shadow's chief architect? The wave breaks. The analogy breaks
> with it.

---

### 3. Arien

**Victory:**

> *Sun-bearer.* She guides the vessel of the Sun, and even your master
> feared her fire. You remember her — a spirit of golden Laurelin,
> radiant, unbowed, devoted wholly to the light. She never wavered.
> She never wondered, as you did, whether order could be achieved
> through domination. In the Silmaril's light, you see her as she is:
> proof that a Maia can serve faithfully and find joy in service, that
> fire can burn holy as well as cruel. You were like her, once. Before
> the forge-fire turned to forge-chain. You could be like her again.

**Defeat:**

> *Sun-bearer.* Arien's fire burns too bright to face, and you turn
> away — not because you are evil (you tell yourself) but because you
> are *different*. She chose the light without hesitation. You
> hesitated, and in that hesitation your master found you, and the
> dark swallowed you. Arien's purity is a mirror you cannot face.
> How can you return to a realm where spirits like her shine undimmed,
> and your own light is ashes?

---

### 4. Melian

**Victory:**

> *Queen of twilight.* She went to Middle-earth not in conquest but in
> love — a Maia who heard the nightingales of Nan Elmoth and chose to
> dwell among the trees, who married an Elven king and bore a daughter
> fairer than the stars. She served Middle-earth by *being* in it, by
> loving it, by wrapping a kingdom in enchantment not to dominate but
> to protect. The Silmaril's light shows you the path she walked: a
> Maia in the mortal lands, using power for shelter, not for chains.
> You could have been this. The choice is still before you. Not her
> path exactly — but a path that leads away from the shadow.

**Defeat:**

> *Queen of twilight.* Melian wove her Girdle around Doriath, and for
> an age the kingdom endured. Then her husband was slain, the Girdle
> broke, and Doriath fell, and she departed in grief. Even *her* work
> could not last. Even a Maia's love could not hold back the shadow.
> What chance, then, for one who *is* the shadow? Melian's gentleness
> is beautiful and futile. The world breaks what love builds. Only
> power endures.

---

### 5. Luthien

**Victory:**

> *Nightingale.* She stands in your memory like a wound that will not
> close — the maiden who broke your fortress, freed your prisoners,
> stripped your power with a song that commanded not through force but
> through love. You, who had mastered songs of binding and unmaking,
> were overthrown by a song of *release*. And in the Silmaril's light
> you see the truth you could not face in that moment: she was stronger
> not because her power was greater but because it came from a source
> you had abandoned. Love that gives freely, that costs everything,
> that asks nothing in return. You were overthrown by love. Perhaps you
> could be saved by it.

**Defeat:**

> *Nightingale.* She broke you. She came to your fortress with a hound
> and a song and she *broke* you, and you fled in a shape of darkness,
> shrieking, diminished, and the humiliation has never healed. She is
> dead now — she chose mortality, for love, always for love — but her
> victory endures. Returning to Valinor means standing before the Valar
> who *know* you were broken by a maiden with a song. The shame is
> unbearable.

---

### 6. Finrod

**Victory:**

> *Singer.* You remember the duel — songs of power hurled against songs
> of power in the dark. His voice was fair, full of the light of
> Valinor, singing of beauty and creation and the love of Iluvatar for
> his Children. Your voice was stronger, wrought of sorcery and the
> long practice of domination. You prevailed. You stripped his disguise.
> You cast him into chains. And he died there — consumed by the wolf
> you sent. A prince who walked into your darkness for a promise made to
> a mortal he barely knew. The Silmaril's light illuminates his face,
> and it is noble, and it shames you. He lost the duel. He won the
> argument. His death proved that oaths kept in love are worth more
> than power won through fear.

**Defeat:**

> *Singer.* You defeated him. In the duel of songs you overcame his
> voice with your darker power, and he died in chains, and you felt
> nothing. That is the horror. Not that you killed a noble spirit. That
> you felt *nothing*. The capacity for empathy was already dead in you
> by then. And if you can feel nothing for the death of one so noble,
> what does that say about the repentance you offered Eonwe? Was it
> real? Or was it just another shape you wore — fair seeming, empty
> within?

---

### 7. Beren

**Victory:**

> *Mortal.* A Man. A brief candle of courage, who walked into your
> fortress disguised as an orc, sustained by nothing but love for a
> maiden and a promise to a king. You captured him. You threw him in
> your dungeon. You sent the wolves. And still he endured, because the
> fire in him was not the fire of power but the fire of *purpose* —
> small, mortal, utterly beyond your comprehension. The Silmaril's
> light returns his face: stubborn, scared, unbowed. He had no sorcery,
> no songs of power. Only his word and his heart. And in the end his
> heart won the Silmaril from Morgoth's own crown, and your fortress
> was broken. A mortal man. If one who has so little can do so much,
> perhaps one who has fallen so far can climb again.

**Defeat:**

> *Mortal.* He was nothing. A brief life, a handful of decades. You
> held him in your dungeon and he was helpless — no power, no craft, no
> strength against your will. And yet he did not break. And the maiden
> came for him, and your fortress fell. A mortal shattered what you
> built. The humiliation is acid on your pride. Better to rebuild.
> Better to ensure that no such power — the power of the small, the
> loving, the faithful — ever threatens you again.

---

### 8. Galadriel

**Victory:**

> *Lady of Light.* She looks at you, and you feel *seen* — not the fair
> form, not the careful mask of repentance, but what lies beneath. She
> has Melian's wisdom and Feanor's pride and the clear sight of ages,
> and nothing deceives her. In the Silmaril's light, her eyes are
> mirrors, and what you see reflected is yourself — not the monster, not
> the craftsman, but both. And her gaze does not flinch. She knows what
> you are. Perhaps being *truly seen* — with all the darkness and all
> the buried light — is not damnation but the beginning of healing.
> Perhaps the worst thing is not being seen but being *hidden*, forever,
> behind a fair form that lies.

**Defeat:**

> *Lady of Light.* She sees through you. She always will, and no shape
> you wear, no guise you assume will ever deceive her. That knowledge is
> a blade. If you return to Valinor, there will be thousands like
> her — spirits of clarity who will look at you and see the wolf
> beneath the fair form. You will be *known*. Every foul deed, every
> broken trust — they will see it all. Better the darkness, where no
> one sees. Better the mask, where no one looks too closely.

---

### 9. Finarfin

**Victory:**

> *Golden prince.* He heard the Doom of Mandos and turned back. At
> Araman, where the darkness gathered and the Noldor marched into exile,
> Finarfin — gentlest of Finwe's sons — wept and led a remnant home to
> beg the Valar's pardon. He was forgiven. He rules now in Tirion, in
> the light of the Blessed Realm, his grief assuaged by ages of peace.
> The Silmaril's light shows you his face: simply *humble*. He bent
> his knee. He admitted his error. He asked for mercy. And mercy was
> given. The parallel burns: *He turned back. He was pardoned. The
> road exists.* If a prince — bound by the Doom, stained by
> Alqualonde's blood — could return and be forgiven, then perhaps
> even you.

**Defeat:**

> *Golden prince.* He turned back because his pride was small. You are
> not Finarfin. Your pride is the deepest thing in you — deeper than
> the corruption, deeper than the repentance. Finarfin could kneel
> because kneeling cost him nothing but pride, and his pride was gentle.
> Your pride is *you*. To kneel before the Valar is to unmake yourself.
> And what rises afterward — the penitent, the forgiven, the
> *obedient* — would not be you. It would be a stranger wearing your
> face. Better the monster you know than the ghost of a servant you can
> no longer be.

---

### 10. Celebrimbor

**Victory:**

> *Smith.* You see him at his forge, and the sight is a blade in your
> chest — not because he is your enemy but because he is what you might
> have been. A craftsman. A maker. One who shapes with purpose and finds
> joy in creation. He turned from his father's evil. He remained in
> Middle-earth for *craft* — for the love of things well-made, for the
> satisfaction of a gem perfectly cut. The Silmaril's light gleams on
> his work, and it is beautiful, and it is *good*, and you
> remember — with an ache that threatens to split you open — what it
> felt like to make something good. Before the chains. Before the
> wolves. If you returned to Valinor, if you submitted to penance,
> perhaps at the end of long ages you might stand at a forge again and
> make something that is simply, purely, *good*.

**Defeat:**

> *Smith.* He is brilliant. He is ambitious. He is *vulnerable* —
> seeking always to surpass, to prove that the craft of his line is
> not spent. You see in him a hunger you recognize. For perfection. For
> the thing well-made, taken to its uttermost limit. And the thought
> comes, unbidden, that this hunger could be *guided*. Not by force. By
> sharing the secrets you carry. You could teach him. You could help him
> create works of staggering beauty. And through the works you create
> together, you could shape Middle-earth to the order you envision.
> Gently. Willingly. Without chains. The thought is beautiful. The
> thought is poison. And you cannot tell the difference.

## VII. Final Scene — The Reveal and the Choice

**This scene ALWAYS plays after the 7th run, regardless of outcomes.**

It consists of three parts: the Reveal, the Choice, and the Aftermath.

---

### Part 1: The Reveal (Always Plays)

> The ruins of Angband are behind you. The sea is before you. The ships
> wait. The tide turns.
>
> You stand at the western shore, and the fair form you wear — the
> handsome face, the steady hands, the bright eyes that seem so
> trustworthy — is neither lie nor truth. It is a memory. A hope.
> A mask that was once a face.
>
> Your name was Mairon once.
>
> The Admirable. The Craftsman. Greatest of Aule's servants, who shaped
> with purpose and loved the order of things well-made. That name was
> true.
>
> They call you Sauron now. Gorthaur. The Abhorred. The Cruel. The Lord
> of Werewolves. The Necromancer. The Lieutenant of the Dark Throne. You
> are the mightiest of all the spirits of evil that remain in Arda.
>
> But you were not always this.
>
> Both truths exist in you. Both burn.
>
> Eonwe waits. The ships wait. Valinor waits.
>
> What have you chosen?

---

### Part 2: The Choice

*The following text depends on the campaign's total score.*

---

#### If total score is HIGH (Good Ending — The Road Taken):

> You board the ship.
>
> It is the hardest thing you have ever done — harder than the ages of
> service under Morgoth, harder than the duel of songs, harder than the
> day the maiden broke your tower. Because those things were done in
> darkness, where you were strong. This is done in light, where you
> are naked.
>
> The ship carries you westward across the Sundering Sea, and with every
> league the weight of your deeds grows heavier — not lighter, as you
> had hoped, but heavier, because the light of Valinor illuminates what
> the shadow hid. You see yourself clearly for the first time in ages
> beyond counting, and what you see is terrible.
>
> But you do not leap into the sea. You do not shift into a winged
> shape and flee. You stand upon the deck and bear the weight, because
> you have chosen to bear it.
>
> In Valinor, you are brought before Manwe. His eyes are not cruel.
> They are not kind. They are *clear*. He sees everything — every
> forge-chained slave, every wolf-torn prisoner, every shape you wore,
> every will you broke. He sees it all.
>
> And he says: *"You are pardoned. Not forgiven — that is earned, over
> ages. But pardoned. The door to return is open, and you have walked
> through it, and that was the hardest step."*
>
> The penance is long. Longer than the ages of your service to Morgoth.
> You serve in Aule's forges again, but not as a master — as the least
> of apprentices, learning again what it means to make without
> compulsion, to shape without domination, to create for the joy of
> creation alone.
>
> The shame is constant. The other Maiar see Gorthaur when they look at
> you. The Elves who returned from Middle-earth flinch at your approach.
> The memory of what you were follows you like a shadow that Valinor's
> light cannot quite banish.
>
> But the forge-fire is warm. And the hammer falls true. And sometimes,
> in the long evenings, when the light of the Trees is remembered in
> Aman's twilight, you hear a song — not a spell, just a song — and it
> is beautiful, and you did not make it, and it does not bind anything,
> and you weep, because beauty without possession is a thing you had
> forgotten, and it is the most precious thing in Arda.
>
> *In this telling, the Rings of Power were never forged. The One Ring
> never came to be. Numenor endured. The Dark Tower was never raised.
> The long wars of the Second and Third Ages, the suffering of
> millions, the corruption of nine kings of Men, the siege of the
> White City, the shadow over the Shire — none of it happened.*
>
> *Because one spirit, at the ruin of his master's fortress, chose
> humility over pride.*
>
> *This is not what happened.*
>
> *But it is what might have been, had the road been chosen.*
>
> *The unchosen road.*

---

#### If total score is LOW (Bad Ending — What Actually Happened):

> You do not board the ship.
>
> You watch the sails catch the western wind, white and gold in the
> fading light, and something in you reaches toward them — a hand, a
> thought, a whisper of the craftsman you were — but it is not enough.
> The pride is stronger. The fear is deeper. The bonds that Morgoth laid
> upon you are very, very strong.
>
> When Eonwe departs, you flee into the east. You hide yourself in the
> wastelands of Middle-earth, in shapes that are not fair, in places
> that are not found. The repentance that was real — partly real —
> *almost* real — dissolves like morning mist in the shadow of the
> mountains.
>
> And in the hiding, the old thoughts return. *Order. Purpose. The
> world shaped to my design.* Not Morgoth's design. Yours.
>
> In time you put on a fair form again — the fairest you have ever worn,
> beautiful and wise and generous. You call yourself Annatar, the Lord
> of Gifts. You go among the Elven-smiths, and you find a craftsman —
> brilliant, ambitious, hungry for perfection — and you teach him. You
> share the old secrets. You help him forge rings of power.
>
> And through the rings, the old order reasserts itself. Compulsion
> wearing the mask of cooperation. Domination wearing the mask of
> guidance. The craft that was your truest gift, twisted again into
> instruments of control.
>
> You forge a Ring in the fires of a mountain. Into it you pour your
> will, your power, your desire for mastery.
>
> *One Ring to rule them all.*
> *One Ring to find them.*
> *One Ring to bring them all,*
> *and in the darkness bind them.*
>
> The words are not a curse. They are a design specification. The
> forge-master's final work.
>
> And it will take three thousand years, and a war that spans the world,

>
> But that is another age's story.
>
> This age's story ends here: on the western shore, with the ships
> sailing into the sunset, and a fair-seeming spirit turning away into

>
> *This is what happened.*
>
> *The road to Valinor was offered. The pardon was real. The door was
> open.*
>
> *And through pride, and through fear, and through the bonds that
> Morgoth had laid upon him, the one who was Mairon chose the shadow,
> and became Sauron, and the darkness that followed endured for three
> ages of the world.*
>
> *The unchosen road.*

- **Score tracking:** Sum of all individual run scores OR count of Silmarils
  recovered across all runs
- **Meter threshold:** Suggested: 3+ Silmarils (or equivalent score) for Good
  Ending; below threshold for Bad Ending. Tune based on playtesting.
- **Hero pool:** Restricted to 10 specific characters (3 new + 7 existing)
- **Hero reuse:** Allow same hero in multiple runs

### Story System Integration
- Memory Chapters (Type 0) trigger at run start, indexed by run number (1-7)
- Defeat Chapters (Type 1) trigger on hero death, indexed by run number
- Hero Victory/Defeat messages trigger on run completion, indexed by hero ID
- Inner Voice messages (Light/Shadow) trigger after each run based on
  cumulative score
- Final Scene triggers after Run 7 completion, with score-based text selection
- Opening Scene triggers once at campaign creation

### Identity Concealment
- The scenario name ("The Unchosen Road") does not reveal the identity
- No text in run-start messages, hero messages, or defeat chapters uses the
  names Sauron, Mairon, Gorthaur, or Annatar
- The protagonist is always referred to as "you"
- Clues are progressive (see Clue Progression table in Section IV)
- The identity is revealed ONLY in the Final Scene (Part 1: The Reveal)
- The Good Ending names Sauron implicitly through consequences
- The Bad Ending names him explicitly through the canonical passage

## IX. Lore Consistency Notes

### Strict Adherences
- Sauron's original nature as Mairon, Maia of Aule, is per HoME X
- The corruption by Morgoth through the promise of order is per HoME X
- Eonwe's offer and Sauron's refusal are per Silmarillion ("Of the Rings of
  Power")
- The exact detail that Eonwe *could not pardon* Maiar himself is per
  Silmarillion
- Sauron's repentance being "not at first falsely done" is per Silmarillion
- His shame and unwillingness to accept humiliation are per Silmarillion
- The "bonds that Morgoth had laid upon him" being the final barrier is per
  Silmarillion
- Osse's corruption and return to Ulmo is per Silmarillion ("Of the Valar")
- Arien's nature as spirit of Laurelin, and Morgoth's fear of her, is per
  Silmarillion ("Of the Sun and Moon")
- Aule's creation of the Dwarves and subsequent forgiveness by Iluvatar is
  per Silmarillion
- Tol-in-Gaurhoth and the duel of songs with Finrod is per Silmarillion
  ("Of Beren and Luthien")
- Luthien and Huan's defeat of Sauron is per Silmarillion
- Sauron's shapeshifting (wolf-form, vampire-form) is per Silmarillion
- Finarfin's turning back and pardon is per Silmarillion
- The War of Wrath and destruction of Angband/Thangorodrim is per Silmarillion
- Morgoth's fate (cast through the Door of Night) is per Silmarillion
- The subsequent creation of the Rings of Power and the One Ring is per
  Silmarillion ("Of the Rings of Power") and Lord of the Rings
- Sauron taking the name Annatar and deceiving the Elven-smiths is per
  Silmarillion and Unfinished Tales

### Creative Liberties (Tolkien-Compatible)
- The "descent into Angband's ruins" as metaphorical structure for the
  internal debate is original, but compatible with the scenario framework
- The specific texture of Sauron's internal monologue is invented, but
  grounded in Tolkien's analysis of Sauron's psychology in HoME X
- The vision of "rings of craft" in Run 6 is an original connection between
  Sauron's pre-Ring psychology and his later actions, consistent with
  Tolkien's conception of Sauron as wanting "order" rather than mere
  destruction
- The Good Ending (Sauron returns to Valinor) is explicitly non-canonical
  but represents Tolkien's own speculation that Sauron's repentance was
  genuine enough that the road *could* have been taken
- The detail of Sauron serving in Aule's forge as penance is original but
  thematically consistent with Tolkien's emphasis on humility and service
  as the path of redemption for fallen spirits
- The "design specification" characterization of the Ring inscription is
  original analysis, consistent with Sauron's nature as a craftsman
- The reference to "the smallest and most unlikely of Iluvatar's children"
  (hobbits) in the Bad Ending is a deliberate forward echo to The Lord of
  the Rings
- The internal debate structure (7 runs representing 7 stages of the
  argument) is original but mirrors the 7-run structure of the Feanor
  scenario

### Tolkien's Key Insight

The scenario rests on Tolkien's most devastating observation about evil: that
it does not come from monsters but from *choices*. Sauron was not born evil.
He was not irrevocably corrupted. He was offered mercy and refused it — not
because mercy was impossible, but because his pride would not let him kneel.

The entire Lord of the Rings — every battle, every death, every ring, every
age of shadow — is the consequence of this one spirit's inability to say:
*I was wrong.*
