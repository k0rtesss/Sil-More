# Scenario Intro Poems

These are the poetic intro texts shown to the player via `print_story_intro()` at the start of each scenario campaign. Each poem is written in Tolkien's elevated prose-verse style, grounded in First Age lore, and subtly conveys the scenario's unique mechanics to the player.

---

## 1. Glorfindel — Hero Reborn (Default Scenario)

*Open campaign. 51 heroes. Silmaril-gated victory (15 recovered). 15 deaths = defeat. Curse/blessing economy. Valar chapters restore memory.*

```
You awaken in darkness.
No name. No memory.
Only a quiet ache of courage deep inside you,
like embers buried beneath ash.

Far below, Morgoth waits upon his throne-
iron-dark and crowned in flame.
Upon his brow shine three Silmarils, stolen stars.
He senses your stirring. He knows you will come.

Far above, beyond the shadows of Angband,
the Valar watch silently.
They offer no guidance, yet their presence
fills you with strength—and dread.

You will return many times, each death and rebirth
etched into the endless stone halls of Mandos.
Each fall will draw your spirit deeper into shadow,
closer to a doom from which you cannot escape.

Yet each victory—each Silmaril wrested from Morgoth's crown—
will brighten the Valar's hope,
even as your soul grows thinner,
your strength fading with every triumph.

You envy the Edain, whose Gift from Iluvatar
frees them from the bonds of Mandos and the world.
Yet you do not know if such release can ever be yours.
You do not know who-or even what-you truly are.

For each time you awaken,
you will carry the names of heroes beloved and feared-
bright spirits, fiery hearts, proud kings and exiles,
wanderers beneath sun and stars,
whose courage you borrow, but whose fates are not your own.

This is the trial set by the Valar:
to reclaim your forgotten name,
to balance shadow and light,
and to find within the borrowed glory of others
your true self.

Now the path before you opens,
and your trial begins.
```

## 2. Feanor — Spirit of Fire

*Fast campaign. 7 fixed runs. Score-based. Each run is a memory of the dying Feanor. Restricted hero pool (those Feanor knew in Valinor). The Oath endures.*

```
You are dying.
The Balrog-fire has left you broken
upon the ash-fields of the North,
and your sons bear you backward
from the ruin you made of hope.

Through smoke you see them still-
the peaks of Thangorodrim, black against the sky,
unconquerable, unchanged,
mocking the oath you swore
in the light of the dying Trees.

You know now, with the terrible clarity of death,
that no power of the Noldor
shall ever overthrow those towers.
You knew it then. You swore regardless.
The fire in you was always greater than wisdom.

Seven sons surround you,
their faces drawn with grief and fury-
seven mirrors of the flame you kindled,
seven oaths that cannot be unsworn,
seven dooms you set in motion
with a word spoken beneath darkening skies.

In the moments before your spirit
consumes the flesh it can no longer bear,
your life returns to you in surges-
each memory a forge-flash, blinding and brief:
the Light before it failed,
the craft that captured it,
the love that could not hold you,
the blood upon the white quays of Alqualonde.

Seven times your memory will burn,
and in each burning you shall wear
the face of one who shaped your fire-
father, wife, rival, friend-
spirits of Valinor's golden age,
whose fates you wove into the doom of all.

What matters now is not whether you prevail,
for the darkness has already won your body.
What matters is the brightness of the burning-
how fiercely your spirit blazes
before the ash takes everything.

Three times you will curse the name of Morgoth.
Then the flame goes out,
and your like shall never again appear in Arda.
But the Oath remains.
```

## 3. Fingolfin — The Last Duel

*Fast campaign. 10 fixed runs (7 wounds + 3 falls). Each run is one heartbeat of the duel with Morgoth. Score-based. Heroes are those Fingolfin loved and led.*

```
You ride alone across the dust of Anfauglith.
Behind you, the world is ashes-
your nephews slain, your vassals fallen,
four hundred years of vigilant peace
broken in a single dawn of fire.

There is no host at your back.
No banner flies beside yours.
Rochallor bears you onward,
and the hooves strike the dead earth
like the drumbeat of a war already lost.

You know you cannot win.
This is not hope that drives you forward-
it is the refusal to be less
than what you swore to be
when you led your people across the Grinding Ice.

You sound your horn before the brazen doors.
You name the Dark Lord craven.
You name him lord of slaves.
And he comes forth-
for even Morgoth cannot refuse a king
who no longer fears death.

He towers above you like a stormcloud,
iron-crowned, shadow-clad,
and Grond descends like thunder
to break the earth apart.

But you are swifter.
In each heartbeat between hammer-fall and death,
a face flashes before you-
someone you loved, someone you led,
someone you left behind upon the farther shore.
Each memory lends your sword-arm strength.
Each dodge is a life remembered.

Ten times Grond shall fall.
Seven times Ringil shall answer,
and Morgoth shall cry out in anguish,
and the hosts of Angband
shall fall upon their faces in dismay.
Three times you shall be beaten to your knees,
and three times rise again.

What matters is not the ending-
for the pits of Grond shall swallow you at last,
and Morgoth's foot shall find your throat.
What matters is the wound you leave upon him:
seven scars that never heal,
a limp he bears forever,
proof that the Noldor were not nothing.

Draw Ringil. It glitters like ice.
You gleam beneath the shadow like a star.
The duel begins.
```

## 4. Luthien — Tinuviel's Lament

*Open campaign. 7 Silmarils to win. No death limit (the song never stops). Weak, ordinary heroes shaped by Luthien's song. Heavy curse stacking. Singing before Mandos.*

```
You stand in the Halls of the Dead,
and your beloved lies beyond all reach.

You are not a warrior.
You are not a king.
You are a voice-
the daughter of starlight and ancient power,
and the only weapon you possess
is the song that made Morgoth sleep
and broke the towers of Sauron.

Now you sing before Mandos,
the Doomsman who has never wept,
the keeper of all fates who bends for none.
You sing of two sorrows woven into one:
the grief of the Eldar, bound to a world that fades,
and the grief of Men, who pass beyond it
into a silence even the Valar cannot hear.

He listens. He does not yield.
Not yet.

Seven lights, he demands.
Seven proofs that love endures
where sword and crown and craft have failed.
Bring them from the Enemy's deepest dark,
and he will hear your plea in full.

But you have no armies.
No bright-mailed princes answer your call.
The voices your song gives shape
are small and quiet ones-
the hunter who guarded the border,
the minstrel who played in empty halls,
the hound who chose loyalty over his master,
the mortal man who wandered into an enchanted wood
and loved what he could not keep.

They are not mighty.
Their names are written nowhere in the great tales.
Yet your song makes them luminous,
and in the darkness of Angband,
an ordinary heart carrying the weight of your voice
burns brighter than any king
who carries only his own pride.

They will fall. They will fall often.
Each death will deepen the shadow upon your song,
each loss a crack in the voice
that once silenced Morgoth himself.
But you will not stop singing.
You have never stopped.
You will sing until Mandos yields,
or until the world itself grows silent.

The song begins.
Let it be the most fair ever woven,
and the most sorrowful the world shall hear.
```

## 5. Earendil — The Mariner's Hope

*Long campaign. 15 Silmarils. No defeat condition. Dream-visions sent by Ulmo. Heroes are refugees of Sirion. Gathering proofs of light for the Valar.*

```
You dream of lights scattered in darkness.

By day you walk among the broken-
survivors of Gondolin, exiles of Nargothrond,
wanderers from the ruin of Doriath-
all gathered here upon the shore,
where the river meets the sea
and the last free people of Beleriand
huddle in the shadow of defeat.

By night, a voice reaches you-
deep as the foundations of the world,
patient as the tides.
Ulmo speaks, though you do not know his name.
He shows you visions:
a jewel that burns upon a brow,
a ship that sails among the stars,
a light that the Enemy cannot extinguish.

You do not understand.
But you cannot look away.

In the visions you descend-
not in body but in spirit,
in the prophetic questing of your soul-
into depths where Morgoth's shadow
has swallowed every brightness.
And there, impossibly, you find
that the light endures.

The people of Sirion are your proof.
Each one a survivor: the shipwright whose patience
outlasts the sea, the king-in-exile
whose star has not yet risen,
your mother whose silver feet
once walked the hidden city's heights,
your father who carried a god's whisper
through the fall of everything he loved.

They are remnants. Refugees. The last of the last.
Yet each time one of them walks into darkness
and returns bearing light,
another proof is gathered:
that Morgoth's victory is not total,
that beauty survives where it should not,
that the Valar must act, or all is truly lost.

Fifteen lights you must gather.
Fifteen proofs that the world is worth saving.
There is no count of deaths to end your quest,
for the sea is patient, and Ulmo does not despair.
But with each loss, the refugees grow wearier,
and the shore grows darker,
and the voice in the deep grows harder to hear.

When the last proof is found,
you will wake with the certainty of stars:
a ship must be built,
a road must be sailed
that no mortal vessel has ever traveled,
and upon your brow a Silmaril must burn
to light the way through the door of night.

The dream awaits.
Close your eyes, mariner.
The sea is calling.
```

## 6. Maedhros — Into the Fire

*Despair campaign. 7 fixed runs (one per brother). Inverse victory: each Silmaril adds curses. Heroes are the sons of Feanor. Always ends in the chasm of fire.*

```
The war is over.
Morgoth is overthrown.
The Silmarils are found.

And you have never been more lost.

You stand in the wreckage of Angband,
eldest son of the flame that burned the world,
the Oath still singing in your blood
like iron drawn too long through fire.
Six brothers lie behind you-
fallen at Doriath, at Sirion, at the gates-
and Maglor alone remains,
his voice breaking as he begs you
to surrender the jewels, to trust in mercy,
to let the Valar judge what the Oath demanded.

But you cannot.
For if the Oath was false-
if it could have been broken, set aside, unmade-
then Alqualonde was not necessity.
Doriath was not fate.
The blood upon the docks at Sirion
was not the price of a curse
but the choice of your own hand.

You stole the Silmarils in the dark of Eonwe's camp.
You took the jewel in your remaining hand-
the left, the one Thangorodrim left you-
and it burned.

Not your flesh alone.
Your spirit.

The Silmarils were hallowed by Varda,
and they will not suffer the touch
of one who came to them through evil,
even evil done in the name of recovering them.
The light your father made,
the light he commanded you to reclaim,
rejects you.

Seven times you shall descend
into the memory of what you have done.
Each brother's face will greet you-
father, hunter, craftsman, singer,
the wrathful, the fleet, the lost-
and in each face you will see
not the enemy's darkness
but your own.

Each Silmaril you bring back
will not lighten your burden.
It will deepen it.
Each proof of light will prove
how far you fell from it.
The brighter the jewel,
the darker the hand that holds it.

There is a chasm in the earth,
a wound torn open by the breaking of Thangorodrim,
and at its root the fires still burn.
You know already that you will go there.
You have known since the jewel first seared your palm.

The only question left
is how many ghosts you must face
before you find the courage
to let the fire take you.

The Silmaril burns.
It will not stop burning.
Begin.
```

## 7. Maglor — The Singer's Lament

*Very long campaign. 21 fixed runs (7 brothers x 3 Silmarils). Score-based. Player begins confused, not knowing who they are. Silmarils restore memory fragments. Identity revealed gradually. Wanderer by the sea.*

```
Your hands hurt.
You do not remember why.

You walk beside the sea,
and the waves speak in voices you almost know.
Songs rise in your throat unbidden-
melodies you did not learn,
words in a language older than the stars-
and you sing them to the water
because there is no one else to hear.

Who are you?

Your hands burn with a pain that has no wound.
Your voice carries grief you cannot name.
The salt wind brings fragments-
a face, half-remembered.
A hall of song, now silent.
A plain between mountains
where you held a realm once,
or someone did,
or someone who wore your face.

You do not know.
You know only that you are walking,
and that you have walked for longer
than memory can hold,
and that the sea does not answer
no matter how you sing.

Twenty-one times you will descend
into the darkness beneath the world,
searching for something you cannot name-
rest, perhaps, or death, or the answer
to the question your broken mind
will not let you ask:
Who am I, and what have I done?

Each light you recover from the deep
will restore a piece of you-
a brother's voice, a harp-string's ring,
the scent of trees that shone with gold.
Then darker memories will follow:
blood on white stone,
children weeping in the ruin of a harbor,
and a jewel held in a hand that it burned.

By the end, you will know everything.
You will know your name.
You will know what your father swore
and what you did to keep that swearing.
You will know why your hands burn,
and why your brother chose the fire,
and why you could not follow him.

The knowledge will not free you.
It will confirm what the waves
have whispered all along:
you are the singer,
and the shore is endless,
and the song will never be done.

Walk on.
The tide is coming in.
```

## 8. Sauron — The Unchosen Road

*Medium campaign. 7 fixed runs. Score determines ending. Player identity hidden (revealed gradually). High score = redemption (non-canonical). Low score = canonical fall. Heroes are voices in an internal argument.*

```
The war is over, and your master is gone.

They dragged him from his throne-
the Valar, the Host of the West,
powers you once served beside
in a life you can barely remember-
and they cast him through the Door of Night
into the Void beyond the world.

You knelt before the herald.
You spoke words of contrition.
You were not wholly false.
Something in you trembled
when the towers fell-
something old, something buried
beneath centuries of shadow,
that remembers what it was
to shape things out of love
rather than dominion.

The herald has spoken:
return to the Blessed Realm.
Submit to judgment.
Begin the long road of penance.
The door is open.
The ships wait in the harbor.

But you hesitate.

For if you return, you must kneel.
Not before a dark lord in a fortress of iron,
but before the light itself-
before those who will see everything,
every deed, every wolf, every prisoner
left to die in a pit beneath your tower,
every shape you wore to do
what you told yourself was necessary.

Seven times you will descend
into the wreckage of what you were-
the craftsman, the servant, the deceiver,
the lord of wolves, the defeated-
and each descent is a voice
in the argument that will decide your fate.

Each light you bring back from the darkness
draws you closer to the ship,
closer to the humiliation of mercy,
closer to the unbearable hope
that what you broke can be remade.

Each failure pulls you toward the shadow-
the whisper that says you need not kneel,
that you could order this broken world yourself,
better than your master did,
without the cruelty, without the waste-
a lie you have always been skilled at telling.

After seven descents,
the ships will sail.
You will either stand upon the deck
with the light of Valinor before you,
or you will watch the white sails
diminish into the West
and turn your face to the East
where new dominions await
and new rings may yet be forged.

One of these roads is mercy.
The other is history.

The herald waits.
Choose.
```

## 9. Maedhros — The Precipice of Pain

*Survival campaign. 12 runs (Silmaril-gated). Player identity unknown at start. Heroes are fragments of dissolving identity. Extremely weak early, grows stronger. Memory restored through recovered Silmarils. Ends with rescue by Fingon.*

```
Pain.

That is what you are.
Not a name. Not a body.
A single white line of agony
drawn from wrist to sky,
and below you, nothing,
and above you, nothing,
and the wind that never stops.

Steel bites your wrist.
How long has it bitten?
A day? A year?
There was a time before the steel-
you are almost certain of this-
but the memory is a coal
that crumbles when you reach for it.

Cold. Wind. The burning in the wrist.
Thirst that never kills.
Hunger that gnaws but will not grant
the mercy of an ending.
This is what remains
when everything else is taken:
the body's stubborn refusal to die,
breath after breath after breath,
and the darkness that asks nothing of you
but endurance.

Somewhere far below-
or is it far above?-
voices call. You cannot tell
if they are real or if the wind
has learned to imitate the living.

Twelve times you will reach
into the abyss of your own unmaking,
grasping for something-
a name, a face, a reason-
any fragment of the self
that hung here once
before the pain devoured it.

At first you will find almost nothing.
Shards. Whispers.
A color you once loved.
A voice singing a song
that makes your broken heart
ache with a longing
it cannot explain.

Then, slowly, more:
a father's command.
Brothers kneeling on grey earth.
An oath spoken beneath failing light.
Ships burning in a harbor far away.
And a treachery-
not yours, yet somehow
yours to bear.

By the twelfth fragment,
you will know your name.
You will know the steel,
and the mountain,
and the thirty years of silence.
And you will hear
what is not the wind at all
but a harp, playing softly,
a melody from a life before the dark-
and a voice calling your name
with a love that crossed
the Grinding Ice to find you.

He cannot break the steel.
But he carries a knife.
And he will cut you free.

Hold on.
The eagle is coming.
```

## 10. The Dispossessed — Seven Fires

*Fast campaign. 7 fixed runs. Score-based. Each run is one son's life seen through dying Feanor's prophetic vision. Companion piece to Spirit of Fire. Same ash-field, looking forward instead of backward.*

```
You are dying,
and you can see everything.

The same ash-field. The same smoke.
The same peaks of Thangorodrim
mocking the oath you cannot unspeak.
But now the clarity of death
grants you a sight beyond sight-
not backward through your own life,
but forward through theirs.

Seven faces surround you.
Seven sons kneel upon the grey earth,
and in each face you see
not only who they are
but who they will become.

Maedhros-
your firstborn, copper-haired, your father's brow.
You see the precipice.
You see the hand.
You see the crown surrendered
and the long war fought with what remains.
You see the chasm, and the fire,
and a jewel falling with him
into the heart of the earth.

Maglor-
always singing, even now.
You see the song that never ends,
the children he will love
though he has no right to love them,
and the shoreline stretching on forever.

You see them all.
The hunter whose hound will choose a stranger's cause.
The dark one who will build great things
and burn them with his temper.
The one who wears your face-
your Curufin, your mirror-
whom even his own son will disown.
The twins, still young, still bright,
who should never have been allowed to swear.

Seven fires you kindled in the Blessed Realm.
Seven lives shaped by your hands,
your pride, your genius, your rage.
Each run is a son's life unfolding in your dying sight-
and in each life you will see
not the Enemy's ruin
but your own.

What matters is not whether they prevail.
You already know they will not.
What matters is the burning-
whether the fires you kindled
shine with something more than wrath,
whether there is mercy in the flame
as well as doom.

You have seen everything.
You command them to hold the Oath regardless.

Look upon your sons.
The vision begins.
```

## 11. Finrod — Song Against Darkness

*Short campaign. 6 fixed runs. Unique scoring: victories add light, defeats subtract. Balance determines how Finrod's sacrifice is remembered. The duel with Sauron is unwinnable. Finrod always dies. The question is the quality of the light.*

```
You swore an oath once-
not in pride, not in fury,
not beneath the darkening of the Trees
with Iluvatar as witness
and the Everlasting Darkness as the price.

You swore in gratitude.
A mortal man cut through a ring of enemies
to save your life upon a burning field,
and you gave him your ring
and your word:
if ever his house should call,
you would come.

His son has come.
And your word is due.

Your people will not follow you.
Sons of Feanor dwell in your own halls,
and their father's Oath
hangs heavier than your promise.
Of all Nargothrond, only ten remain faithful-
Edrahil and nine whose names
the great tales will not record,
whose loyalty outweighs their wisdom.

You go north, disguised by song.
Eleven against the darkness.
You know the odds.

At the bridge of the isle that was once your tower-
the fortress you built and Sauron stole,
now filled with wolves and horror-
he perceives you.
He challenges you.

And so the duel begins:
your voice against his,
song against song,
light of Valinor against the malice of Morgoth.
You sing of the Blessed Realm,
of freedom, of the works of the Valar,
of the Trees before they failed.
He sings of treachery,
of the blood at Alqualonde,
of oaths that break the oath-makers,
of shadows that hide in every heart.

Six times you will face the darkness.
Six verses of a song you cannot win-
for his power here is greater,
and the weight of Morgoth's will
is behind every note he sings.

But you can resist.
Each light you bring into the dark
is a truth spoken against his lies,
a companion's courage upheld,
a moment when the song of Valinor
pierces the shadow
and proves it is not absolute.

Each failure is the darkness closing in-
the disguise stripped away,
the pit, the wolf that comes by night,
a companion taken,
a verse that falters.

You will die.
This is certain.
The wolf will come for Beren at the last,
and you will break your chains
by the power of your voice alone,
and you will fight it with bare hands,
and you will not survive.

But the quality of the sacrifice
is yours to shape.
Whether it blazes like the light of Valinor
or gutters like a candle in the wind-
that is the only question left.

An oath of love brought you here.
Let the song begin.
```

## Style Notes

### Structural Pattern
Each intro follows the same emotional arc:
1. **Situation** — Where you are, what has happened (2-3 stanzas)
2. **Antagonist** — Morgoth's presence and threat (1-2 stanzas)
3. **Mechanics veil** — The scenario's rules conveyed through lore metaphor:
   - Glorfindel: "You will return many times" (open campaign, death/rebirth cycle)
   - Feanor: "Seven times your memory will burn" (7 fixed runs, score-based)
   - Fingolfin: "Ten times Grond shall fall" (10 runs, 7 wounds + 3 falls)
   - Luthien: "Seven lights... you will not stop singing" (7 Silmarils, infinite attempts, heavy curses)
   - Earendil: "Fifteen lights... no count of deaths" (15 Silmarils, no defeat condition, dream-visions)
   - Maedhros Fire: "Seven times... each Silmaril will deepen it" (7 runs, inverse victory, always ends in fire)
   - Maglor: "Twenty-one times... each light will restore a piece" (21 runs, gradual identity recovery)
   - Sauron: "Seven descents... the ships will sail" (7 runs, score = light vs shadow meter, determines ending)
   - Maedhros Thangorodrim: "Twelve times... by the twelfth fragment" (12 Silmaril-gated runs, weak-to-strong, rescue ending)
   - Dispossessed: "Seven fires... each run is a son's life" (7 runs, score-based, Feanor's forward-vision)
   - Finrod: "Six times... six verses of a song you cannot win" (6 runs, +/- scoring, sacrifice quality)
4. **Emotional stakes** — What victory and defeat mean thematically (1-2 stanzas)
5. **Closing** — Short, forward-looking call to action (1-2 lines)

### Voice
- Second person ("You"), present tense
- Prose-verse: not strict meter, but rhythmic and elevated
- No rhyme scheme, but internal echoes and repetitions
- Tolkien's characteristic devices: tripling, negation-as-affirmation ("not hope... the refusal to be less"), kennings, and the weight of names
- Each poem names specific lore elements (Thangorodrim, Grond, Ringil, Alqualonde) to ground the fantasy

### Mechanical Transparency
The poems avoid game terminology but make the rules legible:
- **Run count**: "Seven times," "Ten times," "Fifteen lights," "Twenty-one times," "Twelve times," "Six times"
- **Scoring**: "brightness of the burning" (Feanor), "the wound you leave" (Fingolfin), "the quality of the sacrifice" (Finrod), "whether there is mercy in the flame" (Dispossessed)
- **Hero rotation**: "wear the face of one who shaped your fire" (Feanor), "a face flashes before you" (Fingolfin), "voices your song gives shape" (Luthien), "each brother's face" (Maedhros Fire), "voices in the argument" (Sauron), "each run is a son's life" (Dispossessed), "companions" (Finrod)
- **Identity mechanics**: Glorfindel recovers his name; Maglor doesn't know who he is ("Who am I?"); Sauron's identity is hidden from the player; Maedhros Thangorodrim begins as pure pain with no self; Finrod knows exactly who he is from the start
- **Win/loss framing**: Glorfindel has true victory/defeat; Feanor/Fingolfin/Dispossessed always end the same way with score as measure; Luthien/Earendil have no defeat; Maedhros Fire always ends in the chasm; Sauron's score determines the ending (canonical = failure); Finrod always dies but the light varies; Maedhros Thangorodrim always ends in rescue
- **Death/curse mechanics**: "Each fall will draw deeper" (Glorfindel), "each death will deepen the shadow upon your song" (Luthien heavy curses), "the shore grows darker" (Earendil soft), "each Silmaril will deepen it" (Maedhros Fire inverse), "each failure pulls toward shadow" (Sauron meter), "each failure is the darkness closing in" (Finrod -1 balance)
- **Difficulty curves**: Most scenarios are flat or cursed-harder; Maedhros Thangorodrim uniquely starts impossibly hard and gets easier ("at first you will find almost nothing... then, slowly, more")
