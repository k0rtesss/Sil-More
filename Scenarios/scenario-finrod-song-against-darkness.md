# Song Against Darkness: Finrod's Last Stand

## Scenario Design Document

---

## I. Lore Foundation

### The Sacrifice of Finrod (Silmarillion, "Of Beren and Lúthien")

When Beren son of Barahir came to Doriath and won the love of Lúthien Tinúviel, Thingol her father set an impossible price: bring me a Silmaril from Morgoth's crown, and you may have my daughter's hand. None thought Beren could achieve this. But Finrod Felagund, King of Nargothrond, was bound by an oath.

Years before, Barahir had saved Finrod's life in the Dagor Bragollach, cutting through a ring of enemies when all seemed lost. In gratitude, Finrod swore eternal friendship and gave Barahir his ring, vowing that if any of Barahir's house called upon him, he would come—even unto death.

When Beren came to Nargothrond bearing that ring, Finrod knew what his oath demanded. His people, stirred to rebellion by Celegorm and Curufin (sons of Fëanor dwelling in his halls), refused to march against Morgoth for a Silmaril—Fëanor's Oath hung too heavy over that jewel. But ten remained faithful to their king: Edrahil chief among them.

They went north disguised as orcs, by the power of Finrod's songs. At the bridge of Tol-in-Gaurhoth—the Isle of Werewolves, where Sauron ruled from a captured tower—they were challenged. Sauron perceived that something was amiss, and he challenged Finrod to a contest of power, wrought in songs.

Finrod sang of the light of Valinor, of the Blessed Realm beyond the Sea, of freedom and the works of the Valar. His voice was fair and filled with the memory of ages under the Two Trees, before darkness marred the world.

But Sauron sang in answer of treachery and betrayal, of the pride of the Noldor and the Kinslaying at Alqualondë, of oaths foresworn and trust broken, of shadows that lie in every heart. His power was greater, for Tol-in-Gaurhoth was his seat of strength, and the weight of Morgoth's malice was behind him.

The disguise was stripped away. Finrod and his companions were cast into a deep pit, and Sauron set a werewolf to devour them one by one, seeking to learn who they were and what they sought.

Each day the wolf came. Each day one of the ten fell. Edrahil was taken. The others followed. Finrod and Beren sang songs of valor to give them courage, but they could not break the bonds.

When at last the wolf came for Beren, Finrod's wrath and despair gave him such strength that he burst his chains—by the power of his voice and his will alone. He leaped upon the werewolf with bare hands and fought it in the dark, strangling the beast even as its fangs tore him. When Beren reached him, Finrod spoke his last words, bidding Beren farewell, and died.

Afterward, Lúthien came with Huan the great hound. She broke Sauron's power with a song of love and command. The tower fell. Beren was freed. And together Beren and Lúthien won the Silmaril—the very quest Finrod had died to make possible.

Finrod Felagund, fairest and most noble of the house of Finarfin, went thereafter to the halls of Mandos. Tolkien writes that he "walks with Finarfin his father beneath the trees in Eldamar"—released from death, returned to the Blessed Realm, his sacrifice honored by the Valar themselves.

### Thematic Analysis

This scenario inverts the narrative expectations of heroism. Finrod *loses* the duel with Sauron. He is captured, bound, and watches his companions die one by one. The quest he undertook fails—he never reaches Morgoth, never recovers the Silmaril himself. In every conventional sense, this is a tale of defeat.

And yet it is one of the most heroic acts in the entire Silmarillion.

The inversion is the key: **victory in defeat, strength in sacrifice, light in darkness**. Finrod's greatest triumph is not winning the song-contest but *keeping his oath*. His power is not in overpowering Sauron but in dying for a friend. His legacy is not the Silmaril he won but the Silmaril Beren and Lúthien won *because* Finrod gave his life to bring Beren to the edge of hope.

This creates a **unique scoring dynamic**: the player is not trying to "win" in the traditional sense. The duel with Sauron is unwinnable. The imprisonment is inescapable. The companions will die. Finrod will die. These are fixed narrative points. What the player controls is *how much light Finrod brings into that darkness*—how many moments of truth he speaks, how much courage he kindles in his doomed companions, how much meaning his sacrifice carries.

**High score (many "Silmarils" recovered)** = Finrod fought brilliantly in the song-contest, upheld his companions' spirits, and died with his oath fulfilled in glory. The sacrifice shines like a star. Beren is saved with hope intact. The quest succeeds.

**Low score (few victories, many "deaths")** = Sauron's victory is more complete. The song-duel is one-sided. The companions despair. Finrod dies in darkness, the oath barely kept. Beren survives but is broken. The quest succeeds—but at terrible cost.

Either way, Finrod dies. Either way, Beren is saved. But the *quality* of the sacrifice—the light it brings—varies.

### Key Lore Touchstones Referenced

- **The Dagor Bragollach (Battle of Sudden Flame)** — Morgoth's great assault that broke the Siege of Angband. Finrod's realm was overrun. Barahir saved him. (Silmarillion, "Of the Ruin of Beleriand")
- **The Ring of Barahir** — Finrod's gift, a ring of gold with two serpents and green stones, token of eternal friendship. It passed to Beren, and later to Aragorn. (Silmarillion, Appendices to LotR)
- **The Oath of Finrod** — Sworn to Barahir and his kin: if any should call, Finrod would aid them unto death. Unlike Fëanor's Oath, this was an oath of love and loyalty. (Silmarillion)
- **The House of Bëor** — The first of the Edain (Men) to enter Beleriand. Finrod found them in Ossiriand and taught them language and lore. His friendship with Men was unique among the Noldor. (Silmarillion, "Of the Coming of Men")
- **Nargothrond** — Finrod's great underground fortress-city, built in the Caverns of Narog after the model of Menegroth. The greatest work of the Noldor in hiding. (Silmarillion)
- **Celegorm and Curufin's treachery** — The sons of Fëanor dwelt in Nargothrond after losing their own realms. When Finrod declared his intent to aid Beren, they stirred the people to rebellion, for Fëanor's Oath forbade aid in recovering a Silmaril for another. (Silmarillion, "Of Beren and Lúthien")
- **The Ten Faithful** — Led by Edrahil, Finrod's chief captain. They alone followed when the king's people refused. Their names are not recorded, but their loyalty is. (Silmarillion)
- **Tol-in-Gaurhoth (Isle of Werewolves)** — Originally Minas Tirith, a watchtower built by Finrod himself on an island in the river Sirion. Sauron captured it and filled it with werewolves and horrors. (Silmarillion, "Of Beren and Lúthien")
- **The Song-Duel** — A contest of power wrought in music and poetry, each singer weaving spells to attack and defend. A uniquely Tolkienian form of magical combat. (Silmarillion, "Of Beren and Lúthien"; *The Lay of Leithian* in HoME III)
- **The imprisonment and deaths** — Sauron sought to learn their purpose by terror. One by one the werewolf devoured Finrod's companions in the dark. (Silmarillion)
- **Finrod's death** — Breaking his bonds by the power of song and will, he wrestled the werewolf and died. (Silmarillion)
- **Lúthien and Huan's victory** — Lúthien sang songs of enchantment. Huan, the great hound of Valinor, fought Sauron in wolf-form. Sauron yielded the tower and fled. (Silmarillion, "Of Beren and Lúthien")
- **The Quest of the Silmaril** — Beren and Lúthien reached Angband, won the Silmaril, escaped (barely), and fulfilled the bride-price. All made possible by Finrod's sacrifice. (Silmarillion)
- **Finrod's return** — Alone among those slain by Sauron or Morgoth in the First Age, Finrod was released from Mandos during the Elder Days and returned to Valinor. His nobility earned this singular grace. (Silmarillion; *The Peoples of Middle-earth*, HoME XII)

## II. Scenario Overview

**Name:** Song Against Darkness
**Subtitle:** Finrod's Last Stand
**Type:** Short Campaign (run-count-based)
**Runs:** 6 (representing the stages of the contest and imprisonment)
**End Condition:** After 6 runs, the campaign concludes regardless of outcome
**Scoring:** Unique mechanic—"Silmarils" (light/victories) ADD to score; defeats/deaths SUBTRACT from score. The balance determines the ending's tone.
**Hero Pool:** 9 heroes (3 new + 6 existing) representing those central to Finrod's quest and sacrifice

### Narrative Frame

The player is Finrod Felagund, King of Nargothrond, bound by oath to aid Beren son of Barahir in an impossible quest. You stand at the bridge of Tol-in-Gaurhoth, disguised as an orc by the power of your songs. Sauron the Cruel perceives something amiss and challenges you.

You know you cannot win. Sauron's power here is too great. But you can resist. You can speak truth against his lies. You can bring light into his darkness, even if only for a moment.

Each "run" represents a stage of the confrontation:
1. The approach to the tower
2. The first challenge—Sauron's suspicion
3. The song-duel itself
4. The imprisonment—the first deaths
5. The long dark—watching companions fall
6. The final sacrifice—death for Beren

The mechanics invert the typical game structure:
- **Recovering a "Silmaril" (victory in a run)** = You bring light into darkness. A moment of truth spoken, a verse of the song that pierces Sauron's lies, a companion's courage upheld. +1 to the balance of the sacrifice.
- **Defeat/death in a run** = Sauron's power prevails. Darkness covers the light. A companion despairs. A verse of the song falters. -1 to the balance of the sacrifice.

The final score determines not *whether* Finrod dies (he always does) but *how his death is remembered*—as a blaze of defiant light, or as a flicker swallowed by shadow.

### Progression Structure

| Run | Stage | Narrative Focus |
|-----|-------|-----------------|
| 1 | The Road North | The oath sworn. The ten companions. The weight of choice. |
| 2 | The Disguise | Songs of concealment. The power of Finrod's voice. The shadow's notice. |
| 3 | The Challenge | Sauron's suspicion. The duel begins. Light against darkness. |
| 4 | Songs of Power | The contest's height. Valinor's memory against Morgoth's malice. |
| 5 | The Pit | Imprisonment. The werewolf's nightly coming. Companions falling. |
| 6 | The Last Battle | The wolf comes for Beren. Finrod breaks his chains. Death and victory. |

Tolkien notes this as particularly tragic—the most loyal companion, the first to die, unable to see the quest's end. Edrahil embodies faithful service unto death, the ideal of the bond between king and follower.

Finrod never married in Middle-earth—he remained faithful to her across the Sundering Sea. When he was released from Mandos and allowed to return to Valinor (a unique grace), they were reunited. Amarië represents the love Finrod carried in his heart through all his years in exile—a memory of light, a reason to remain noble, a waiting grace.

### Design Rationale

Each hero represents a voice in the narrative of Finrod's sacrifice:

**The Oath's Origin:**
- **Barahir** — The friend who saved Finrod, creating the debt of honor. The reason Finrod goes to his death.

**The Faithful:**
- **Edrahil** — The loyal captain who follows unto death. The ideal of service.
- **Beren** — The one Finrod dies for. The bearer of the Ring, the fulfiller of the quest.

**The Bonds of Love:**
- **Amarië** — The love waiting in Valinor. The light Finrod remembers in the dark.
- **Andreth** — The mortal perspective. The wisdom that sees Finrod's sacrifice from the outside.

**The Avengers:**
- **Lúthien** — The power of love that breaks Sauron's tower. The one who finishes what Finrod began.
- **Huan** — The strength of Valinor. The hound whose might defeats the werewolves.

**The Family:**
- **Finarfin** — The father who chose mercy over pride. The gentle strength Finrod inherited.
- **Galadriel** — The sister who understood Finrod's nobility. The heir to his wisdom.

### Thematic Groupings

**Light-bearers (argue for the nobility of the sacrifice):**
- Barahir: "You honor the oath. This is what friendship means."
- Edrahil: "We follow because you are worth following."
- Amarië: "You carry my light into the dark. You are not alone."
- Lúthien: "Your death makes the victory possible. Without you, Beren never reaches me."
- Finarfin: "To choose mercy over pride is the truest kingship."

**Witnesses (show the cost and the context):**
- Beren: "I did not ask for this. But I see what you give, and I am humbled."
- Andreth: "You Elves see death as a curse. But to die for love is the greatest gift."
- Huan: "Loyalty unto death is written in the Music itself."
- Galadriel: "I see you, brother. I see your choice. And it is beautiful and terrible."

## V. Narrative Script

### Opening Scene (Plays Once, at Campaign Start)

> **Title: The Ring on His Hand**
>
> You stand in the throne-hall of Nargothrond, and the weight of choice presses
> upon you like the weight of the mountain above. Before you stands Beren son
> of Barahir—ragged, wild-eyed, desperate. On his hand he wears your ring,
> the green stones glinting in the lamplight like distant stars.
>
> You know this ring. You gave it to his father on a battlefield, when Barahir
> cut through fire and darkness to save your life. You swore an oath then:
> *If any of your house call upon me, I will come—even unto death.*
>
> And now death calls.
>
> Beren's quest is impossible: to steal a Silmaril from Morgoth's crown, from
> the deepest heart of Angband, from the iron throne of the Enemy of the World.
> None could achieve this. Not even you.
>
> Your people stand behind you, murmuring. Celegorm and Curufin, the sons of
> Fëanor who dwell in your halls, stand with arms crossed, faces dark. They
> know what you will say, and they are already moving to prevent it. The Oath
> of Fëanor forbids aid in recovering a Silmaril for another. If you help
> Beren, you defy them. And your people will not follow.
>
> Edrahil stands to your right, his hand on his sword-hilt, his eyes on you
> alone.
>
> You remember Barahir's face as he cut you free from the encircling enemies.
> You remember the ring on his hand—this same ring—as he reached down to pull
> you from the ash. You remember saying: *I will not forget.*
>
> You look at Beren. You look at the ring. You speak.
>
> "I will come."
>
> Behind you, Celegorm's voice rises in anger. Your people waver. But Edrahil
> steps forward, and nine others follow him, and their loyalty is a flame in
> the growing dark.
>
> Ten companions. One impossible road. And the tower of Tol-in-Gaurhoth waiting
> in the north, where Sauron sits enthroned in a fortress you yourself once
> built.
>
> You know you will not return. But the oath was sworn, and the ring is on
> Beren's hand, and honor demands what honor demands.
>
> You walk out of Nargothrond into the shadow, and you do not look back.

### Memory Chapters (Run Progression — Type 0: Light)

These play at the START of each run. They represent stages of the quest.

---

#### Run 1 — The Road North

> **Title: The Weight of Choice**
>
> The road north from Nargothrond is long and dark. You travel in secret,
> hidden from Morgoth's spies, moving through the wild lands where Men dwell
> in hiding and Elves dare not go.
>
> Your ten companions are silent, grim. They know the odds. Edrahil walks at
> your right hand, and you see in his eyes the same acceptance you feel: this
> is likely death. But it is *chosen* death. Death with honor. Death for a
> friend.
>
> Beren walks apart, hunched under the weight of what he has asked. You watch
> him and remember his father—bold Barahir, bright-eyed, laughing even in the
> fire of battle. The son carries his father's courage, but also his father's
> doom. The House of Bëor was ever fated for sorrow and glory entwined.
>
> You remember Amarië's face in Valinor, before the Exile. She asked you once:
> "If you could choose between a long life in peace or a short life in glory,
> which would you choose?" You laughed and said: "Neither. I choose a long life
> in glory." She smiled, but her eyes were sad. She knew you better than you
> knew yourself.
>
> A short life. A glorious death. Perhaps she was always right.
>
> Ahead, the shadow of Thangorodrim rises against the northern stars. Somewhere
> in that darkness, Tol-in-Gaurhoth waits—the tower you built as a beacon of
> light, now corrupted into a dungeon of wolves.
>
> You begin to sing, softly, a song of concealment. Your companions join their
> voices to yours. The spell settles over you like a cloak, and when you look
> at each other, you see not Elves but orcs—foul, bent, loyal servants of the
> Enemy.
>
> The disguise is perfect. Your voice makes it so. You are Finrod Felagund,
> and in songs of power, none of the Noldor save Fëanor himself was your equal.
>
> But Sauron was a Maia before the world was made. And this is his fortress.
>
> The road narrows. The bridge of Tol-in-Gaurhoth rises ahead, stone span over
> black water, leading to the tower's gate.
>
> You do not hesitate. You walk forward, singing.

---

#### Run 2 — The Disguise

> **Title: Songs of Concealment**
>
> The gates of Tol-in-Gaurhoth loom above you—iron-bound, carved with runes of
> malice. You remember raising these gates in elder days, when this tower was
> Minas Tirith, the Tower of Guard, a beacon against Morgoth's forces. You
> built it to be impregnable.
>
> Sauron holds it now, and the irony is bitter.
>
> Guards stand at the gate—true orcs, corrupt and cruel. They see you
> approaching and call out: "What business?"
>
> Edrahil answers in the Black Speech, his voice rough and guttural. "We bring
> word from the north. Let us pass."
>
> The orcs hesitate. One narrows its eyes. "You have the look of servants of
> the Lord. But..." It sniffs the air. "Something is amiss."
>
> You feel Sauron's attention turn toward you—not his physical eyes, but his
> *will*, vast and cold, pressing against the spell of concealment like a hand
> testing a locked door.
>
> You sing, softly, weaving the disguise tighter. You sing of darkness and
> service, of the north and the iron mountains, of loyalty to Morgoth. The
> words are bitter in your mouth, but the spell requires belief—you must
> *become* what you pretend, at least in the song's logic.
>
> The orc steps back. The gate opens.
>
> But as you pass through, you feel Sauron's will sharpen. He has noticed. He
> does not know *what* you are—not yet—but he knows something is wrong.
>
> Beren whispers: "We should flee."
>
> You shake your head. "Too late. He has seen us. If we run, he will know. We
> go forward."
>
> Into the tower. Into the dark.

---

#### Run 3 — The Challenge

> **Title: The Duel Begins**
>
> The throne-hall of Tol-in-Gaurhoth is vast and cold. Sauron sits at its
> heart, not in his true form—which is formless and terrible—but in a shape
> of dark majesty, beautiful and cruel, eyes like embers in a face carved
> from night.
>
> He looks at you, and you feel his gaze strip away the outer layers of the
> disguise. The spell holds—barely—but his suspicion is certain now.
>
> "You come from the north," he says, voice soft as poisoned silk. "Yet I do
> not know you. And I know all my master's servants."
>
> You do not answer. Edrahil and the others stand frozen, hands on weapons
> they dare not draw.
>
> Sauron leans forward. "Let us play a game. I will sing, and you will answer.
> If your song is true, I will let you pass. If it is false..." He smiles.
> "The wolves are hungry."
>
> You know what this is: a duel of power, wrought in words and will. The same
> art you have used for concealment, he will use for unveiling. If he
> overpowers your voice, the disguise will shatter, and you will be revealed.
>
> But if you do not answer, you are revealed by silence.
>
> There is no escape. Only the contest.
>
> You draw breath. You begin to sing.
>
> Your voice rises, clear and bright, and you sing of light—the light of the
> Two Trees in Valinor, the radiance of the Blessed Realm before the Darkening,
> the works of the Valar in beauty and joy. You sing of freedom, of creation
> for creation's own sake, of the Music of the Ainur that shaped the world in
> love.
>
> Sauron's eyes narrow. He sings in answer.
>
> His voice is deep and cold, and he sings of treachery—the pride of the
> Noldor, the kinslaying at Alqualondë where blood stained the white quays,
> the oaths sworn and broken, the darkness in every heart that claims to be
> noble. He sings of Fëanor and the Silmarils, of the greed that drove the
> Noldor into exile, of the doom that lies upon them all.
>
> His words are true. That is the horror. Every accusation lands because it
> is not wholly a lie.
>
> You sing back: of redemption, of choice, of the freedom to become better
> than what you were. You sing of Bëor and his people, of the friendship
> between Elves and Men, of the hope that light might yet overcome the shadow.
>
> But Sauron's voice is stronger here. This is his seat of power. Morgoth's
> malice is behind him. And the weight of history—the Doom of Mandos, the
> blood of kin, the failure of the Noldor's pride—bears down on your song
> like a mountain pressing on a candle-flame.
>
> The disguise begins to fray. You see your companions flicker—Elf-shapes
> beneath the orc-seeming.
>
> Sauron sees it too. He laughs. And his voice rises to a shout, a command,
> and the spell shatters like glass.
>
> You stand revealed: Finrod Felagund, King of Nargothrond, far from home,
> surrounded by enemies.
>
> Sauron rises. "I know you now. And I know why you have come."
>
> The werewolves howl in the dark below. Guards pour into the hall.
>
> You are taken.

---

#### Run 4 — Songs of Power

> **Title: Light Against Darkness**
>
> The chains are cold. You sit in a pit beneath the tower, and the darkness
> is absolute—not merely the absence of light, but the *presence* of malice,
> Sauron's will pressing down like a weight on your chest.
>
> Your companions huddle near you. Edrahil's voice comes from the dark: "My
> king. I am sorry. I have failed you."
>
> You answer: "You have not failed. We knew the odds."
>
> Beren says: "This is my fault. You came for me."
>
> You turn toward his voice. "I came for your father. For an oath I swore when
> I still believed oaths were sacred. I do not regret it."
>
> Above, you hear Sauron's voice, cold and mocking: "You sang beautifully,
> Elf-king. But beauty is not strength. And here, in my domain, your light is
> nothing."
>
> You do not answer him. But you begin to sing—not a spell, not a weapon, but
> a *song*. The song of the Blessed Realm. The song of the stars over
> Cuiviénen where the Elves first woke. The song of Amarië waiting in Valinor,
> of Finarfin walking beneath the trees, of all the light that endures beyond
> Sauron's reach.
>
> Your companions join you. Their voices are weak, worn by fear and despair,
> but they sing. Edrahil's voice is steady. Beren's is fierce.
>
> Sauron's laughter echoes: "Sing, then. Sing while you can. The wolf will
> come tonight. One of you will feed it. And tomorrow night, another. Until
> you tell me what I wish to know."
>
> You do not stop singing.
>
> Because even if the song does not save you—even if Sauron's power is too
> great, even if the darkness swallows every note—the act of singing is itself
> defiance. To make music in hell is to deny hell's claim on your soul.
>
> The song rises. For a moment—one shining moment—the dark seems less absolute.
>
> Then you hear the howl. The scrape of claws on stone. The wolf is coming.
>
> Edrahil stands. "My king. It will be me. I am the eldest. Let me go first."
>
> You reach for him, but the chains hold you fast.
>
> The wolf's eyes gleam in the dark. Red as embers. Hungry.
>
> Edrahil does not scream. He sings, even as the jaws close. And the last
> words you hear from him are the words of the song you taught him in
> Nargothrond, in the days when the world was bright:
>
> *"A star shall shine upon the hour of our meeting."*
>
> The wolf drags him into the dark.
>
> You weep. But you do not stop singing.

---

#### Run 5 — The Pit

> **Title: The Long Dark**
>
> Days pass. Or perhaps it is weeks. In the absolute dark of Sauron's pit,
> time loses meaning. You mark its passage only by the coming of the wolf.
>
> Each night it comes. Each night one of your companions is taken.
>
> You learn their voices in the dark, in the songs you sing to give them
> courage. Their names are not preserved in the great tales—Tolkien
> records only Edrahil among the ten—but you know each one. One sings of
> the mountains of Dorthonion in a voice deep as stone. Another was a
> smith in Nargothrond, and speaks of the joy of making. A third hums
> the lullabies of Valinor that his mother sang before the Darkening.
>
> One by one, they are devoured.
>
> Sauron comes sometimes, standing at the edge of the pit, speaking into the
> dark: "Tell me who you are. Tell me why you came. Tell me what you seek."
>
> You do not answer. Beren does not answer. The others, even as they are
> dragged to the wolf, do not answer.
>
> Sauron's voice turns contemplative: "I admire this, in a way. Such loyalty.
> Such pointless, beautiful loyalty. You will all die, Elf-king. The quest
> will fail. Beren will never reach Lúthien again. The Silmaril will remain
> in Morgoth's crown. And your deaths will mean nothing."
>
> You sing in answer. Not words. Just a melody. The melody Amarië used to sing
> in Valinor, beneath the golden flowers of Laurelin's light.
>
> Sauron falls silent. And you realize: he knows this melody. He was in
> Valinor once, before the world was made, before he fell. He remembers.
>
> For a moment you think you hear something in his silence that might be
> regret. But then he laughs, and the moment passes, and the dark closes in
> again.
>
> The wolf comes. Another companion falls.
>
> You and Beren are all that remain.

---

#### Run 6 — The Last Battle

> **Title: Death and Victory**
>
> The wolf comes for Beren.
>
> You hear its breathing—hot, foul, eager. You hear Beren's sharp intake of
> breath, the rattle of his chains as he tries to stand.
>
> And something in you breaks. Not despair. Not fear. *Wrath*.
>
> You have watched your companions die. You have sung songs of light into
> absolute darkness. You have honored your oath to Barahir, kept faith with
> Beren, held the line against Sauron's malice. You have done everything that
> honor and love demanded.
>
> But you will not let the wolf take Beren.
>
> You sing—not a spell, not a weapon, but a *command*. Your voice rises with
> a power you did not know you still possessed, and you sing to the chains:
> *Break.*
>
> The iron shatters.
>
> Your hands are free.
>
> The wolf turns toward you, eyes blazing, jaws opening. It is twice the size
> of a natural wolf, bred from shadow and malice, its breath like the smoke
> of Angband.
>
> You have no sword. No armor. Only your hands and your will.
>
> You leap upon it.
>
> The battle is brief and terrible. The wolf's strength is immense, its jaws
> like iron traps. But you are Finrod Felagund, of the house of Finarfin,
> and your strength is not in your body but in your *spirit*—and the spirit
> does not yield.
>
> You lock your hands around its throat. It tears at you with claws and teeth.
> You do not let go.
>
> You sing, even as the fangs pierce your flesh. You sing the song of the
> Blessed Realm, the song of light, the song Amarië sang. You pour every last
> breath into the song, and the wolf *hears* it, and for one moment the
> shadow in its eyes flickers—not banished, but *seen*, recognized for the
> lie it is.
>
> The wolf dies. You have strangled it with bare hands.
>
> But the wounds are mortal. You know this.
>
> Beren crawls to you in the dark, calling your name. You reach for him,
> and your hand finds his, and you feel the ring—your ring, Barahir's
> ring—cold and solid against his finger.
>
> You smile. You have kept the oath.
>
> You whisper: "Beren. Go. Lúthien will come. She will break this tower. She
> will free you. Go to her. Win the Silmaril. Live."
>
> Beren is weeping. "You are dying for me. I did not ask for this."
>
> You say: "I know. But your father asked nothing either, when he saved me.
> This is what friendship means."
>
> The darkness is closing in. Not Sauron's darkness. The other kind. The
> darkness that comes for all living things.
>
> You think of Amarië. You think of Finarfin. You think of Nargothrond, the
> kingdom you will never see again.
>
> And you think: *I have lived well. I have loved. I have kept faith. I die
> free.*
>
> Your last breath carries the song. And the song does not end—even as your
> voice fails, even as your spirit departs, the melody lingers in the dark,
> bright and defiant.
>
> Beren holds your hand until it grows cold.
>
> Above, in the tower, Sauron sits in silence. He has won the duel. He has
> slain the king. But the song echoes in his halls, and he cannot silence it,
> and for the first time in ages uncounted he feels something he thought he
> had forgotten.
>
> Doubt.

### Defeat Chapters (Type 1: Run Ends in Death)

These play when a run ends in defeat. They represent Sauron's power prevailing,
the darkness closing in, the sacrifice losing its luster.

---

#### Run 1 Defeat — The Road Falters

> The road north is long, and the shadow is heavy. Your companions stumble.
> Beren looks at you with hollow eyes and asks: "Are we going to die?" And
> you cannot answer, because you do not know how to lie. The weight of choice
> becomes the weight of doubt.

---

#### Run 2 Defeat — The Disguise Cracks

> The spell falters before you even reach the gate. Sauron's will presses
> against your song like a hammer on glass, and the disguise shatters too
> soon, too easily. You are taken without even the dignity of the duel. Your
> voice, your greatest weapon, fails you.

---

#### Run 3 Defeat — The Duel Lost

> Sauron's song overwhelms yours like a wave over a candle. His voice
> speaks truths you cannot counter: the pride of the Noldor, the blood at
> Alqualondë, the doom that lies upon you all. Your song of light sounds
> hollow even to your own ears. You are cast into the pit not as a defiant
> king but as a defeated liar.

---

#### Run 4 Defeat — The Song Fades

> You try to sing in the darkness, but the words will not come. Your
> companions huddle in despair. Edrahil does not sing before the wolf takes
> him. He only weeps. And the silence that follows is heavier than the dark.

---

#### Run 5 Defeat — Despair in the Pit

> One by one your companions fall, and each death breaks you a little more.
> You do not sing. You cannot. The dark is too absolute, Sauron's power too
> great. When only you and Beren remain, you whisper: "I am sorry. I have
> failed you." And Beren does not answer.

---

#### Run 6 Defeat — The Hollow Sacrifice

> When the wolf comes for Beren, you try to break the chains, but your
> strength is spent. Your voice cracks. The iron holds. The wolf lunges—
> and you wrench free at the last instant, not by the power of song but
> by blind animal desperation. You fight the beast in silence, without
> music, without light, without the defiance that should have made this
> death mean something. The wolf dies. You die. Beren lives—but he holds
> the hand of a king who died gasping, not singing. The oath is kept.
> But barely. And the keeping of it carries no light into the world.

## VI. Hero Victory and Defeat Messages

Each message is written from Finrod's perspective, filtered through the
sacrifice. Victory messages (with Silmaril/light) show the meaning and beauty
of the sacrifice. Defeat messages show the cost and the darkness.

---

### 1. Barahir

**Victory:**

> *Friend.* I see your face in the fire—bold, bright, laughing even as the
> enemies closed around you. You cut through the darkness to save me, and I
> swore an oath I knew might cost me everything. And now it has. But I do
> not regret it. Because this—this death for your son—is what the oath
> meant. Not words spoken in a moment of gratitude, but a life given in the
> fullness of time. The light I carry into this darkness is *your* light,
> the light of that moment when you reached down and pulled me from the ash.
> I have honored the debt. The ring passes to Beren. And he will live, because
> I chose to die. That is enough.

**Defeat:**

> *Friend.* I am sorry. I swore to come if your house called, and I came. But
> the oath is hollow now—kept in letter, broken in spirit. Beren lives, but
> he lives because I died gasping, not singing. The sacrifice carries no
> light. The ring passes to your son, but the hand that wears it trembles,
> and the friendship it represents is shadowed by the manner of my death.
> You saved me with such glory, Barahir. I have repaid you in mere survival.
> Forgive me. I tried.

---

### 2. Edrahil

**Victory:**

> *Captain. Friend.* You stood forth when all others faltered. You gathered
> nine and led them north, knowing it was doom. And when the wolf came, you
> went first—not because you were commanded, but because you *chose*. Your
> last words were the song I taught you in Nargothrond, and they ring in my
> ears even now as I die: *"A star shall shine upon the hour of our meeting."*
> The star shines, Edrahil. You were the truest of my people, and your
> loyalty is a light that will outlast this darkness. I will see you again,
> in Mandos, and I will tell you: *you did not fail. You were glorious.*

**Defeat:**

> *Captain.* You followed me unto death, and the death was meaningless. The
> wolf took you, and you did not sing. You wept. And I could not save you.
> Your loyalty deserved better than this—a king who led you into a trap, a
> cause that failed, a darkness that swallowed your light. I am sorry. I am
> so sorry.

---

### 3. Amarië

**Victory:**

> *Beloved.* You wait for me in Valinor, and you do not know if I live or
> have fallen. But I carry you with me into this dark. Your voice is the song
> I sing. Your light is the memory that keeps me whole. When I break the
> chains and leap upon the wolf, it is not wrath that drives me but *love*—
> the love you taught me, the love that endures across the Sundering Sea, the
> love that says: *I will not let the darkness take those I have sworn to
> protect.* I am dying now, but I die free, and I die *yours*, and when I
> come at last to Mandos I will seek you, and the waiting will be over.

**Defeat:**

> *Beloved.* I am failing. The darkness is too strong. I cannot break the
> chains. I cannot keep the oath. I am dying in Sauron's pit, far from the
> light of Valinor, far from your voice, and the last thought I have is of
> your face, and the question: Will you forgive me for choosing this doom?

---

### 4. Andreth

**Victory:**

> *Wise-woman.* You said once that we Elves do not understand death, that we
> see it as a curse when it is a gift. I did not believe you then. But I
> understand now. I am dying, and it is not a curse. It is a *choice*. I
> chose to come. I chose to stand against Sauron. I chose to die for Beren.
> And in that choosing, I am more free than I have ever been. Mortality is
> not the taking away of life but the making sacred of the life that is
> given. You were right, Andreth. And I thank you for teaching me.

**Defeat:**

> *Wise-woman.* You spoke of death as a gift, but this death is not a gift.
> It is waste. The wolf tears me, the oath lies broken, the darkness wins. I
> die not in freedom but in failure. Mortality may be a gift for Men. For me,
> it is only ending.

---

### 5. Beren

**Victory:**

> *Son of Barahir.* I die for you, and I do not ask for thanks. But I see in
> your eyes as you hold my hand that you understand the weight of what has
> been given. You did not ask for this. But your father did not ask either,
> when he saved me. This is the nature of love: it gives without demand, and
> it trusts the gift will not be wasted. Live, Beren. Go to Lúthien. Win the
> Silmaril. Let my death be the bridge that carries you to the life you were
> meant to have. And when you are old, and the quest is long past, remember:
> a king of the Noldor thought you were worth dying for.

**Defeat:**

> *Son of Barahir.* I am sorry. I swore to aid you, and I have brought you
> only to darkness. The wolf comes, and I kill it, but without song, without
> light, without the defiance that should have made your rescue a thing of
> glory. You will live. The quest will succeed. But when you think of me in
> the years to come, you will remember not a king who died singing but a
> friend who died in silence. I deserved to give you more. Forgive me.

---

### 6. Lúthien

**Victory:**

> *Nightingale.* You will come to this tower when I am dead. You will sing,
> and Sauron will yield, and Beren will be freed. And together you will do
> what I could not: reach Angband, win the Silmaril, defy Morgoth himself.
> My death makes your victory possible. Without me, Beren would never have
> reached the edge of hope. Without my sacrifice, your song would have no
> foundation to build upon. I am the road you walk. I am glad to be that
> road. Sing beautifully, Lúthien. Sing for all of us who fell in the dark.

**Defeat:**

> *Nightingale.* You will come, and you will find Beren alive—but broken.
> The light I should have kindled in him is dim. The sacrifice I made was
> real, but graceless, and the road from this pit to Angband will be harder
> for the shadow I left on his spirit. Your song will still break the tower.
> Your love will still carry him forward. But the foundation I laid is
> cracked, and the weight of my failure will haunt the quest like a wound
> that does not heal.

---

### 7. Huan

**Victory:**

> *Hound of Valinor.* You will come with Lúthien, and you will fight the
> wolves, and you will stand against Sauron himself. Your strength is the
> strength of the Blessed Realm, and even the Cruel One cannot stand before
> you. I wish I could see that battle. I wish I could see Sauron's face when
> he realizes that the tower he holds is not his, but borrowed, and that the
> true powers of the West have not forgotten this place. Bite deep, Huan.
> Avenge us. And guard Lúthien well.

**Defeat:**

> *Hound of Valinor.* When you come to this place, you will find Beren alive
> but diminished, and Finrod dead without glory. The sacrifice was made, but
> it carried no song, no defiance, no light for you to build upon. You will
> break Sauron's power regardless—your jaws and Lúthien's voice need no
> foundation from me. But the tower you topple will feel hollow, and the
> victory will taste of ash.

---

### 8. Finarfin

**Victory:**

> *Father.* You chose mercy over pride. At Araman, when the Doom of Mandos
> was spoken, you turned back and led a remnant home to beg the Valar's
> pardon. Your brothers marched into exile and doom. You knelt and were
> forgiven. I marched into Middle-earth with pride in my heart, but I have
> learned your lesson at last: true kingship is not in conquest but in
> sacrifice. I die keeping an oath of love, not an oath of wrath. I think
> you would be proud of me, Father. I think you would say: *This is what it
> means to be of the house of Finarfin.*

**Defeat:**

> *Father.* You turned back, and you were wise. I marched forward, and I have
> found only death. Pride drove me here as much as honor. And now I die in
> the dark, far from the light of Valinor, and I will not see you again until
> Mandos releases me—if he releases me. Forgive me for choosing exile over
> wisdom.

---

### 9. Galadriel

**Victory:**

> *Sister.* You see everything, and you will see this: that I died well. Not
> easily. Not without fear. But *well*. I kept faith with a friend. I led my
> people with honor. I sang light into darkness. And when the last choice
> came—when the wolf came for Beren—I did not hesitate. You always said I was
> too gentle for this world. Perhaps you were right. But gentleness is not
> weakness. It is the choice to love even when love costs everything. Remember
> me, Galadriel. And when you rule in Middle-earth, as I know you will, let
> my death be a reminder: power is worthless if it is not used in service of
> love.

**Defeat:**

> *Sister.* You will hear of my death and shake your head, unsurprised. *Too
> gentle,* you will say. *Too trusting. He went to his doom for a mortal he
> barely knew.* And you will be right. I was a fool. But even in failure, even
> in death, I cannot regret the love that drove me here. Better a fool who
> loves than a wise one who does not.

---

## VII. Final Scene — The Sacrifice's Measure

**This scene ALWAYS plays after the 6th run.**

It consists of three parts: the Death, the Aftermath, and the Legacy.

---

### Part 1: The Death (Always Plays)

> The wolf lies dead at your feet. Its body is dissolving into shadow,
> returning to the formless malice from which Sauron shaped it.
>
> You lie beside it, breathing in short, ragged gasps. The wounds are mortal.
> You have known this from the moment the fangs tore your throat.
>
> Beren kneels over you, his face a mask of grief. "Finrod. Finrod, no. You
> cannot—"
>
> You reach up and touch his cheek. Your hand leaves a smear of blood. "Beren.
> Listen."
>
> He leans closer.
>
> "Lúthien will come. She loves you. She will break this tower. She will
> free you. Go to her. Go to Doriath. Do not give up the quest."
>
> "The quest?" Beren's voice breaks. "You are dying for a quest that was
> never yours—"
>
> "It was mine the moment I swore the oath to your father. And I do not
> regret it." You smile, though the pain is terrible. "I am Finrod Felagund,
> son of Finarfin, friend of Men. This is a good death."
>
> Beren weeps. "I will remember you. I will tell Lúthien. I will tell the
> world what you did."
>
> You nod. Your vision is fading. The darkness is closing in—not Sauron's
> darkness, but the other kind, the darkness that comes for all who fall in
> battle. You are not afraid. You have done what you came to do.
>
> Your last thought is of Amarië, waiting in Valinor beneath the golden trees.
>
> Your last breath carries a song—wordless, soft, but bright.
>
> And then you are gone.

---

## VIII. Implementation Notes (For Future Coding)

### Campaign Structure
- **Run count:** 6 (fixed, representing stages of the quest and imprisonment)
- **End trigger:** After 6th run completion, fire Final Scene
- **Scoring:** UNIQUE MECHANIC—Silmarils/victories ADD to score (+1 each), but
  defeats/deaths SUBTRACT from score (-1 each). This creates a balance that can
  go positive (high score = bright sacrifice) or negative (low score = dark
  sacrifice).
- **Score threshold:** Suggested: Total score ≥ +2 for Bright Sacrifice ending;
  Total score < +2 for Shadowed Sacrifice ending. Tune based on playtesting.
- **Hero pool:** Restricted to 9 specific characters (3 new + 6 existing)
- **Hero reuse:** Allow same hero in multiple runs

### Unique Scoring Mechanic
- **Victory with Silmaril:** +1 to balance (light prevails in this stage)
- **Victory without Silmaril:** +0 (neutral)
- **Defeat/death:** -1 to balance (shadow prevails in this stage)
- **Final tally determines ending tone, not outcome** — Finrod always dies,
  Beren always lives, quest always succeeds. Only the *meaning* changes.

### Story System Integration
- Memory Chapters (Type 0: Light) trigger at run start, indexed by run (1-6)
- Defeat Chapters (Type 1: Shadow) trigger on hero death, indexed by run (1-6)
- Hero Victory/Defeat messages trigger on run completion, indexed by hero ID
- Final Scene triggers after Run 6, with three parts:
  1. The Death (always plays)
  2. The Aftermath (score-conditional: Bright or Shadowed)
  3. The Ring (always plays)
- Opening Scene triggers once at campaign creation

### Unique to Finrod Scenario

- **First scenario where the protagonist always dies** — The death is not a
  failure state but a narrative fixed point. What varies is the *quality* of
  the sacrifice.
- **Inverted scoring** — Defeats subtract from score, creating a balance that
  can go negative. This reflects the struggle between light and shadow.
- **Dual endings based on tone, not outcome** — Both endings have the same
  events (Finrod dies, Beren lives, quest succeeds), but one is luminous and
  one is shadowed.
- **Low average power (~1.6)** — With existing Barahir (P:0) and Andreth (P:0)
  in the pool, the hero selection has genuine strategic weight. Picking weaker
  heroes makes the +/- scoring harder, reinforcing the theme that Finrod's
  companions were ordinary people in extraordinary circumstances.
- **Focus on a single, pivotal event** — Unlike Fëanor (whole life) or Maglor
  (ages of wandering), this scenario compresses into one concentrated moment:
  the duel, the imprisonment, the sacrifice.

- Andreth and her love for Aegnor (Finrod's brother) is per *Athrabeth Finrod
  ah Andreth* (HoME X)

### Creative Liberties (Tolkien-Compatible)

- The specific details of what Finrod sang in the duel are expanded from the
  brief description in the Silmarillion, but consistent with the themes
  Tolkien describes (light vs. treachery)
- The other nine companions (besides Edrahil) are deliberately left unnamed in
  the narrative, matching Tolkien's silence. Earlier drafts named them, but
  this was corrected to avoid lore conflicts (e.g., "Gorlim" was the
  traitor who betrayed Barahir's outlaws, not a faithful companion)
- The emotional texture of Finrod's internal monologue is original but
  grounded in Tolkien's characterization of Finrod as noble, loyal, and
  devoted to friendship with Men
- The dialogue between Finrod and his companions in the pit is invented but
  thematically consistent
- Finrod's final words to Beren are expanded from the Silmarillion's summary
- The reunion with Amarië in Valinor is described in emotional detail,
  consistent with the references to it in HoME but elaborated
- The "scoring mechanic" as a metaphor for the balance between light and
  shadow in the sacrifice is a game-design interpretation of Tolkien's themes
- The two endings (Bright vs. Shadowed) reflect the same events but with
  different emotional weight, consistent with how Tolkien sometimes presents
  the same story from different perspectives (e.g., the different accounts of
  events in Unfinished Tales)
- Sauron's moment of apparent regret upon hearing Amarië's melody (Run 5) is
  a creative liberty, but grounded in Tolkien's characterization of Sauron as
  a fallen Maia who once served Aulë and knew the beauty of Valinor before his
  corruption

### Implementation Notes on Character Overlap

- **Barahir** (N:24) and **Andreth** (N:44) already exist in `character.txt`
  and are used as-is. Their power levels (P:0 each) are lower than originally
  proposed, making the scenario harder. This is thematically appropriate:
  Barahir is a mortal lord, brave but without Elven advantages; Andreth is a
  wise-woman, not a warrior. Their weakness underscores the sacrifice.
- **Edrahil** appears as a NEW hero in both this scenario and the Lúthien
  scenario. During implementation, a single character entry should be created
  and shared, with stats adjusted if scenarios require different power levels.
- **Huan** does not yet exist in `character.txt` and must be created. He also
  appears in the Lúthien scenario. A single entry should serve both.
- **Orodreth** (Finrod's nephew or brother, depending on version) held Minas
  Tirith as its warden when Sauron captured it. He was considered for the hero
  pool but excluded to keep focus on the personal sacrifice and the oath to
  Barahir. He could be added if the pool needs expansion.
