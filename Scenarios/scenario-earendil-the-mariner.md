# The Mariner's Hope: Eärendil's Vision

## Scenario Design Document

---

## I. Lore Foundation

### Eärendil the Mariner (Silmarillion, Chapters 23-24)

Eärendil the Blessed, son of Tuor son of Huor and Idril Celebrindal daughter of
Turgon, was born in Gondolin before its fall. He was the first of the
Half-elven, bearing in his veins the blood of all three kindreds of the Eldar
and the three Houses of the Edain. He grew to manhood in the Havens of Sirion,
dwelling among the survivors of the great kingdoms — Gondolin, Nargothrond,
Doriath — all brought to ruin.

In Sirion he wed Elwing the White, daughter of Dior, who bore upon her breast
the Silmaril that Beren had won from Morgoth's crown. Together they had twin
sons: Elrond and Elros.

Eärendil was a mariner. His heart yearned for the Sea, and with the aid of
Círdan the Shipwright he built Vingilot, fairest of all the ships of song. He
sought the Straight Road to Valinor, attempting again and again to find the way
west, driven by a purpose he did not fully understand — a sense that the Valar
must be reached, that Middle-earth's darkness could not be overcome by mortal
strength alone.

When the sons of Fëanor attacked Sirion seeking the Silmaril, Elwing in despair
cast herself into the sea. Ulmo transformed her into a white bird, and she flew
across the waves bearing the jewel to Eärendil upon his ship. Together, guided
by the light of the Silmaril, they found at last the Straight Road and came to
Valinor.

Eärendil stood before the Valar as advocate for both Elves and Men — the Two
Kindreds whom Morgoth had sundered and afflicted. He asked not for himself but
for mercy upon the peoples of Middle-earth, and his plea was heard. The Valar
prepared for war.

But Eärendil was forbidden to return to mortal lands. Instead, Manwë hallowed
Vingilot and set it in the heavens. Eärendil, bearing the Silmaril upon his
brow, sails the airs of the firmament as the Star of High Hope — the Evening
Star and the Morning Star, Gil-Estel, the light that endures when all other
lights have failed.

### Thematic Analysis

Where other scenarios tell stories of *remembering* or *choosing*, the Eärendil
scenario tells a story of **seeking forward** — a hero compelled by visions he
does not yet understand, gathering light to bring hope to a darkened world.

This is the theme of *prophetic purpose*. Eärendil does not know why he builds
his ship. He does not know why he sails west. He knows only that he *must*. The
dreams sent by Ulmo (guardian of the Edain and of exiled Elves) show him
fragments: lights scattered in darkness, a jewel burning like a star, voices
crying for aid across an impossible distance.

The scenario begins before Vingilot is built. Eärendil dwells in Sirion, among
refugees haunted by loss. He has the Silmaril through Elwing, but he does not
yet understand its significance. The dreams come night after night, compelling
him into the darkness of Angband — not literally, but in vision, in spirit, in
the questing of his soul.

Each descent is a prophetic journey. Each Silmaril recovered is not a literal
jewel but a *proof of light* — evidence that hope endures in the deepest
darkness, that the scattered remnants of beauty can be gathered, that a messenger
bearing these proofs before the Valar might move them to action.

Three Silmarils. Three lights. Three proofs:
1. **The Light of the Past** — The glory of Gondolin, Nargothrond, Doriath, now
   fallen but not forgotten
2. **The Light of the Present** — The Silmaril Eärendil bears, won by Beren and
   Lúthien, proof that even Morgoth can be defied
3. **The Light of the Future** — The hope he will carry to Valinor, the star he
   will become, the salvation of the Two Kindreds

When the three lights are gathered in vision, Eärendil awakens with clarity of
purpose. He builds Vingilot. He sails west. The star is kindled.

In gameplay terms, this creates a **long Silmaril-gated campaign** with
a diverse hero pool representing the refugees of Sirion — survivors from every
fallen realm, bearing the mixed blood of Elves and Men, united in desperate hope.

### Key Lore Touchstones Referenced

- **Eärendil's parentage** — Son of Tuor (Man) and Idril (Elf), first of the
  Half-elven. (Silmarillion, "Of Tuor and the Fall of Gondolin")
- **The Fall of Gondolin** — Eärendil was seven years old when Morgoth destroyed
  the last great Elven-realm. He escaped with his parents through the Secret Way.
  (Silmarillion, "Of Tuor and the Fall of Gondolin"; Unfinished Tales)
- **The Havens of Sirion** — Founded by survivors of Gondolin, Nargothrond, and
  Doriath. A place of refuge and mingled hope. (Silmarillion, "Of the Voyage of
  Eärendil")
- **Círdan the Shipwright** — Master mariner, builder of the great ships, teacher
  of Eärendil's craft. (Silmarillion, throughout)
- **Gil-galad the High King** — Sent by Círdan to dwell in Sirion under Eärendil's
  protection after Nargothrond fell. Last High King of the Noldor in Middle-earth.
  (Silmarillion, "Of the Fifth Battle")
- **Elwing the White** — Daughter of Dior, bearer of the Silmaril, wife of
  Eärendil. (Silmarillion, "Of the Voyage of Eärendil")
- **Ulmo's guidance** — The Vala who alone did not forsake the exiles, who sent
  warnings and visions to many heroes. He sent Tuor to Gondolin; he must have
  guided Eärendil's voyage. (Silmarillion, throughout; explicitly stated regarding
  Tuor)
- **The Silmaril of Beren and Lúthien** — Won from Morgoth's crown, passed to
  Dior, then to Elwing, then borne by Eärendil across the sea. (Silmarillion,
  "Of Beren and Lúthien"; "Of the Voyage of Eärendil")
- **The Third Kinslaying at Sirion** — The sons of Fëanor attacked the Havens.
  Elwing leaped into the sea. Elrond and Elros were orphaned. (Silmarillion,
  "Of the Voyage of Eärendil")
- **Vingilot (Vingilótë)** — "Foam-flower," the ship Eärendil built with Círdan's
  aid. Later hallowed by Manwë and set among the stars. (Silmarillion, "Of the
  Voyage of Eärendil")
- **Eärendil's plea before the Valar** — He came not for himself but as
  representative of both kindreds, and his humility and sacrifice moved the Valar
  to pity. (Silmarillion, "Of the Voyage of Eärendil")
- **The hallowing of Vingilot** — Manwë hallowed the ship and set Eärendil as a
  star in the sky, bearing the Silmaril. (Silmarillion, "Of the Voyage of
  Eärendil")
- **Gil-Estel, the Star of High Hope** — The name the Elves gave to Eärendil's
  star. It rose before Morgoth's final defeat and heralded the salvation of
  Middle-earth. (Silmarillion, "Of the Voyage of Eärendil")

## II. Scenario Overview

**Name:** The Mariner's Hope
**Subtitle:** Eärendil's Vision
**Type:** Long Campaign (Silmaril-gated, unlimited runs)
**Victory Condition:** Recover 15 Silmarils from Angband
**Defeat Condition:** None. The quest continues until the lights are gathered.
**Hero Pool:** 10 heroes (2 new + 8 existing) — refugees of Sirion
**Difficulty:** Medium-Hard (war-weary survivors, representing mixed refugees)

### Narrative Frame

The player is Eärendil, dwelling in the Havens of Sirion in the years before he
builds Vingilot. By day he walks among the refugees — survivors of fallen
kingdoms, Elves and Men mingled, bearing the weight of loss and the stubborn
refusal to surrender to despair. By night he dreams.

The dreams are sent by Ulmo, though Eärendil does not know this yet. In the
dreams he descends into Angband — not in body but in vision, in spirit, in the
questing of his prophetic soul. He sees lights scattered in darkness. He sees a
jewel that burns like a star. He hears voices crying for aid.

He does not understand the dreams. But he cannot ignore them.

Each run into Angband is a vision-journey. The heroes Eärendil sends (or becomes
in the dream-logic) are the people of Sirion: his parents Tuor and Idril, the
High King Gil-galad, his beloved Elwing, the ancient shipwright Círdan, the
scattered lords and mariners and common folk who have lost everything but hope.

The Silmarils he recovers are proofs — not literal jewels but demonstrations
that light endures in darkness, that the enemy can be defied, that hope is not
foolishness. Fifteen proofs. Fifteen lights. Each recovered Silmaril represents
another aspect of why Middle-earth is worth saving, another reason the Valar
must intervene. When the fifteenth is brought back from the abyss, Eärendil
awakens with perfect clarity:

*I must build the ship. I must sail west. I must carry these proofs to the Valar.*

And so he does.

### Progression Structure

The campaign is Silmaril-gated, like the Glorfindel and Lúthien scenarios,
requiring 15 Silmarils (same as Glorfindel). This creates a long campaign that
reflects Eärendil's many years of seeking, the multiple voyages he attempted
before finding the Straight Road, and the gradual accumulation of proofs he will
carry to Valinor.

### Hero Pool Philosophy

The heroes are refugees. Survivors. People who have lost their homes, their
kingdoms, their kin — but not their will to endure. They represent all three
kindreds of the Eldar, the three Houses of the Edain, and the mingling of Elf
and Man that defines Sirion and Eärendil himself.

Power levels are moderate-low (average ~1.5), reflecting skilled but war-weary
heroes. Only Glorfindel stands at Power 3; most are Power 1-2. These
are people who have survived, not people who have conquered.

### Design Rationale

The hero pool represents the mingled peoples of Sirion — Gondolindrim (Tuor,
Idril, Galdor, Glorfindel), Falathrim (Círdan), Sindar (Celeborn), Noldor
(Galadriel, Gil-galad), and the unique figure of Elwing who carries all three
lineages of Dior (Maia, Sindar, and Edain blood).

Every hero embodies a facet of Eärendil's world and calling:

- **Tuor** — The mortal who heard Ulmo's call and heeded it. Eärendil's heritage
  as Half-elven begins here. Tuor represents the courage to listen to visions
  even when they make no sense.

- **Idril** — The foresight that sees disaster coming and prepares anyway. She
  built the Secret Way when no one believed Gondolin would fall. Eärendil inherits
  her perceptiveness and her refusal to despair.

- **Gil-galad** — The future. The light that will endure when Eärendil becomes a
  star. He represents the kingship Eärendil could have claimed but gave up to
  sail west.

- **Elwing** — The beloved. The bearer of the Silmaril. The one who will leap
  into the abyss for love and be transformed. She is central to Eärendil's
  destiny — her leap brings the Silmaril to his ship; her presence makes the
  voyage possible.

- **Círdan** — The shipwright. The ancient mariner who has seen all the ages of
  Middle-earth and endured. He teaches Eärendil the craft that makes Vingilot
  possible. He is patience and skill and the long view.

- **Galadriel** — The survivor. She endured the Darkening, the Flight, the
  Kinslaying, the curse of Mandos, the fall of every Noldorin kingdom. She is
  the one who will remain when all others have departed. She represents the
  unyielding will that Sirion embodies.

- **Celeborn** — The Sindarin prince who married across kindreds. He represents
  the blending of peoples that defines Sirion and Eärendil's very existence.

- **Galdor** — A lord of Gondolin, a warrior of the rearguard, one of the fallen.
  He represents the cost of survival — for every refugee in Sirion, a dozen fell
  to bring them there.

- **Glorfindel** — The hero who died destroying a Balrog in the fall of Gondolin
  (this is pre-rebirth). He is martyrdom and the proof that even in defeat,
  valor endures.

- **Voronwë** — The faithful guide who led Tuor through the wilderness to
  Gondolin. A mariner who survived shipwreck, a companion who kept his word. He
  represents the ordinary faithfulness that makes great quests possible.

## V. Narrative Script

### Opening Scene (Plays Once, at Campaign Start)

> **Title: The First Dream**
>
> You wake in the dark.
>
> The sound of the Sea fills your ears — not the gentle lapping at Sirion's
> wharves but the vast, cold voice of the Sundering Sea, the waters that divide
> Middle-earth from the Blessed Realm, the boundary no mortal has crossed since
> the world was young.
>
> You are standing on the shore, though you do not remember walking there. The
> stars above are wrong — brighter, closer, arranged in patterns you do not
> recognize. Before you stretches the water, black and endless. Behind you...
>
> You turn.
>
> The mountains of Angband rise like a wound in the world. Thangorodrim, the
> Towers of Tyranny, vast beyond measure, crowned with smoke and fire. You have
> never seen them — you were born in Gondolin, far to the south — yet you *know*
> them. You know the darkness that dwells there. You know the Enemy who sits
> enthroned in the deeps beneath those peaks.
>
> And you know — with the strange certainty of dreams — that you must go there.
>
> Not in body. You stand safe in the Havens of Sirion, sleeping beside Elwing in
> the house Círdan built for you. But in vision, in spirit, in the questing of
> your soul, you must descend. You must enter the darkness. You must find...
>
> What?
>
> A light. Lights. Scattered in the deeps. Jewels that burn like stars. Voices
> crying for aid across an impossible distance.
>
> You do not understand.
>
> But the Sea is calling. And beneath the Sea's voice — deeper, older, vast as
> the foundations of the world — another voice:
>
> *"The darkness is great, son of Tuor. But light endures, if those with eyes to
> see will gather it. Seek. Find. Bring forth the proof. The Valar must be shown
> that Middle-earth is worth the saving."*
>
> Ulmo. The Lord of Waters. The Vala who sent your father to Gondolin. The Vala
> who alone did not forsake the exiled Eldar.
>
> The vision shifts. You are no longer on the shore but in the depths of Angband
> itself, and you are not alone. Faces surround you — your father Tuor, your
> mother Idril, the High King Gil-galad, the shipwright Círdan, your beloved
> Elwing. They walk into the dark.
>
> They are seeking the lights.
>
> And so must you.

#### Vision 1 — The Light of Remembrance (1 Silmaril)

> You wake with a jewel in your hand.
>
> Not truly. Your hand is empty, clutching only the woolen blanket of your bed
> in Sirion. But the dream-memory remains: the weight of the Silmaril, the light
> that burned cold and pure, brighter than any star.
>
> You rise and walk to the window. Dawn is breaking over the Havens. Below, the
> refugees are waking — Gondolindrim with the star of the House of Fingolfin on
> their shields, Nargothrondians bearing the broken badge of Finrod, survivors
> of Doriath with their star-mantles tattered and ash-stained.
>
> All the great kingdoms have fallen. Gondolin. Nargothrond. Doriath. The Falas.
> Hithlum. Dor-lómin. They are ash and memory.
>
> But the people endure. Here, in Sirion. Broken but not destroyed. Scattered
> but not dissolved. And in your dream-hand, the light you carried from the
> darkness was their light — the glory of what they were, the proof that they
> lived, the memory that they mattered.
>
> *The Light of Remembrance.*
>
> You understand, dimly, that this is why you must descend. Not to recover the
> past — the past is gone. But to prove that it *was*. To bring evidence of
> beauty and valor from the darkness, to carry it west, to show the Valar:
>
> *Look. See what was made in Middle-earth. See what Morgoth destroyed. See what
> is worth fighting for.*
>
> One light recovered. Two remain.

---

#### Vision 2 — The Light of Defiance (2 Silmarils)

> The second dream is different.
>
> You are not seeking a light. You are *remembering* a light — one that already
> exists, already shines, already burns in defiance of all Morgoth's malice.
>
> Elwing stands beside you, and upon her breast gleams the Silmaril of Beren and
> Lúthien.
>
> You know the tale. Everyone in Middle-earth knows the tale. A mortal man and
> an Elf-maiden entered Morgoth's fortress, stole a Silmaril from his very crown,
> and lived to tell of it. Beren lost his hand. Lúthien lost her immortality.
> But they *won*. They defied the Enemy and survived.
>
> In the dream, you reach out and touch the jewel. It is warm. Not with heat but
> with *presence* — the light of the Two Trees, the making of Fëanor, the
> blessing of Varda, the blood Beren shed, the song Lúthien sang.
>
> And you understand:
>
> This is the second proof. Not just a memory of glory but a demonstration of
> *resistance*. Morgoth is not invincible. The darkness can be entered and
> returned from. Even the mightiest Enemy can be defied by the smallest heroes,
> if their courage is great enough.
>
> You wake, and Elwing stirs beside you. The Silmaril gleams faintly in the
> pre-dawn darkness. She does not know you have been dreaming of it. She does
> not know what it means.
>
> But you are beginning to understand.
>
> *The Light of Defiance.*
>
> Two lights. One remains.

### Shadow Chapters (Type 1: Hero Deaths)

These chapters trigger as the cumulative death count rises. They represent the

every survivor, many fell.

#### Death 1 — The Weight

> You wake from the dream-vision of Angband, and the first thought is:
>
> *They died.*
>
> Not in truth. Not in the waking world. But in the vision-descent, in the
> prophetic seeking, the hero you sent into darkness did not return. The light
> was too far. The darkness too deep.
>
> It is only a dream. Only a vision.
>
> But the grief is real.
>
> You walk among the Havens in the early morning, and everywhere you see the
> faces of the lost. The empty seats in the hall where Gondolin's lords once
> sat. The silent forges where Nargothrond's smiths once worked. The spaces
> between people where brothers and sisters and children once stood.
>
> Sirion is a city of survivors. And to survive is to carry the weight of those
> who did not.
>
> The vision will come again tonight. You will descend again. You do not have
> the luxury of stopping.
>
> But the weight grows heavier.

---

#### Death 3 — The Cost of Light

> Three dead in the seeking. Three heroes who entered the dark and did not
> return.
>
> You begin to question. Is this right? Is this worth it? You are chasing
> dream-lights, vision-jewels, proofs that may not even be real. And with each
> descent, you feel the cost — not in your own flesh but in the grief that clings
> to the visions, the sense of *spending* something precious.
>
> Elwing finds you on the cliffs above the harbor, staring west toward the empty
> horizon.
>
> *"What are you searching for?"* she asks.
>
> You do not know how to answer. How can you explain the dreams? The lights in
> the darkness? The voice of Ulmo calling you to build a ship and sail into the
> void?
>
> *"Hope,"* you finally say. *"I am searching for hope."*
>
> She takes your hand. The Silmaril on her breast gleams faintly in the twilight.
>
> *"Then keep searching,"* she says. *"Hope is always worth the cost."*

---

#### Death 5 — The Shadow of Doubt

> Five lost. More than fell in some battles. You are spending lives — dream-lives,
> vision-lives, but still *lives* — on a quest whose purpose you do not fully
> understand.
>
> What if the dreams are false? What if Ulmo is silent, and these are only your
> own fears taking shape? What if the ship you plan to build sails nowhere, and
> the Valar do not listen, and Middle-earth falls into darkness despite all your
> seeking?
>
> You almost stop. Almost turn away from the visions.
>
> But then you remember: *You are the son of Tuor, who heard Ulmo's voice and
> walked to Gondolin though the road was hidden. You are the son of Idril, who
> built the Secret Way though no one believed her. You are the husband of Elwing,
> who bears a Silmaril won from Morgoth's crown by a mortal man and an
> Elf-maiden who refused to accept impossibility.*
>
> Hope is your inheritance. Seeking is your calling.
>
> The visions will continue. And so will you.

---

#### Death 7+ — The Unyielding

> The deaths mount. The weight grows. But you do not stop.
>
> Because Sirion does not stop. The refugees do not lie down and surrender. They
> plant gardens. They forge swords. They sing songs. They marry and bear
> children and *hope*, despite everything Morgoth has done to break them.
>
> You are their representative. You will carry their hope west. You will show the
> Valar what endures in Middle-earth.
>
> And if the cost is high, then so be it. The light is worth it.
>
> The light is always worth it.

## VI. Hero Victory and Defeat Messages

Each message is written from Eärendil's perspective as he experiences the
vision-descent.

---

### 1. Tuor

**Victory:**

> *Father.* In the light of the recovered Silmaril, I see you clearly: standing
> in Ulmo's rain-cloak at the Gate of the Noldor, bearing the Vala's message to
> a king who would not listen. You walked through darkness and wilderness because
> a voice in the waters told you to go, and you did not question. You defied
> Morgoth's servants, married an Elf-princess, fathered a child of two kindreds.
> You heard the call of the Sea and heeded it, even when it made no sense. I am
> your son. I inherit your gift: the ability to hear what others do not, to
> trust the voice that calls from the deeps, to sail into the unknown because
> the known is not enough. This light is proof of your faithfulness, Father. I
> will carry it west.

**Defeat:**

> *Father.* The darkness takes you, as it took Gondolin, as it has taken so much.
> But you taught me: darkness is not the end. You walked through shadow to find
> the Hidden City. You led survivors through fire to find safety. You sailed
> west into the void because the Sea called. I will do the same. This vision
> failed, but the calling endures. I will descend again.

---

### 2. Idril

**Victory:**

> *Mother.* Celebrindal, Silverfoot, who walked barefoot on snow. I see you now
> with the Silmaril's light illuminating your face: the foresight in your eyes,
> the tunnel you dug in secret, the escape route no one believed would be needed
> until the night it saved us all. You saw disaster coming and prepared anyway.
> You did not despair; you *acted*. I am your son. I inherit your gift: the
> sight that pierces shadow, the refusal to accept fate, the quiet preparation
> that makes survival possible. This light is proof that foresight matters, that
> preparation is not cowardice, that hope requires *work*. I will build the ship.
> I will sail west. Because you taught me: the future is not fixed.

**Defeat:**

> *Mother.* The vision fails, and I see you in the fall of Gondolin — fleeing
> through the Secret Way, carrying me as a child, the city burning behind us.
> You saved so many that night. But you could not save everyone. This descent
> was the same: the light was there, but the darkness was too deep. I will try
> again. Foresight is not certainty. But it is still worth the seeing.

---

### 3. Gil-galad

**Victory:**

> *High King.* Ereinion, Star of Radiance, last scion of Finwë's line. In the
> Silmaril's light I see you as you will be: bearing the spear Aeglos, wearing
> the crown of the Noldor, standing against the returning Shadow in ages yet to
> come. You dwell in Sirion now under my protection, a young king whose realm
> is refugees and memory. But you will endure. When I sail west and become a
> star, you will remain — the light of the Eldar in Middle-earth, the kingship
> that bridges the Elder Days and the ages that follow. This recovered light is
> proof that the future is not empty. You are the future, Gil-galad. And you are
> worth the fighting for.

**Defeat:**

> *High King.* You fall in the vision as you will fall in the ages to come —
> fighting the Enemy, unyielding unto death. But falling is not failing. You
> will cast Sauron down even as you fall, and your name will be remembered as
> long as the Eldar sing. This vision ended in darkness, but your light endures.
> I will seek again.

---

### 4. Elwing

**Victory:**

> *Beloved.* Elwing the White, daughter of Dior, bearer of the Silmaril, my
> wife, my star. In this recovered light I see our future: the night the sons of
> Fëanor will come with swords to claim the jewel you bear. The moment you will
> stand on the cliff above the burning Havens, holding our children behind you,
> and leap into the void. The transformation — Ulmo's mercy — that will turn you
> into a white bird, bearing the Silmaril across the Sea to find me. The moment
> we will stand together on Vingilot's deck with the jewel blazing between us,
> guiding us west. You are the reason this quest is possible. You are the light
> I seek. And I will find you, Elwing. Across the Sea, across the darkness,
> across the very boundaries of the world. I will find you.

**Defeat:**

> *Beloved.* You fall in the dark, and I wake in terror, reaching for you. You
> are still there, sleeping beside me, safe in Sirion for now. But the vision
> was a warning: the darkness is coming. The Fëanorians will come. You will leap.
> I must be ready. I must have the ship built. I must be on the water when you
> fly across the waves. The vision failed, but the purpose is clearer. I will
> descend again. I will find the lights. I will be ready when you need me.

---

### 5. Círdan

**Victory:**

> *Shipwright.* Ancient mariner, grey and patient, who has sailed every water and
> seen every shore. In the Silmaril's light I see your hands shaping the timbers
> of Vingilot — not yet, but soon. You do not ask why I want to sail west. You
> do not question the visions. You simply *build*, because that is what you do:
> you build ships for those who must sail into the unknown. You built ships for
> the Teleri in the First Age of starlight. You will build ships for the
> Númenóreans in ages to come. And now you will build *my* ship, the ship that
> carries hope to the Valar. This light is proof that the work is worth doing.
> Thank you, Círdan. Your craft will save the world.

**Defeat:**

> *Shipwright.* You fall in the vision, but I know you cannot truly fall — you
> are deathless, patient, enduring beyond all ages. You will outlast kingdoms
> and kings. You will stand at the Grey Havens when the last ship sails west.
> You are the constant against which all change is measured. This vision ended
> in failure, but your work continues. Tomorrow we will return to the harbor. We
> will shape more timbers. We will build.

---

### 6. Galadriel

**Victory:**

> *Kinswoman.* Galadriel, Artanis, Nerwen, called by many names, survivor of
> every fall, bearer of Nenya in ages to come. In the Silmaril's light I see
> the long chain of your endurance: Valinor before the Darkening, the rebellion
> you joined not for the Oath but for pride and the desire to rule, the
> Kinslaying you witnessed, the curse you bore, the kingdoms you saw fall one by
> one — Nargothrond, Doriath, Gondolin. And yet you endure. You will endure when
> I am a star, when Gil-galad is fallen, when the Elder Days are legend. This
> light is proof that endurance is not passivity. You have survived by refusing
> to yield, and that refusal is itself a victory. I carry your strength with me.

**Defeat:**

> *Kinswoman.* Even you can fall — in visions, if not in truth. The darkness
> takes the strongest, the wisest, the most enduring. But you will rise. You
> always rise. That is your gift. And I will descend again, carrying the lesson
> you embody: to fall is not to fail. Only to stop trying is failure.

---

### 7. Celeborn

**Victory:**

> *Prince of Doriath.* Tall and silver, the steady hand, husband of Galadriel,
> survivor of Menegroth's fall. In the Silmaril's light I see what you represent:
> the blending of kindreds, the Sindar and Noldor united, the breaking of old
> divisions in the face of shared loss. You married the Noldorin rebel-princess.
> She married the Sindarin prince. Together you embody Sirion itself — the place
> where all the sundered peoples come together, not in triumph but in mutual
> recognition: we are all refugees now. This light is proof that division can be
> overcome. We are stronger together. I, who am Half-elven, carry this truth
> west.

**Defeat:**

> *Prince of Doriath.* You fall in the vision, but your steadiness endures. You
> are the one who does not burn bright but burns *long*. The reliable. The
> patient. The one still standing when the flames have passed. This descent
> failed, but I will try again, and I will remember your lesson: steady progress,
> not heroic gestures, wins the long campaign.

---

### 8. Galdor

**Victory:**

> *Lord of the Tree.* I remember you from Gondolin — tall, golden-haired, lord
> of the House of the Tree, one of the captains who held the rearguard when the
> city fell. You died so that others could flee. In the Silmaril's light I see
> your final stand: the gate collapsing, the Balrogs surging forward, and you
> stepping into the gap with your household, buying seconds that became minutes
> that became the margin by which my mother led us through the Secret Way. You
> are the cost of survival. For every refugee in Sirion, a hero like you fell.
> This light is recovered in your name, Galdor. You will be remembered.

**Defeat:**

> *Lord of the Tree.* You fall again, as you fell in Gondolin. Some heroes are
> defined by their falling — not failure, but *sacrifice*. The rearguard that
> holds the line so others can escape. You taught me: victory is not always
> measured in glory. Sometimes victory is simply that *someone* survived to tell
> the tale. I survived. I will tell it.

---

### 9. Glorfindel

**Victory:**

> *Golden-flower.* I was too young to remember you clearly, but my mother told
> the tale: Glorfindel of the House of the Golden Flower, who fought a Balrog on
> the narrow pass above Cirith Thoronath and cast it down into the abyss, and
> fell with it, and died so that the refugees could cross. Your body was buried
> with honor. Your name is sung with reverence. And in ages to come, the Valar
> will re-embody you and send you back to Middle-earth, because your sacrifice
> earned grace beyond measure. In the Silmaril's light I see the pattern: those
> who give everything for others are not forgotten. This light is proof of your
> valor, Glorfindel. And proof that death is not the end.

**Defeat:**

> *Golden-flower.* You fall again, as you fell on the pass. But Glorfindel — you
> will return. The Valar do not forget the noble dead. And I do not forget the
> lesson of your death: that the greatest victory is not survival but fidelity.
> You were faithful unto death. I will be faithful unto the West.

---

### 10. Voronwë

**Victory:**

> *Faithful guide.* I know you only from my father's tales: Voronwë the mariner,
> sole survivor of a shipwreck on Ulmo's errand, who guided Tuor through the
> wilderness to Gondolin. You kept your word. You led my father to the Hidden
> City, and though the city fell, the guidance mattered — because it brought
> Tuor to Idril, and their union brought *me*, and I will carry the light west.
> In the Silmaril's recovered glow I see the chain of faithfulness: Ulmo sent
> you. You guided Tuor. Tuor fathered me. I will complete the work. This light
> is proof that ordinary faithfulness shapes the fate of the world. Thank you,
> Voronwë. Your name will not be forgotten.

**Defeat:**

> *Faithful guide.* You fall in the dark, as you fell (perhaps) in Gondolin's
> ruin. My father never told me your final fate. But I know you were faithful to
> the end. And faithfulness is not measured in success. It is measured in the
> trying. I will try again.

## VII. Final Scene — The Star Is Kindled

**This scene plays when 15 Silmarils have been recovered.**

It consists of three parts: The Awakening, The Building, and The Voyage.

---

### Part 1: The Awakening (Always Plays)

> The third light blazes in your dream-hand, and you wake.
>
> Not slowly. Not groggily. You wake with perfect clarity, as though you have
> been asleep for years and only now opened your eyes to see the world as it
> truly is.
>
> Elwing stirs beside you. Outside, the Sea murmurs its eternal rhythm against
> the wharves. The stars are fading. Dawn is coming.
>
> You rise and walk to the window. Below, the Havens are stirring: fishermen
> preparing their boats, smiths stoking their forges, bakers kneading bread,
> children running through the streets. Ordinary people doing ordinary things.
> Refugees who have lost everything, rebuilding small lives from the ashes of
> great kingdoms.
>
> And you understand.
>
> The visions were not literal. You did not truly descend into Angband. The
> Silmarils you recovered were not physical jewels (save the one Elwing already
> bears). They were *proofs* — demonstrations that light endures in darkness,
> that hope is not a fantasy, that the Valar must be shown what is worth saving.
>
> You have gathered the proofs. Now you must carry them west.
>
> You must build the ship.

---

### Part 2: The Building

> The building of Vingilot takes years.
>
> Círdan works beside you, his ancient hands shaping timbers with a skill born
> of ages beyond count. Gil-galad brings you wood from the forests of Ossiriand.
> Your father Tuor, who once built ships in Nevrast, offers counsel. Your mother
> Idril weaves sails of a strange, light fabric that catches wind like no cloth
> of Middle-earth.
>
> And slowly, timber by timber, sail by sail, the ship takes form.
>
> She is unlike any vessel ever made. Not a warship. Not a merchant vessel. A
> ship built for one purpose: to sail the Straight Road, the path the Valar laid
> from Middle-earth to Valinor, hidden from all save those they call.
>
> On the day Vingilot is finished, you stand on the deck and feel the Sea
> calling — not with words but with a pull deeper than speech, the same call
> your father felt, the same call that has haunted your dreams for years.
>
> Elwing comes aboard. She says nothing. She does not need to. The Silmaril on
> her breast gleams in the twilight, and you know: she will sail with you. Not
> because you asked. Because she chooses it.
>
> Together you set sail from Sirion. Your sons Elrond and Elros stand on the
> shore, too young to understand. Círdan raises his hand in blessing. Gil-galad
> watches with the eyes of a king who knows he will never see you again.
>
> And Vingilot, Foam-flower, fairest of all ships, catches the wind and sails
> into the West.

---

### Part 3: The Voyage

> The journey is long. Longer than any voyage you have known. Days blur into
> weeks, weeks into months. The stars wheel overhead. The Sea stretches endless.
>
> And then — one dawn — the sky changes.
>
> The light is different. Brighter. Not the light of the Sun (which is the last
> fruit of Laurelin, golden and warm) but a mingled radiance: silver and gold
> together, the light of the Two Trees as they were before Morgoth and Ungoliant
> destroyed them.
>
> You have found it. The Blessed Realm. Valinor.
>
> Vingilot sails into the Bay of Eldamar, and the Teleri come forth in their
> white ships to meet you, marveling. No mortal has come to these shores since
> the world was young. No Half-elven has ever walked in Aman.
>
> You are brought before the Valar.
>
> They sit in the Ring of Doom upon Ezellohar, where the Two Trees once grew:
> Manwë the Elder King, Varda the Star-kindler, Ulmo the Lord of Waters, and all
> the other Powers of Arda. Their eyes are upon you, and you — Half-elven
> mariner, son of a mortal and an Elf-maid, bearing a Silmaril stolen from
> Morgoth's crown — stand before them and speak.
>
> Not for yourself. Never for yourself.
>
> For the peoples of Middle-earth. For the Elves who defied the Valar's counsel
> and suffered for it. For the Men who were born into a world already marred by
> Morgoth's evil. For the refugees of Gondolin and Nargothrond and Doriath and
> Sirion. For Tuor and Idril and Elwing and Gil-galad and Círdan and all the
> uncounted brave who endured when endurance seemed madness.
>
> You show them the lights you gathered in the visions: the proof of remembrance,
> the proof of defiance, the proof of hope. You show them the Silmaril on
> Elwing's breast. You show them your own hands, weathered and scarred from
> shipbuilding and sea-sailing and the endless, stubborn work of hope.
>
> And you ask — not demand, not plead, but *ask* with the humility of one who
> knows he has no right but asks anyway:
>
> *"Have mercy. Send aid. Do not let Middle-earth fall into darkness forever."*
>
> Silence.
>
> Then Manwë speaks:
>
> *"Eärendil, greatest of mariners, most faithful of messengers. For thy sake,
> and for the sake of the Two Kindreds thou dost represent, we will hear thy
> plea. The Valar will go to war. Morgoth will be cast down. Middle-earth will
> be saved."*
>
> Your knees buckle. Elwing catches you. The Silmaril blazes with light.
>
> But Manwë is not finished:
>
> *"Yet a doom is set upon thee. Thou hast come to the land forbidden to mortals.
> Thou canst not return. Neither thou, nor thy wife who came with thee, nor thy
> ship that bore thee, may set foot again upon mortal lands."*
>
> You expected this. You knew the price.
>
> *"What, then, is my fate?"*
>
> Manwë gestures upward, toward the sky where the first stars are kindling in
> the evening.
>
> *"Thy ship shall be hallowed and set among the airs of the firmament. Thou
> shalt bear the Silmaril upon thy brow and sail the skies as a sign to all
> peoples: the Star of High Hope, the herald of the dawn, the light that endures
> when all other lights have failed."*
>
> *"Thy sons will be given the choice of the Half-elven: to be counted among
> Elves or among Men. Thy wife will be with thee, transformed, sailing at thy
> side. And thy name will be remembered as long as the world endures: Eärendil
> the Blessed, the Mariner, the Bearer of Light."*
>
> You look at Elwing. She nods.
>
> You look at the Valar. You think of your sons, growing up without you. You
> think of Middle-earth, which you will never see again. You think of the Sea
> you loved, which you will sail forever but never touch.
>
> And you say:
>
> *"I accept."*

---

### Epilogue: The Star

> Vingilot is hallowed by the Valar and set in the heavens. Eärendil, bearing
> the Silmaril of Beren and Lúthien upon his brow, sails the airs above the
> world. By day he rests in the West. By night he rises, and the Silmaril blazes
> like a star.
>
> In Middle-earth, the peoples look up and see the new light. They call it
> Gil-Estel, the Star of High Hope. It rises before the Sun in the morning. It
> shines after the Sun sets in the evening. It does not waver. It does not fade.
>
> And those who suffer in darkness — Elves in hiding, Men in thrall, survivors
> clinging to the edge of despair — see the star and know:
>
> *He reached them. He brought the message. The Valar are coming.*
>
> *Hope endures.*
>
> The War of Wrath begins. The Host of Valinor marches. Morgoth is cast into the
> Void. The Silmarils are lost forever — one in the earth, one in the sea, one
> in the sky.
>
> Eärendil sails on. Deathless. Transformed. Alone save for Elwing, who
> sometimes takes the form of a white bird and flies to him among the stars.
>
> He cannot return to Middle-earth. He cannot embrace his sons. He cannot walk
> the shores he loved.
>
> But every night, the Star rises. And every night, hope is kindled in the
> hearts of those who see it.
>
> This is the doom and the grace of Eärendil the Mariner.
>
> *And the light endures.*

## VIII. Implementation Notes (For Future Coding)

### Campaign Structure
- **Run count:** Unlimited (Silmaril-gated, not run-count-based)
- **Victory trigger:** 15 Silmarils recovered across all runs
- **Defeat trigger:** None. The campaign continues until victory.
- **Score tracking:** Sum of all individual run scores
- **Hero pool:** Restricted to 10 specific characters (2 new + 8 existing)

- Opening Scene: Fires once at campaign creation

- Eärendil's plea on behalf of both Elves and Men per Silmarillion
- The Valar's decision to go to war per Silmarillion
- The prohibition on returning to mortal lands per Silmarillion
- The hallowing of Vingilot and its placement in the sky per Silmarillion
- Eärendil becoming Gil-Estel, the Star of High Hope per Silmarillion
- The choice given to Elrond and Elros per Silmarillion
- Ulmo's role as protector and guide of exiles per Silmarillion (explicitly for
  Tuor; implied for Eärendil)
- Gil-galad dwelling in Sirion under Eärendil's protection per Silmarillion
- Tuor's eventual fate (sailing west with Idril, "sundered from the fate of
  Men") per Silmarillion and Unfinished Tales
- Idril's foresight and the Secret Way per Silmarillion and Unfinished Tales
- The role of the Silmaril as beacon and guide per Silmarillion

### Creative Liberties (Tolkien-Compatible)
- The **prophetic dream framework** is original. Tolkien does not describe
  Eärendil having visions before building Vingilot. However:
  - Ulmo frequently sent visions (Tuor, Finrod, Turgon)
  - Eärendil's sense of purpose is described as a longing he "did not fully
    understand"
  - The Straight Road was hidden; finding it required divine guidance
  - The creative liberty: making the "longing" into explicit visions is
    compatible with Tolkien's metaphysics and narrative patterns

- The **three Silmarils as symbolic proofs** is original. In Tolkien's text,
  Eärendil bears one Silmaril (Elwing's). The scenario's conceit that he
  "recovers" three in vision-descents is gameplay-driven, but it is framed as
  metaphorical/prophetic, not literal. This is acceptable because:
  - The scenario explicitly states the descents are visions, not reality
  - The "recovered Silmarils" are proofs/demonstrations, not physical jewels
  - The three lights (Past, Present, Future) are thematic interpretations

- The **"no defeat" condition** mirrors Lúthien's scenario and reflects
  Eärendil's canonical success — he *does* reach Valinor, he *does* move the
  Valar to action. The gameplay reflects this: the campaign cannot fail, only
  take longer.

- The **hero pool as "people of Sirion"** is slightly simplified. Not all the
  heroes lived in Sirion simultaneously (Glorfindel died in Gondolin's fall;
  Galdor may have as well; Voronwë's fate is uncertain). However, in the
  scenario's prophetic-dream framing, the heroes are not literally present but
  are *remembered/envisioned* by Eärendil, making their inclusion narratively
  justified.

- Elwing's **personality and active role** in the scenario is inferred from
  sparse canonical details. Tolkien tells us she leaped into the sea and was
  transformed; the scenario builds a character around this moment of desperate
  courage, which is compatible with the text.

- **Existing character stats** for Tuor (N:29), Idril (N:31), Gil-galad (N:32),
  and others are used as-is from `character.txt`. Their power levels are lower
  than the originally proposed scenario-specific stats, resulting in a harder
  campaign (avg Power ~1.5 vs. originally planned ~2.0). This is thematically
  appropriate: these are refugees, not heroes at their peak.

### Tolkien's Core Theme

The Eärendil story is, for Tolkien, about *intercession* and *sacrifice*. A
Half-elven mariner who gives up everything — his home, his children, his mortal
life — to carry a message of hope to powers who had seemingly abandoned
Middle-earth.

The scenario rests on this theme: Eärendil is not a conqueror. He does not slay
Morgoth. He does not reclaim the Silmarils by force. He *asks*. He pleads. He
shows the Valar what is worth saving, and his humility and sacrifice move them
to act.

The "three lights" framework is designed to reinforce this: Eärendil gathers
*proofs* (remembrance, defiance, hope) to demonstrate to the Valar that
Middle-earth deserves their aid. The gameplay is about gathering evidence for a
case of mercy.

And the ending — Eärendil transformed into a star, forever separated from the
world he saved — is the ultimate Tolkienian bittersweet victory. He succeeded.
He saved the world. But the cost was everything he loved. And yet — the star
shines. Hope endures. The light does not fade.

That is the scenario. That is Eärendil. That is the Mariner's Hope.
