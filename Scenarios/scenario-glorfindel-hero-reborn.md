# Hero Reborn: Glorfindel's Return

## Scenario Design Document

---

## I. Lore Foundation

### The Fall and Rebirth of Glorfindel (Silmarillion, Chapter 23; HoME XII)

When Gondolin fell to the treachery of Maeglin and the hosts of Morgoth, the
survivors fled through the secret way of Idril's Tunnel into the mountains. Upon
the pass of Cirith Thoronath, the refugees were ambushed by Orcs and a Balrog.
Glorfindel, Lord of the House of the Golden Flower, stepped forth and fought the
Balrog alone upon a pinnacle of rock. He cast it down, but in its fall the demon
clutched him by his golden hair, and together they plunged into the abyss.

Thorondor bore his body from the depths, and the refugees buried him beneath a
cairn of stones upon the mountain. It is told that yellow flowers grew upon that
mound, though all around was barren rock.

By the grace of the Valar, Glorfindel's spirit was not long in the Halls of
Mandos. For his sacrifice in defending the people of Gondolin, he was granted a
rare gift: rebirth. He was re-embodied in Valinor and dwelt there in the peace
of the Blessed Realm until the Valar sent him back to Middle-earth in the Third
Age, bearing power almost equal to the Maiar, a living weapon against the
darkness that would rise again.

### Thematic Analysis

This is the **default scenario** of Sil-QH. Where other scenarios tell focused
stories with fixed run counts and restricted hero pools, the Glorfindel scenario
tells the widest story: *the entire war against Morgoth*, experienced through the
eyes of every hero of the First Age.

The narrative frame is Glorfindel's slow return from death. He lies in the Halls
of Mandos, his memory shattered by the fall and the fire. Each Silmaril recovered
from Angband restores a fragment of his identity; each chapter is a Vala reaching
through the veil to touch his sleeping spirit. The story is one of *remembering
forward* -- a hero gradually recovering lost identity and finding renewed purpose.

But Glorfindel is more than a protagonist. He is a *witness*. The 51 heroes the
player sends into Angband are the entire roster of the First Age's champions --
sons of Feanor, children of Fingolfin, lords of Doriath, dwarven smiths, mortal
warriors. Each run is a thread in the tapestry of the War of the Jewels, and
Glorfindel's awakening consciousness perceives them all: every triumph and every
fall, every Silmaril won and every hero lost. His rebirth is earned not by his
own hand alone but by the cumulative courage of every soul who dared Angband's
depths.

The tragedy is dual. On the victory path, Glorfindel awakens fully, remembers
Gondolin, remembers the Balrog, remembers his sacrifice -- and is sent forth
again, because the darkness is never truly defeated, only held at bay. On the
defeat path, the deaths mount, the curses deepen, and the Valar's voices fall
silent one by one, until Morgoth's laughter echoes in the void.

In gameplay terms, this creates an **open campaign** (Silmaril-gated, not
run-count-based) where the player has access to the full hero roster of 51
characters across 6 houses, and must recover 15 Silmarils before suffering 15
deaths.

### Key Lore Touchstones Referenced

- **The Fall of Gondolin** -- The hidden city's betrayal and destruction, the
  flight of the refugees through Idril's Tunnel, and Glorfindel's duel with the
  Balrog upon the Cirith Thoronath. (Silmarillion, "Of Tuor and the Fall of
  Gondolin"; HoME II, The Fall of Gondolin)
- **The Halls of Mandos** -- Where Elvish spirits dwell after death, awaiting
  judgment or rebirth. Mandos, the Doomsman, remembers all fates and keeps all
  accounts. (Silmarillion, "Of the Beginning of Days")
- **Re-embodiment** -- In Tolkien's metaphysics, Elves can be re-embodied after
  death by the Valar's grace, though this is rare. Glorfindel is the only named
  Elf confirmed to have been reborn and returned to Middle-earth. (HoME XII,
  "The Peoples of Middle-earth"; HoME X, "Morgoth's Ring")
- **The Fourteen Valar** -- Each of the fourteen Valar appears in the story
  chapters as Glorfindel's spirit is restored: Nienna (mercy), Manwe (breath),
  Varda (stars), Tulkas (courage), Yavanna (life), Ulmo (the deep), Vaire
  (memory), Mandos (doom), Vana (grace), Este (rest), Orome (the hunt), Nessa
  (joy), Aule (strength), and Irmo (dreams). (Silmarillion, "Valaquenta")
- **The Silmarils in the Iron Crown** -- Morgoth set the three Silmarils in his
  iron crown and wore them always. Their light burned his flesh perpetually, and
  his hands were scorched black and never healed from the pain. (Silmarillion,
  "Of the Silmarils")
- **The War of the Jewels** -- The five great battles of Beleriand, the Siege of
  Angband, the kingdoms of the Noldor and Sindar, and the alliances of Elves,
  Men, and Dwarves against Morgoth. The entire scope of the First Age is the
  backdrop for this scenario. (Silmarillion, Chapters 13-24)

## II. Scenario Overview

**Name:** Hero Reborn
**Subtitle:** Glorfindel's Return
**Type:** Open Campaign (Silmaril-gated, no fixed run count)
**Victory Condition:** Recover 15 Silmarils from Angband
**Defeat Condition:** 15 hero deaths (Glorfindel's deaths do not count; Edain
deaths do not count)
**Hero Pool:** 51 heroes across 6 houses (full roster)
**Quests:** 6 Valar quests, each unlocking a bindable Oath

### Narrative Frame

The player is Glorfindel, lying in the Halls of Mandos, shattered by his fall
from the pinnacle of the Cirith Thoronath. His memory is gone. His identity is
ash. But the Valar have not abandoned him -- one by one, they reach through the
veil to touch his sleeping spirit, each Silmaril recovered from Morgoth's crown
rekindling a fragment of who he was.

The heroes the player controls are not Glorfindel himself (except when the player
chooses him from the roster). They are the champions of the First Age -- the
people Glorfindel knew, fought beside, loved, or only heard of in song. Each
run is a thread in the tapestry of the War of the Jewels, and Glorfindel's
dreaming mind perceives them all.

When 15 Silmarils have been recovered, Glorfindel's memory is fully restored.
He stands in the ruins of Gondolin in his mind's eye, remembers everything, and
is sent forth again by Varda, Manwe, and Mandos. If 15 deaths are reached first,
the dream collapses into darkness, and Morgoth's triumph is complete.

### Progression Structure

Unlike the fixed-run scenarios (Feanor: 7 runs, Fingolfin: 10 runs), the
Glorfindel scenario has no predetermined length. The campaign continues as long
as the player can recover Silmarils faster than heroes fall. This creates a
dynamic tension between aggressive play (more Silmarils but more risk of death)
and cautious play (fewer deaths but slower progress).

### Scoring System

- Each successful escape from Angband contributes to a combined score
- Progressive diminishing returns: best/1 + second/2 + third/4 + fourth/8...
- Quest completions add bonus points
- Blessing points from fallen heroes unlock permanent blessings for future runs

### Curse/Blessing Economy

- Each death adds curses (stat and skill penalties) to subsequent runs
- Accumulated blessing points from fallen heroes' scores unlock blessings
- Blessings grant: improved supply limits, starting artifacts, reduced thresholds
- Oaths from completed quests carry over to future heroes in the same campaign
- Quests can be completed multiple times (capped at 7 per metarun)

### Design Rationale

The full hero pool is the defining feature of this scenario. Where the Feanor
scenario restricts the player to 9 heroes representing Feanor's memories, and
the Fingolfin scenario restricts to 10 heroes representing Fingolfin's loved
ones, the Glorfindel scenario embraces the *entire War of the Jewels*.

This reflects Glorfindel's unique position in Tolkien's mythology. He is not a
king or an oath-maker. He is a *defender* -- someone who stepped forward at the
critical moment and gave his life for others. His story is not about his own
ambition or tragedy but about the people he protected. In the Halls of Mandos,
dreaming toward rebirth, he perceives the totality of the war: every hero's
struggle is part of his awakening.

The six houses create natural gameplay diversity:

- **Feanorians** are powerful but cursed -- high stats, smithing mastery, but
  KINSLAYER and TREACHERY flags threaten stolen Silmarils and murdered kin
- **Fingolfinrim** are the balanced warriors -- strong in melee and will, with
  Glorfindel himself as the "safe" hero whose deaths don't count
- **Finarfinrim** are the song-masters -- grace-focused, with powerful song
  abilities but lower combat stats
- **Sindar** are the deep roster -- balanced and diverse, anchored by Luthien
  and Melian's extraordinary powers
- **Naugrim** are the crafters -- smithing specialists with Telchar's unmatched
  weapon-craft, offset by archery penalties
- **Edain** are the mortals -- their deaths don't count (GIFTERU on the house),
  making them ideal for risky runs, but their stats tend lower and some carry
  Morgoth's Curse

## IV. Special Hero: Glorfindel of the Golden Flower

Glorfindel is the scenario's default and most important hero. He occupies a
unique mechanical and narrative position.

```
N:10:Glorfindel
A: of the Golden Flower
B:You rise in valor-golden defender of Gondolin, whose courage felled a Balrog.
F:MEL_AFFINITY | ARC_PENALTY | GIFTERU
S:2:2:1:1
C:2:9:0:13  # Controlled Retreat, Rapid Attack
P:3
E:40:0:1:1  # Amulet of Last Chances
```

**Stats:** STR +2, DEX +2, CON +1, GRA +1 (Total +6, Power 3)

**Key Abilities:**
- Rapid Attack (melee speed)
- Control (subtlety in combat)
- Controlled Retreat (evasion)

**Special Traits:**
- **GIFTERU** -- When Glorfindel dies, the campaign death counter is *not*
  incremented. This makes him the "safe" hero for learning and exploration.
  Narratively, this reflects his special status: he has already died once and
  been granted rebirth. Death holds no final claim on him.
- **Amulet of Last Chances** -- A unique starting artifact, Glorfindel's anchor
  to the waking world, his last thread of identity from the fall.

**Lore significance:** Glorfindel is described by Tolkien as having returned
from Mandos with power "almost equal to the Maiar." He is the bridge between
the First Age (where this game is set) and the Third Age (where he appears in
*The Lord of the Rings* aiding Frodo at the Ford of Bruinen). The game captures
the *moment of transition* -- his spirit in Mandos, slowly awakening toward
rebirth.

## V. Narrative Script

### Opening Scene (Plays Once, at Campaign Start)

> **Title: Nienna's Mercy**
>
> A cold, empty darkness surrounds you. Your memories scatter like ashes on the
> wind, leaving only faint sparks of courage within. You sense deep sorrow,
> gentle and unseen -- Nienna's quiet weeping steadies your uncertain heart.

This opening is deliberately spare. Unlike the Feanor scenario's vivid
ash-field imagery or the Fingolfin scenario's thunderous challenge at Angband's
gates, Glorfindel's story begins in *nothing*. No identity. No memory. Only
the faintest warmth of divine compassion. The player begins as a blank slate,
and the entire campaign is the process of filling it in.

### Victory Path Chapters (Type 0: Silmaril Recovery)

These chapters trigger as the cumulative Silmaril count across all runs
increases. Each represents a Vala reaching through the veil of Mandos to touch
Glorfindel's sleeping spirit, restoring a fragment of memory and identity.

---

#### Chapter 1 -- Whisper of Manwe (1 Silmaril)

> A faint, distant echo stirs a forgotten defiance within you. You feel you
> once stood against darkness, but details slip away. A cool breeze brushes
> your spirit, the breath of Manwe, offering comfort and guiding you forward.

---

#### Chapter 2 -- Stars of Varda (2 Silmarils)

> Dim images drift through your mind: stone halls, green forests, lands bathed
> in starlight. You feel deeply drawn to them, yet cannot grasp why. Above,
> the brief glimmer of distant stars -- Varda's silent eyes -- remind you of
> forgotten hope.

---

#### Chapter 3 -- Courage of Tulkas (3 Silmarils)

> You feel weight upon your shoulders, like armor worn proudly. A fragment of
> bravery stirs within you, an echo of battles faced with courage. A joyful
> strength fills your limbs -- the unseen presence of laughing Tulkas urging
> you onward.

---

#### Chapter 4 -- Breath of Yavanna (4 Silmarils)

> Vague shapes of companions appear briefly, allies of various races -- faces
> blurred, names lost. Yet loyalty and friendship resonate deep within. The
> gentle scent of new leaves fills your senses, a subtle gift from Yavanna,
> encouraging remembrance.

---

#### Chapter 5 -- Call of Ulmo (5 Silmarils)

> A haunting song, older and deeper than you can comprehend, drifts past. You
> sense an ancient melancholy, stirring hidden memories of endless seas. Far
> away, waves whisper -- Ulmo calling softly, pulling your heart toward truth.

---

#### Chapter 6 -- Weaving of Vaire (6 Silmarils)

> Your fingers trace flowing runes carved gently into stone walls. The symbols
> resonate with warmth, though you cannot yet read them clearly. Delicate
> threads of silver glimmer faintly -- Vaire, the Weaver, slowly restoring your
> fractured memories.

---

#### Chapter 7 -- Doom of Mandos (7 Silmarils)

> Time stretches behind you endlessly. A sense of duty fills your spirit --
> ageless, unwavering, and grave. A distant bell echoes faintly -- Mandos,
> keeper of fate, reminding you of unfinished tasks awaiting your return.

---

#### Chapter 8 -- Bloom of Vana (8 Silmarils)

> You glimpse yourself wielding a sword with graceful mastery -- swift, precise,
> radiant. Your spirit swells with pride and confidence, yet the moment is
> fleeting. Petals dance unseen around you, a subtle touch from ever-young Vana,
> honoring your grace.

---

#### Chapter 9 -- Rest of Este (9 Silmarils)

> Soft words rise clearly within your mind: "Estel brillies teni mornie canta."
> Hope shines brightest when darkness gathers. Calm spreads gently through you,
> easing pain and doubt -- a comforting gift from gentle Este.

---

#### Chapter 10 -- Horn of Orome (10 Silmarils)

> You sense a hidden city -- gleaming towers, secret valleys, beautiful yet
> fragile. Your heart fills with a fierce protective instinct. Far off, a
> hunting horn sounds urgently, boldly -- Orome, calling your spirit to reclaim
> its path.

---

#### Chapter 11 -- Steps of Nessa (11 Silmarils)

> A sudden pulse of self-sacrifice fills your heart -- a moment when you
> willingly traded your life for others. Your spirit trembles at the strength
> of that choice. Quick, joyful steps echo beside you, invisible yet clear --
> Nessa's fleet encouragement.

---

#### Chapter 12 -- Strength of Aule (12 Silmarils)

> A towering shadow engulfed in flame looms in memory -- fearsome, roaring, yet
> you faced it bravely, unyielding. Solid ground firms beneath your feet, rock
> and stone reinforced by the mighty hand of Aule, grounding your courage.

---

#### Chapter 13 -- Light of Varda and Manwe (13 Silmarils)

> Your spirit recalls death, sacrifice -- but also purpose beyond defeat. Gentle
> yet majestic voices whisper in harmony: "Even death cannot silence your
> courage. Rise once more." Radiance pierces darkness -- starlight and sky,
> blessings of Varda and Manwe.

---

#### Chapter 14 -- Dreams of Irmo (14 Silmarils)

> Fragments of golden radiance flash clearly in your memory -- hair like sunlit
> gold, a fall amidst flames, cheers of saved souls. Your identity nearly
> emerges, bright yet elusive. Calmness washes over you like deep sleep, the
> healing dreams of Irmo gently restoring your mind.

---

#### Chapter 15 -- Mandate of the Valar (15 Silmarils -- VICTORY)

> You stand now amid the ruined stones of a hidden city -- Gondolin. Memory
> rushes fully clear: you fell here, defeating a Balrog, your life sacrificed
> to protect your people. Three powerful voices -- Varda, Manwe, and Mandos --
> call to you clearly: "Rise again, Glorfindel, hero reborn. Morgoth's shadow
> stirs once more -- your light must stand against it."

### Defeat Path Chapters (Type 1: Hero Deaths)

These chapters trigger as the cumulative death count rises. They tell the
story of Glorfindel's spirit failing to awaken -- the Valar's voices fading,
the darkness of Mandos deepening from sanctuary into prison, and ultimately
into void.

The defeat path is deliberately longer than the victory path (36 chapters vs
16), reflecting that failure is gradual and grinding. Each death adds curses to
future runs, creating a downward spiral that mirrors the narrative.

---

#### Death 1 -- Ash and Iron

> Darkness absolute, yet far off the ringing of hammers upon iron -- echoes from
> Morgoth's forge. A stern whisper, chill as Angband's stone: "Once has thy
> courage faltered."

---

#### Death 2 -- Chain Rattle

> Iron links scrape upon stone, mourning your failure. A voice vast and mocking
> speaks from above, dreadful in authority: "Thy return is woven into fate
> itself."

---

#### Death 3 -- Tarnished Star

> Amid bitter smoke, one pale star -- Varda's grace dimmed by shadow. Courage
> flickers within, shrouded yet not extinguished, fading as it retreats from
> your grasp.

---

#### Death 4 -- The Mocking Wind

> A cruel wind whispers falsely in Manwe's voice, bringing neither comfort nor
> counsel. Grim banners flutter, black as despair. Once you walked taller than
> this fall.

---

#### Death 5 -- Unwatered Seeds

> Within your palm, a seedling withers to dust. Fragments of friendship drift
> past, their warmth drowned by barren earth turned slag. Hope falters, vanishing
> into ashes.

---

#### Death 6 -- Drowned Hymn

> An ancient song rises, deep and mournful -- Ulmo's lament silenced beneath
> Angband's stone. Black water drips, relentless and cold, drowning melody and
> memory alike.

---

#### Death 7 -- Unwoven

> Silver threads unravel before your eyes, gentle runes blurring like tears. A
> weaver's unseen hand draws back sorrowfully -- Vaire's tapestry frays, memories
> lost once more.

---

#### Death 8 -- Bell of Doom

> A bell tolls in distant halls, solemn as Mandos' judgment. He condemns not,
> yet neither does he comfort. His memory endures, marking well each failure.

---

#### Death 9 -- Petals in Soot

> A pale blossom floats downward, scorched by unseen flame. As it lands softly
> upon your armor, it crumbles into ash. Far away, joy dances still -- but here
> lies ruin.

---

#### Death 10 -- Spent Breath

> Words stir within you -- *Estel brillies...* -- yet your breath freezes in
> your lungs. No rest comes; your spirit is compelled once more into torment,
> or oblivion.

---

#### Death 11 -- Far Horn, Near Chains

> Orome's horn echoes distant and bold, a summons you cannot answer. Chains bind
> tighter with each futile struggle, mocking your imprisoned valor.

---

#### Death 12 -- Stilled Steps

> You rush to protect, but find neither footing nor strength. Heroism unrewarded;
> your sacrifice hollow. A heavy voice intones your failure, each fall a deeper
> wound.

---

#### Death 13 -- Broken Stone

> You face fire and shadow, defiant -- but the earth betrays you. Stone shatters
> beneath your feet, and Aule's strength recedes. Yet deep stone remembers
> courage still.

---

#### Death 14 -- False Dawn

> A pale light rises, false and cold -- a mockery of Varda's starlit grace.
> Morgoth laughs within the cruel dawn, yet even false light hints at true
> radiance hidden far beyond your reach.

---

#### Death 15 -- The Shut Gate

> Great iron doors clang shut, a sound of dreadful judgment. Three voices fall
> silent, their disappointment a heavy weight. Your path grows darker still,
> the light of hope a distant memory.

---

#### Deaths 16-35 -- The Deepening Dark

As deaths accumulate beyond 15, the narrative descends through increasingly
bleak stages: Echo of Silence, Fading Embers, The Weight of Chains, Shattered
Shield, The Longest Night, Nameless Dread, The Barren Field, The Unheard
Prayer, The Shadow's Grasp, The Final Tear, The Broken Oath, The Empty Throne,
The Coldest Steel, The Dying Star, The Lost Path, The Silent Bell, The Last
Echo, The Unmaking, The Void, and finally Morgoth's Triumph.

These chapters track a slow erasure of identity -- the inverse of the victory
path. Where each Silmaril added a Vala's voice, each cluster of deaths removes
one, until Glorfindel's spirit dissolves entirely and the Dark Lord's laughter
is the last sound in the emptiness.

---

### Special Chapter: Gift of Iluvatar (Death 0)

> Warm darkness embraces you -- not Morgoth's chains, but a gentle silence.
> This death is no defeat, but the Gift of Iluvatar itself. Your spirit passes
> briefly beyond the Circles of the World -- returning untouched by Mandos'
> count.

This chapter triggers specifically for GIFTERU heroes (Glorfindel, Durin, and
all Edain). Their deaths are narratively distinct: they pass beyond the world
entirely and return untouched, rather than adding to the doom-count. This is
Tolkien's "Gift of Men" made mechanical -- mortality as grace rather than curse.

---

## VI. The Quest System (Valar Quests)

Six great quests from the Valar are woven into the dungeon, each unlocking a
bindable Oath for future heroes. The quests are thematically tied to the
victory path chapters -- each Vala who appears in Glorfindel's awakening also
offers a challenge within Angband itself.

---

### Quest 1: Tulkas the Strong

**Type:** Hunt quest (roulette-based, depths 6-19)
**Objective:** Slay a named shadow creature
**Reward:** Legendary weapon artifact + **Oath of the Valorous Heart**

> "Ho, champion! I have marked your passage through these lightless depths.
> There is fire in your heart and strength in your arm! Such valour pleases
> me. But valour must be tested, as steel is tested in the forge!"

**Oath of the Valorous Heart:** +1 STR. Cannot attack fleeing enemies.

---

### Quest 2: Aule the Smith

**Type:** Crafting quest (vault-based, requires Smithing 10+)
**Objective:** Forge a worthy item at Aule's forge
**Reward:** +2 Smithing, Aule's Forge ability + **Oath of the Smith**

> "I have seen your works, mortal. There is a spark of the fire of creation
> within you. But a spark is not a flame. Here is my forge. Its fires burn
> with the heat of the world's heart. Create for me a thing of worth."

**Oath of the Smith:** +5 Smithing. Cannot equip weapons or armor not
self-crafted.

---

### Quest 3: Mandos the Doomsman

**Type:** Vault-based quest
**Objective:** Free Brodda's bound spirit (Turin's victim trapped by Morgoth)
**Reward:** +1 CON, Fear immunity + **Oath of Iron**

> "Child of Middle-earth, here lingers a spirit that should long ago have
> departed the circles of the world. The malice of Morgoth has kept it bound.
> Grant the tormented spirit its final release."

**Oath of Iron:** +1 CON. Cannot flee upward without possessing a Silmaril.

---

### Quest 4: Nienna, Lady of Pity

**Type:** Pacifist challenge (roulette-based, depths 14-25)
**Objective:** Reach the downward stair without taking a life
**Reward:** +1 GRA, Stealth enhancement + **Oath of Mercy**

> "Child, this place is filled with pain. The creatures that dwell here are
> not all born of evil, but are twisted and marred by the darkness they have
> long endured. Find the path downward, but raise no hand against the
> tormented things that dwell here."

**Oath of Mercy:** +1 GRA. Cannot harm Elves or Men.

---

### Quest 5: Orome, the Great Hunter

**Type:** Kill-count quest (roulette-based, depths 2-10)
**Objective:** Slay 100 wolves, 80 spiders, 60 serpents, or 30 vampires
**Reward:** +1 DEX, Hunter's Insight + **Oath of Silence**

> "The foulest kindreds of the Enemy have made their lairs throughout this
> realm. I call upon you to cleanse this darkness! Hunt any of these
> monstrous broods without pity!"

**Oath of Silence:** +1 DEX. Cannot use any singing abilities or songs.

---

### Quest 6: Varda, Lady of the Stars

**Type:** Vault assault (roulette-based, depths 1-3)
**Objective:** Slay Duruin the Balrog in his Bastion of shadow
**Reward:** Choice of blessed light items + **Oath of Light**

> "A shard of my light lies smothered below, stolen by a petty balrog named
> Duruin. Seek out that stronghold. Break his hold upon the stolen light."

**Oath of Light:** +1 Light radius. Cannot equip items that bear darkness or
reduce light radius.

---

### Quest Design Rationale

The six quests and their oaths create a **risk/reward specialization system**.
Each oath grants a significant bonus but imposes a permanent restriction for
that hero's run. Oaths unlocked in one run carry over to the full campaign,
meaning later heroes can choose to bind themselves to oaths earned by
predecessors.

This mirrors Tolkien's thematic treatment of oaths in the Silmarillion. Oaths
in Tolkien are never free -- the Oath of Feanor is the most terrible ever
spoken, and it binds its speakers to doom. The game's oaths are gentler but
follow the same logic: power comes at the price of freedom.

The oaths also create natural build archetypes:
- **Oath of Iron** + strong melee hero = aggressive "no retreat" build
- **Oath of the Smith** + Telchar/Feanor = self-sufficient crafting build
- **Oath of Silence** + stealth hero = silent assassin build
- **Oath of Mercy** + high-grace hero = pacifist/stealth build
- **Oath of Valor** + warrior hero = honorable combat build
- **Oath of Light** + perception hero = light-based exploration build

## VII. The Dungeon

### Structure

**Depth 0** (Surface) to **Depth 20** (Morgoth's Throne Room)

The dungeon represents the descent into Angband itself -- twenty levels of
increasing danger, from the outer tunnels to the throne room where Morgoth
sits with the Silmarils burning in his Iron Crown.

**Key Depths:**
- **0-5:** Outer tunnels. Early enemies, tutorial-level threats. Valar quests
  begin appearing here.
- **6-13:** The mid-deeps. Serious opposition. Most Valar quest challenges
  trigger in this range.
- **14-19:** The deep halls. Nienna's pacifist challenge lives here. The
  strongest non-unique enemies.
- **20:** Morgoth's throne room. The Iron Crown. Three Silmarils. The endgame.

### The Gameplay Loop

1. **Choose a hero** from the full 51-character roster (or Glorfindel for safety)
2. **Optionally bind an Oath** unlocked from a previous quest completion
3. **Descend** through procedurally generated levels
4. **Complete Valar quests** as they appear (optional but rewarding)
5. **Reach depth 20** and claim Silmarils from Morgoth's Iron Crown
6. **Escape to the surface** (Silmarils added to campaign total)
7. If the hero dies: curses accumulate; pick a new hero; repeat
8. Continue until **15 Silmarils** (victory) or **15 deaths** (defeat)

### Run Difficulty Modes

| Mode | Curses | Points per Blessing |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Normal | None | 400 |
| Hard | Light curses | 500 |
| Very Hard | 1 stack of 12 curses | 500 |
| Impossible | 2 stacks of 12 curses | 550 |

## IX. Lore Consistency Notes

### Strict Adherences
- Glorfindel's death fighting a Balrog at the Cirith Thoronath is per
  Silmarillion and HoME II ("The Fall of Gondolin")
- His re-embodiment by the Valar is per HoME XII ("The Peoples of Middle-earth")
  and HoME X ("Morgoth's Ring")
- The fourteen Valar who appear in the story chapters match Tolkien's Valaquenta
  exactly (excluding Morgoth/Melkor, who is counted among the Valar but has
  fallen)
- The Silmarils set in Morgoth's Iron Crown burning his flesh is per Silmarillion
- The Halls of Mandos as a place of waiting and judgment is per Silmarillion
- All 51 heroes are drawn from Tolkien's published works (Silmarillion, HoME,
  Unfinished Tales) and placed in their correct houses and relationships
- The Gift of Iluvatar (mortality as grace for Men) reflected in the GIFTERU
  mechanic for Edain is per Silmarillion ("Of Men")
- The six Valar quest-givers (Tulkas, Aule, Mandos, Nienna, Orome, Varda) and
  their characterizations are faithful to the Valaquenta
- Brodda the Easterling in Mandos' quest is from the Silmarillion (he was slain
  by Turin)

### Creative Liberties (Tolkien-Compatible)
- The "memory restoration through Silmaril recovery" conceit -- each Vala
  reaching through Mandos to touch Glorfindel's spirit -- is original but
  consistent with Tolkien's metaphysics, where the Valar can perceive and
  influence spirits in the Halls
- The specific Quenya phrase "Estel brillies teni mornie canta" (Hope shines
  brightest when darkness gathers) is invented but uses Tolkien's linguistic
  patterns
- The connection between Glorfindel's GIFTERU status and his canonical rebirth
  is an elegant gameplay-lore synthesis not found in Tolkien
- Durin's GIFTERU status reflects his epithet "the Deathless" -- in Tolkien,
  Durin's spirit is believed to reincarnate in his descendants, making him
  uniquely death-resistant among Dwarves
- The oath system generalizes Tolkien's specific treatment of oaths (primarily
  the Oath of Feanor) into a broader mechanical framework, but each oath's
  flavor text maintains Tolkienian gravity
- The defeat path's progressive erasure of Valar voices is original but
  thematically consistent -- Tolkien writes that Morgoth's power can "cloud"
  even the Valar's perceptions in his domain
