# The Precipice of Pain: Maedhros on Thangorodrim

## Scenario Design Document

---

## I. Lore Foundation

### The Captivity of Maedhros (Silmarillion, Chapter 13; HoME)

After the Dagor-nuin-Giliath and the death of Fëanor, Morgoth feigned a desire for parley with the Noldor. Maedhros, as eldest son and heir, came to the appointed place with more riders than was agreed. But Morgoth sent yet more, and with Balrogs. Maedhros was captured through treachery and borne to Angband.

There Morgoth hung him from a precipice of Thangorodrim by the right wrist upon a band of steel. He was held upon the face of the mountain where he could neither climb up nor fall down, in torment and despair, beneath the storms and the cold, within sight of his people's camps far below but beyond all rescue.

For nearly thirty years he hung there — twenty-eight, by the reckoning of the Sun that had not yet risen. In agony and darkness, with no hope of release save death, which would not come.

At last Fingon, son of Fingolfin, Maedhros's cousin whom Fëanor had betrayed by burning the ships, came seeking him out of pity and love that recalled their friendship in Valinor before the Oath and the Kinslaying. Fingon could not reach Maedhros to free him, and Maedhros in torment begged to be slain. Fingon prepared to shoot him through the heart as an act of mercy.

But Manwë sent Thorondor, King of Eagles, who bore Fingon up to the precipice. Fingon could not break the steel band, so he cut off Maedhros's hand at the wrist with his knife, and the Eagle bore them both down to safety.

Maedhros thanked Fingon with tears and said that his pain was healed by Fingon's deed, for the anguish of betrayal by his father Fëanor was now answered by the mercy of Fingolfin's house. He ceded the kingship of the Noldor to Fingolfin, though by right it was his, and thus prevented a civil war among the exiled Elves.

### Thematic Analysis

Where the Fëanor scenario is about *remembering backward* in the moment of death, and the Fingolfin scenario is about *remembering in combat*, and the Maglor scenario is about *remembering through confusion*, the first Maedhros scenario is about **forgetting in torment** — the dissolution of identity under unbearable suffering, and the question of what remains when everything else is stripped away.

This is not the heroic Maedhros of legend. Not the eldest son, not the leader, not the one-handed warrior-king. This is Maedhros *becoming* those things through the crucible of suffering. This is the moment when the fire of Fëanor's line is purified — or broken — by pain.

The scenario explores a simple question: *Who are you when you cannot remember your name?* When the Oath is forgotten, when your brothers' faces are lost, when even the reason you are suffering has been erased by endless agony — what core remains?

For Maedhros, the answer Tolkien gives is terrible and beautiful: *endurance*. Not heroism. Not defiance. Not even hope. Just the stubborn refusal to die, breath after breath, heartbeat after heartbeat, through decades of darkness.

And then, at the end: *grace*. Fingon's mercy. The hand severed. The rescue. The kingship surrendered. The first act of healing in a life defined by wounds.

In gameplay terms, this creates a **survival campaign** where the player does not know who they are playing as. The heroes are fragments of identity — faces half-remembered, voices from before the darkness, pieces of a self that is trying to reassemble itself. Early runs are pure confusion and weakness. Middle runs begin to recover fragments of memory. Late runs restore clarity and strength. The final run, when enough Silmarils (memory-fragments) are gathered, reveals the truth: you are Maedhros, and you are being rescued.

### Key Lore Touchstones Referenced

- **The Battle-under-Stars (Dagor-nuin-Giliath)** — First battle of the Wars of Beleriand, where Fëanor was slain and Maedhros commanded the host after his father's death. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **Morgoth's treacherous parley** — Morgoth offered to treat for peace and the return of a Silmaril. It was a trap. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **The capture** — Maedhros came with cavalry; Morgoth sent more, and Balrogs. All Maedhros's company were slain save himself. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **The hanging on Thangorodrim** — Hung by the right wrist from a precipice, in torment, for nearly thirty years. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **Fingon's search** — Fingon came alone, playing his harp, singing songs of Valinor. Maedhros heard and cried out. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **Thorondor's aid** — Manwë sent the King of Eagles to bear Fingon to the precipice. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **The severing of the hand** — Fingon could not break the steel, so he cut Maedhros's hand off to free him. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **The healing** — Maedhros was tended by Fingon, and the pain of betrayal was healed by this act of grace. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **The surrender of the crown** — Maedhros ceded the High Kingship to Fingolfin, preventing war among the Noldor. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **The friendship before exile** — Maedhros and Fingon were close in Valinor, before the Oath and Kinslaying divided their houses. (Silmarillion, "Of Eldamar")
- **The burning of the ships** — Fëanor burned the ships at Losgar, betraying Fingolfin's host. This treachery was laid at Maedhros's feet as well. (Silmarillion, "Of the Return of the Noldor")
- **Maedhros Russandol ("copper-top")** — His nickname in Valinor, for his red-copper hair inherited from Nerdanel. (HoME XII, "The Peoples of Middle-earth")

## II. Scenario Overview

**Name:** The Precipice of Pain
**Subtitle:** Maedhros on Thangorodrim
**Type:** Survival Campaign (Silmaril-gated, progressive identity recovery)
**Runs Required:** 12 (for the ~30 years of captivity, compressed)
**Victory Condition:** Recover 12 Silmarils (memory fragments) and survive
**Hero Pool:** 10 heroes, representing fragmented aspects of Maedhros's dissolving identity
**Difficulty:** Extreme early, moderate late (heroes grow stronger as identity recovers)

### Narrative Frame

The player does not know they are Maedhros. They are simply *pain*. A consciousness hanging in darkness, with no memory of how they came here, who they are, or why they suffer. There is cold. There is wind. There is the burning in the right wrist where steel cuts into flesh. There is thirst that never ends and hunger that gnaws but does not kill.

Each descent into "Angband" is not literal — it is the shape the mind's search takes. A drowning consciousness reaching down through layers of agony to find *something*, *anything* to hold onto. A name. A face. A reason.

Each Silmaril recovered is a fragment of memory: a brother's voice, the light of Valinor, a father's command, a betrayal, a song from before the darkness. Slowly, painfully, the player pieces together who they are and why they are here.

By the 12th Silmaril, the truth is complete: you are Maedhros, eldest son of Fëanor, and you have hung on Thangorodrim for thirty years, and you are about to be rescued.

### Progression Structure

The campaign is divided into four phases of three runs each:

| Phase | Runs | Theme | Memory Focus |
|-------|------|-------|--------------|
| 1 | 1-3 | Dissolution | "I am pain. I am cold. I am nothing." |
| 2 | 4-6 | Fragments | Names without faces. Voices without context. |
| 3 | 7-9 | Reconstruction | The Oath. The betrayal. The trap. The guilt. |
| 4 | 10-12 | Recognition | "I am Maedhros. And I am being rescued." |

### Hero Mechanics

Early heroes (Runs 1-3) are **extremely weak** — representing a shattered mind barely clinging to existence. Stats are penalized, skills are minimal. These runs are desperate scrambles for survival.

Middle heroes (Runs 4-6) begin to **recover strength** — as memory returns, so does a sense of self, and with it, capability.

Late heroes (Runs 7-12) are **moderately powerful** — the full identity is reassembling, and Maedhros's natural strength and leadership return.

The curve inverts the typical difficulty: the campaign starts nearly impossible and becomes progressively easier as the player recovers their identity.

## III. New Heroes

Eight new heroes to be added to `character.txt` for this scenario. These represent different aspects of Maedhros's fragmenting and recovering identity.

### Hero 1: The Pain

*Pure suffering, no identity, no memory*

```
# The first fragment. Not a person. Just the sensation of agony.
# Extremely weak. High Con (endurance) but crippled in all other stats.
# Stats +2, Power 0

N:XX:The Pain
Q:[auto-generated]
A:
B:You are agony-nameless, faceless, the cold and the steel and the endless burning wrist.
F:CURSE | SLOW_DIGESTION | CANNOT_SPEAK
S:-2:0:4:0
C:0:0  # Power
P:0
D:You are not a person. You are a sensation. The cold wind on skin that has
D:forgotten warmth. The steel band cutting into the right wrist, biting deeper
D:with each movement, each breath. The hunger that gnaws but does not kill. The
D:thirst that cracks the throat. You do not remember having a name. You do not
D:remember having a body before this body of pain. There is only the precipice,
D:the darkness, the storm, and the knowledge that this will not end, cannot
D:end, because even death has abandoned you to the cold.
```

**Lore notes:** This is the absolute nadir. Maedhros at his lowest, reduced to pure suffering. Tolkien describes the captivity as "torment and despair" with Maedhros "beyond all rescue." This hero embodies that state.

### Hero 2: The Wrist

*The wound, the point of torment, the focus of all suffering*

```
# The right wrist where the steel band cuts. Obsessive focus on the wound.
# Weak but slightly more capable than The Pain. Fixated on survival.

N:XX:The Wrist
Q:[auto-generated]

F:CURSE | CANNOT_USE_SHIELD | SLOW_REGEN

C:4:7  # Listen (hypersensitive to pain)
P:0
D:There is a hand below you. Your hand. You think it is yours. It moves when
D:you will it to move, though the movement sends white fire up the arm. The
D:steel band cuts into the wrist above it, and you have tried everything —
D:twisting, pulling, gnawing at the metal with teeth you can barely feel. The
D:hand is grey now, bloodless, the fingers curled like claws. Sometimes you
D:forget it is there. Sometimes it is all you can think about. You wonder if
D:you could cut it off. You wonder if that would free you, or only add a new
D:dimension to the pain.
```

**Lore notes:** The right wrist is where Maedhros is bound. Eventually Fingon will cut off the hand at this point. This hero represents the hyperfocus on the wound that suffering brings.

### Hero 3: The Storm-Watcher

*Awareness of the external world, the weather, the mountain*

```
# Slightly more external focus. Watches the storms, the stars (when visible).
# Beginning to perceive the world beyond the pain.
# Stats +4, Power 1

N:XX:The Storm-Watcher
Q:[auto-generated]
A:
B:You count the lightning-you mark the stars' wheel, the only clock in the dark.
F:PER_AFFINITY | SLOW_DIGESTION
S:-1:1:3:1
C:4:7:4:2  # Listen, Keen Senses
P:1
D:The storms come in patterns. You have learned this. Seven days of wind, then
D:three of cold stillness, then the lightning, then the snow. You count them.
D:It is something to do. Something other than the pain. At night, when the
D:clouds break, you see stars — the same stars you saw when you first came to
D:this place (when was that? decades? centuries?). They have not moved. Or they
D:have moved so slowly you cannot perceive it. You watch them turn, and the
D:watching is a kind of companion, and you are grateful for it, though you do
D:not remember what gratitude is.
```

**Lore notes:** Tolkien mentions that Maedhros was held "beneath the storms." The awareness of natural cycles — storms, stars, cold — would be one of the few external anchors for a mind dissolving in pain.

### Hero 4: The Voice

*Memory of being able to speak, of commanding, of identity through speech*

```
# Recovery of voice. Weak but can communicate. First hint of social identity.
# Stats +4, Power 1

N:XX:The Voice
Q:[auto-generated]
A:, the Echo
B:You speak into wind-your words scatter, but they are yours, proof you once were someone.
F:SNG_AFFINITY | WIL_AFFINITY
S:0:0:2:2
C:7:3  # Song of Freedom (symbolic, represents speech recovering)
P:1
D:You spoke today. You do not remember the last time. The sound of your own
D:voice startled you — cracked, thin, barely human. You said a word. What word?
D:You cannot remember. But you said it, and the wind took it, and it was *yours*.
D:You try again: "I..." The word falls away. I *what*? I am? I was? I want? You
D:do not know. But the capacity to speak is returning, and with it the sense
D:that you are not merely pain, not merely body. You are something that can
D:speak. And if you can speak, you can *name*. And if you can name, you might
D:remember your own name.
```

**Lore notes:** Maedhros would have lost the ability to speak coherently after years of isolation. The return of speech is the return of identity. Tolkien notes that when Fingon sang, Maedhros "cried out" — he could still produce sound.

### Hero 6: The Fire-Son

*Memory of Fëanor, the father, the Oath, the reason for everything*

```

# Stats +6, Power 2

N:XX:The Fire-Son
Q:[auto-generated]
A:
B:You remember fire-a voice like thunder, a command you could not refuse, an oath that burns still.
F:WIL_AFFINITY | MEL_AFFINITY | CURSE
S:2:1:2:1
C:5:9:0:10  # Majesty, Zone of Control
P:2
D:His face comes back in fragments: eyes like embers, hair black as the void
```

**Lore notes:** Fëanor's dying command bound his sons to continue the Oath. Maedhros, as eldest, bore the heaviest burden. The memory of Fëanor is central to understanding why Maedhros is suffering.

### Design Rationale

Each hero represents a stage in the reassembly of a shattered psyche:
- **Phase 1** is dissolution and survival
- **Phase 2** is fragmentation and the first sparks of identity
- **Phase 3** is reconstruction through painful memory
- **Phase 4** is recognition and rescue

The arc mirrors trauma recovery: from being consumed by pain, to noticing the external world, to recovering language and social memory, to understanding the cause of suffering, to finally accepting rescue and grace.

## V. Narrative Script

### Opening Scene (Plays Once, at Campaign Start)

> **Title: The Precipice**
>
> Cold.
>
> That is the first thing. The only thing. Cold that seeps into bones you can
> barely feel, cold that makes the steel band cutting into your wrist burn like
> fire even though fire is the opposite of cold, cold that is the only
> companion in the dark.
>
> You do not know how long you have been here.
>
> You do not know where "here" is, except that it is high (the wind screams
> proof of that) and it is exposed (the storms find you every time) and it is
> inescapable (you have tried).
>
> There is a band of steel around your right wrist. You hang from it. Below you:
> nothing. Darkness. A fall that would end this, if you could reach it. But the
> steel holds, and your body (thin now, wasted, but still inexplicably alive)
> will not die.
>
> Above you: the band is anchored to something. Rock. The face of a mountain.
> You cannot reach it. You have tried to climb the band, hand over hand, but
> your strength failed, and you fell back, and the jerk of the steel nearly tore
> your hand off, and you hung gasping for hours (days?) afterward.
>
> You are alone.
>
> You do not remember your name. You do not remember how you came here. There
> are fragments — fire? voices? a face laughing in the dark? — but they scatter
> when you reach for them.
>
> All you know is this: the cold. The steel. The wind. The hunger that gnaws
> but does not kill. The thirst that cracks your throat but leaves you living.
> The pain in the wrist that has become the center of the universe.
>
> And below, far below, when the clouds break: tiny lights. Fires. A camp.
> People.
>
> They do not come.
>
> You have screamed. You have begged. Your voice (when it still worked) carried
> nothing but wind.
>
> And so you hang. And the days pass. Or years. You have lost count.
>
> In the darkness of your mind, something stirs. A fragment. A memory of...
> what? Warmth? Light? A purpose?
>
> You reach for it, down through the layers of pain, down into the dark inside
> yourself where something might still remain.

### Memory Chapters (Run Progression — Type 0: Identity Recovery)

These play at the START of each run, representing Maedhros's consciousness reaching down through trauma to find fragments of self.

---

#### Run 1 — Phase 1: I Am Pain

> **Title: The First Fragment**
>
> You are pain.
>
> There is no you. There is only the sensation: the burning wrist, the cold
> wind, the hunger, the emptiness.
>
> In the darkness — the darkness inside your mind, not the darkness of night —
> you search for something. Anything. A handhold. A name. A reason.
>
> The darkness does not answer.
>
> You descend anyway. Because there is nothing else to do. Because the pain on
> the precipice is unbearable, and the pain in the search might at least be
> *different*.

---

#### Run 2 — Phase 1: The Wrist Remembers

> **Title: The Point of Torment**
>
> The steel cuts deeper. Or perhaps it has always been this deep, and you are
> only now noticing. The wrist is swollen, grey, bloodless below the band. The
> hand dangles like a dead thing.
>
> You wonder: if you cut it off, would you be free?
>
> The thought is almost comforting. An action. A choice. Something other than
> passive endurance.
>
> But you have no knife. And your teeth cannot reach. And even if they could,
> the pain of gnawing through your own wrist would likely kill you before the
> cutting was done.
>
> So you hang. And you focus on the wrist. And in the focusing, a fragment of
> memory stirs: *You had two hands once. You held things. You made things.*
>
> What things?
>
> The darkness swallows the question.

---

#### Run 3 — Phase 1: The Storms Are Counted

> **Title: Patterns in the Dark**
>
> The storms come in cycles. You have begun to notice this. Seven days of wind.
> Three of stillness. Then lightning. Then snow.
>
> You count them. It gives you something to do. Something to anchor your mind
> to, other than the pain.
>
> Today is the fourth day of wind. Or the fifth. You lose count sometimes, when
> the cold steals your thoughts.
>
> But the counting is important. Because if you can count, you can *think*. And
> if you can think, you are not just pain. You are something that observes pain.
>
> A self. Small. Fragile. But real.
>
> In the darkness, you reach for more fragments.

---

#### Run 4 — Phase 2: The Voice Returns

> **Title: Speaking into Wind**
>
> You spoke today. The first word in... how long?
>
> It was cracked, thin, barely recognizable as language. But it was *yours*.
>
> You try again: "I..."
>
> I what? I am? I was? I will?
>
> The sentence will not complete. But the capacity to speak is returning, and
> with it the sense that you are a thing that can *name*.
>
> If you can name the world, perhaps you can name yourself.
>
> The darkness inside your mind is still vast, but at its edges, you see lights
> now. Fragments. Faces. Words.
>
> You descend toward them.

---

#### Run 5 — Phase 2: The Brothers Were Seven

> **Title: Faces Without Names**
>
> There were others.
>
> You remember them suddenly, vividly: red-haired, like you (your hair is red,
> you know that now, though you cannot see it). They stood in a line. They
> spoke in unison. They swore—
>
> What did they swear?
>
> The words are lost. But the weight remains. An oath. A promise. A binding.
>
> You try to count them. Six? Seven? Their faces blur when you focus, but the
> love is sharp, and the grief sharper.
>
> They are gone. Or you are gone from them. The separation is agony worse than
> the steel.
>
> In the darkness, you search for their names.

---

#### Run 6 — Phase 2: The Father's Face

> **Title: Spirit of Fire**
>
> His face comes back like a lightning strike: black-haired, eyes like embers,
> hands that shaped wonders and commanded obedience without question.
>
> Fëanor.
>
> Your father.
>
> He died. You carried his body. His last words were a command: *Hold the Oath.
> Avenge me.*
>
> And you did. You tried. You—
>
> The memory fractures. But the core remains: you are Fëanor's son. The eldest.
> The one who bore the burden when he fell.
>
> And the burden led you here. To the steel. To the cold. To the endless years.
>
> Was it worth it?
>
> The question echoes in the dark, unanswered.

---

#### Run 7 — Phase 3: The Parley Was a Trap

> **Title: The Betrayed**
>
> You remember now.
>
> Morgoth offered to treat. To return a Silmaril (the Silmarils! — the jewels
> your father made, the reason for the Oath, the center of everything). You
> came to the parley. You brought riders. He brought more.
>
> And Balrogs.
>
> Your company was slaughtered. You were taken. Dragged to Angband in chains.
> And Morgoth laughed.
>
> He hung you here. Not to kill you. To *display* you. A warning. Proof that
> even Fëanor's heir is nothing.
>
> You were a fool. And the price of folly is decades of steel and cold and the
> mocking laughter of the Dark Vala echoing in your memory.
>
> In the darkness, the rage rises. And with the rage, strength.

---

#### Run 8 — Phase 3: The Crown You Did Not Wear

> **Title: The High King in Chains**
>
> You are the High King of the Noldor.
>
> By right. By birth. By your father's death.
>
> Below you, far below, your people are camped. You have seen their fires. They
> think you are dead. Or they have given up.
>
> And Fingolfin — your uncle, whom your father betrayed by burning the ships —
> he leads them now. He has cause to hate you.
>
> If you are ever freed (and you begin, for the first time, to believe you
> might be), what will you do? Claim the crown? Demand your right?
>
> Or surrender it, as you have surrendered everything else?
>
> The question waits in the dark. Unanswered.

---

#### Run 9 — Phase 3: The Lost Hand

> **Title: The Maiming**
>
> The hand below the steel band is dead. Or nearly so. Grey. Cold. Unfeeling.
>
> You have tried to move the fingers. They curl slightly, like claws, but
> without strength.
>
> If you are freed, the hand will not come with you. The steel has cut too
> deep. The blood has stopped flowing.
>
> You will be one-handed. Maimed. A king who cannot hold a sword properly. A
> son of Fëanor who cannot craft.
>
> The thought should terrify you. Instead, it brings a strange calm.
>
> Let the hand go. Let it be the price. If losing it means freedom, the trade
> is fair.
>
> In the darkness, you prepare to let go.

---

#### Run 10 — Phase 4: A Harp in the Dark

> **Title: The Song from Valinor**
>
> You hear singing.
>
> At first you think it is madness. The wind playing tricks. Your mind
> fracturing further.
>
> But no—there—again. A voice. A harp. A melody from the Blessed Realm, from
> before the Exile, from when the world was young and you were whole.
>
> You know that voice.
>
> Fingon.
>
> Your cousin. Fingolfin's son. Whom you betrayed. Who has no cause to love you.
>
> He is here. Climbing the mountain. Singing. Searching.
>
> For you.
>
> The knowledge breaks something in your chest. You try to call out, but your
> voice is too weak, too ruined.
>
> In the darkness, you reach with all your strength toward the song.

---

#### Run 11 — Phase 4: The Eagle Comes

> **Title: Thorondor's Wings**
>
> A shadow passes over you. Vast. Winged. Not a dragon. Not a fell beast.
>
> An Eagle.
>
> Thorondor, King of Eagles, whom Manwë sent to bear the harpers of the Noldor
> in ancient days. He descends, and on his back: Fingon.
>
> Your cousin reaches for you. His face is close now—golden-haired, weeping,
> resolute. He has a knife.
>
> "I cannot break the steel," he says. His voice is shaking. "Forgive me,
> cousin. This will hurt."
>
> You understand. The hand must go.
>
> "Do it," you whisper. Or try to. Your voice is ash.
>
> The knife rises.
>
> In the darkness, you brace for the final pain.

---

#### Run 12 — Phase 4: The Severing

> **Title: Freedom's Price**
>
> The knife cuts.
>
> There is pain—bright, sharp, overwhelming—and then there is *falling*, no,
> *flying*, the Eagle's wings beating, Fingon's arms around you, and the steel
> band is *gone*, and the precipice recedes above you, and you are FREE.
>
> Your right hand remains behind. Dangling from the band. A piece of you left
> on Thangorodrim forever.
>
> But you are alive. And you are descending. And below, the camp fires grow
> closer.
>
> You look at Fingon. He is weeping. You try to speak, to thank him, but the
> words will not come.
>
> It does not matter. He understands.
>
> And in your heart, something broken begins, impossibly, to heal.
>
> You are Maedhros. Eldest son of Fëanor. The Tall. The Maimed.
>
> And you have been rescued by grace you did not earn, from a house you
> betrayed, by a cousin who loved you despite everything.
>
> The darkness inside your mind begins, at last, to recede.

### Victory/Defeat Messages (Type 1: Per Run)

These play at the END of each run.

#### Runs 1-3 (Phase 1): Survival/Dissolution

**Victory:**
> You crawled back from the dark. The fragment you sought is clutched in your
> mind like a drowning man clutches driftwood. It is not much. But it is
> something. And something is more than the nothing you were.

**Defeat:**
> The dark swallowed you. You dissolve back into pain, into cold, into the
> steel band that cuts and cuts and cuts. There is no self. There is no name.
> There is only the precipice, and the wind, and the endless waiting.

---

#### Runs 4-6 (Phase 2): Fragmentation/Recognition

**Victory:**
> A name. A face. A voice. The fragment comes back with you, and you hold it
> close. You are not whole. But you are less broken than before. The darkness
> inside is receding, inch by inch, memory by memory.

**Defeat:**
> The fragment slipped away. You reached for it, but the pain was too great,
> the cold too deep. You return to the precipice empty-handed, and the
> forgetting begins again. Who are you? The question mocks you.

---

#### Runs 7-9 (Phase 3): Reconstruction/Understanding

**Victory:**
> The memory is bitter. The Oath. The trap. The folly. But it is *yours*, and
> reclaiming it means reclaiming yourself. You are Maedhros. And you are
> beginning to understand why you suffer.

**Defeat:**
> The past is too painful to hold. You let it go, and it sinks back into the
> dark. Perhaps it is better not to remember. Perhaps ignorance is mercy. But
> the steel still cuts, and the cold still burns, and forgetting changes nothing.

---

#### Runs 10-12 (Phase 4): Rescue/Grace

**Victory:**
> The song is real. The Eagle is real. Fingon is real. You hold the memory
> close: proof that grace exists, that mercy can come even to the betrayer,
> that love endures despite all reason. The rescue is near.

**Defeat:**
> The song fades. Was it real? Or did you imagine it, a fever-dream born of
> desperation? You hang in the cold, and the doubt gnaws worse than hunger.
> Perhaps no one is coming. Perhaps you will hang here forever.

## VI. Final Scene — The Surrender of the Crown

**This scene plays after the 12th Silmaril is recovered, regardless of individual run outcomes.**

### The Healing

> The Eagle sets you down in the camp of the Noldor. Fingon bears you to a
> tent. Healers come—Sindarin healers from Mithrim, who know the arts of
> binding and herb-lore.
>
> They clean the stump of your wrist. They wrap it in linen soaked in
> athelas-water. The pain is... less. Not gone. It will never be gone. But less.
>
> You sleep. For the first time in thirty years, you sleep without the steel
> cutting into your flesh, without the wind screaming, without the cold.
>
> You dream of Valinor. Of your mother Nerdanel. Of your brothers, all seven,
> standing in a line, speaking the Oath in unison.
>
> You wake weeping.
>
> Fingon is there. He has been sitting vigil beside you.
>
> "Cousin," you say. Your voice is still cracked, but it works. "Thank you."
>
> He shakes his head. "Do not thank me. I did what love required."
>
> Love. The word is a knife. Your father's love led to the Oath. Your love for
> your father led you into the trap. And now Fingon's love has freed you.
>
> Love is the most dangerous force in Arda.
>
> "The crown," you say. "It is mine by right. Fëanor's heir."
>
> Fingon's face tightens. He does not argue. The law is clear.
>
> You continue: "I cede it to your father. Fingolfin is High King. Let there be
> no dispute."
>
> Fingon stares. "You would—after all you have suffered—you would surrender—?"
>
> "I have hung on Thangorodrim for thirty years," you say. "I have forgotten my
> name and remembered it. I have been broken and remade. And I have learned
> this: the crown is not worth a war among our people. Your father crossed the
> Helcaraxë. He led the host when I was captive. He has earned it."
>
> "And you?" Fingon asks quietly.
>
> You look at the stump of your wrist. "I will fight. I will lead, if I am
> needed. But I will not be king."
>
> Fingon's eyes fill with tears. "You are the noblest of us all, cousin."
>
> You shake your head. "I am Fëanor's son. And I am bound by his Oath. Nobility
> is beyond me. But perhaps... perhaps I can be useful. One-handed. Broken. But
> alive."
>
> Outside the tent, you hear the camp stirring. Word has spread: Maedhros the
> Tall has returned. Fingon the Valiant rescued him. The houses of Fingolfin
> and Fëanor are reconciled.
>
> It is not true. Not wholly. The Oath still binds. The Silmarils are still in
> Morgoth's crown. The darkness still waits.
>
> But for now—for this moment—there is peace.
>
> You close your eyes. The pain in your wrist remains. The memory of the steel
> remains.
>
> But you are free.
>
> And that is enough.

## VII. Implementation Notes (For Future Coding)

### Campaign Structure
- **Run count:** 12 (symbolic of the ~30 years of captivity)
- **Silmaril requirement:** 12 (one per run, representing memory fragments)
- **End trigger:** After 12th Silmaril recovered, fire Final Scene
- **Hero progression:** Weak early, strong late (inverted difficulty curve)

### Hero Availability by Phase
- Runs 1-3: The Pain, The Wrist, The Storm-Watcher available
- Runs 4-6: Add The Voice, The Brother-Memory
- Runs 7-9: Add The Fire-Son, The Betrayed
- Runs 10-12: Add The King-Unclaimed, Fingon (Memory), Maedhros the Rescued

### Unique Mechanics
- **MAIMED_HAND flag:** Cannot use two-handed weapons or shields (for late heroes)
- **MAEDHROS_LEFTHANDED unique:** Bonus to one-handed weapons (compensates for maiming)
- **HUAN_GRIP unique:** (if Huan is included from Luthien scenario)
- **CURSE flag:** Many heroes carry curses (representing trauma)

### Narrative System Integration
- Memory Chapters (Type 0): 12 chapters, indexed by run number
- Victory/Defeat messages (Type 1): 4 sets (one per phase)
- Final Scene: Always plays after Run 12
- Opening Scene: Plays once at campaign start
